---

## 1. Why [3] and not the covariate-shift paper [2]

The obvious starting point is weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019) — reweight calibration points by the likelihood ratio between deployment and calibration covariate distributions, get back exact coverage. The blueprint's own honest-scope framing (§6.1) rules this out as the primary anchor: covariate shift moves the distribution of X while holding the conditional relationship Y | X fixed, and operator shift does not do that. Changing the forward operator changes the measurement process itself — the reconstruction and the score computed from it are different *functions* of the true image under A_cal versus A_deploy, not just different draws of the same function's input. There is no single shared "Y | X" for a likelihood-ratio correction to preserve.

[3] is the right anchor because it does not assume covariate shift at all. It bounds the coverage gap for *arbitrary* non-exchangeability, with covariate-shift weighting recovered as one special case where the bound happens to be exact. That generality is what a non-covariate-shift problem like operator shift needs.

---

## 2. Split conformal, as the baseline being relaxed

Standard split conformal: a nonconformity score function is fixed (trained on data disjoint from calibration). Calibration scores S_1, ..., S_n and a test score S_{n+1} are computed. If (S_1, ..., S_n, S_{n+1}) are **exchangeable** — their joint law is invariant under permutation — then the rank of S_{n+1} among all n+1 scores is uniform on {1, ..., n+1}, which is the entire proof. Coverage is exact and distribution-free; no assumption on the *shape* of the score distribution is needed, only exchangeability of the sequence.

Everything in this project's problem sits downstream of one fact: calibration scores come from acquisitions under A_cal, the deployment score comes from A_deploy, and if A_cal ≠ A_deploy the sequence is not exchangeable. That is the entire mechanism of the failure described in blueprint §6 — nothing more exotic is going on than a broken permutation-invariance.

## 3. The non-exchangeable framework [3]

### 3.1 Setup

Fixed, non-negative weights w_1, ..., w_n, w_{n+1} are assigned to the n calibration points and the test point — fixed in the sense of not depending on the calibration data itself, chosen in advance from side information about how the sequence is expected to be non-exchangeable. (Weights derived from covariate-shift likelihood ratios are the motivating example in [2]; [3] allows any fixed choice.)

### 3.2 Weighted quantile

The prediction set is built from the weighted empirical quantile of the calibration scores, weight w_{n+1} assigned to the test point exactly as in ordinary weighted split conformal. Setting all weights equal recovers standard split conformal.

### 3.3 The coverage-gap bound — verified against the source

**Verified against `Paper/barber_beyond_exchangeability/paper5.tex` (arXiv:2202.13415).** Notation: for weights $w_1,\dots,w_n \in [0,1]$ on the $n$ calibration points, define normalized weights

$$\tilde{w}_i = \frac{w_i}{w_1+\cdots+w_n+1} \ (i=1,\dots,n), \qquad \tilde{w}_{n+1} = \frac{1}{w_1+\cdots+w_n+1}.$$

Let $Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_{n+1})$ be the full calibration-plus-test sequence and $Z^i$ the sequence with calibration point $i$ and the test point swapped. Let $R(Z)$ denote the vector of nonconformity residuals computed from $Z$ (for split conformal, from a pre-fitted, calibration-independent model; for full conformal, refit on the whole sequence).

**Theorem 4 (Nonexchangeable full conformal prediction, [3]):**

$$\mathbb{P}\{Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}_n(X_{n+1})\} \;\geq\; 1 - \alpha \;-\; \sum_{i=1}^n \tilde{w}_i \cdot d_{TV}\big(R(Z), R(Z^i)\big).$$

Split conformal is stated as the special case of full conformal where the fitted model is a fixed pre-trained function independent of the calibration data — this is exactly this project's setting (score function fixed before calibration, per T1.1), so the "nonsymmetric algorithm" machinery (random swap-index $K$, tags) in the paper's more general full-conformal treatment does not add anything here; the split-conformal statement above is the one that applies directly.

**Two important corrections to my earlier paraphrase:**

1. **The theorem is stated on residuals $R(Z)$, not on the raw data $Z$.** The looser, "more interpretable" bound $\text{Coverage gap} \leq \sum_i \tilde{w}_i \cdot d_{TV}(Z, Z^i)$ follows only as a corollary, since $d_{TV}(R(Z), R(Z^i)) \leq d_{TV}(Z, Z^i)$ always — and the paper explicitly demonstrates (§ Examples, "Covariate time series") a case where the residual-level distance is vanishingly small while the raw-data distance is bounded away from zero. This matters directly for this project: our "$R(Z)$" is the non-conformity **score**, exactly the quantity T1.1 defines and the score bank caches. Any operator-distance bound T3.2/T3.3 derive should target $d_{TV}(R(Z), R(Z^i))$ — i.e. bound how much the *score distribution* moves under a swap — not the distance between raw (operator, image, measurement) tuples, which is the weaker and likely far looser quantity.
2. Under true exchangeability, $d_{TV}(Z,Z^i)=0$ for all $i$ and the bound is exactly zero coverage loss — confirmed directly in the source (§ Remarks on the theorems: "there is no coverage lost by introducing fixed weights... without sacrificing any guarantees if indeed exchangeability happens to hold").

### 3.4 What the bound needs, and what it does not

**Needs:**
- Weights fixed in advance (not fit on the calibration data being used for the guarantee). Confirmed in the source: the paper explicitly separates *fixed* weights (its own setting, proven) from *data-dependent* weights $w_i = w(X_i)$ as used in covariate-shift/localized conformal prediction, and states unifying the two is **left to future work**, not already covered by Theorem 4 (§ Extensions and explorations, "Fixed versus data-dependent weights"). This is a direct, sourced constraint on **T3's L3 item**: "known vs. estimated weights" cannot simply cite [3] for estimated-weight validity — that extension does not exist yet in the base framework and would need its own argument (the paper does sketch a conditional-TV version of the bound for random/data-dependent weights, conditioning on the weights themselves, which is the natural starting point if L3 needs it).
- The nonconformity score function itself must be fixed independently of calibration data, exactly as in ordinary split conformal (no new requirement here).

**Does not need — corrected:**
- A "well-defined swap" is **not a genuine hurdle**, contrary to what I wrote in the previous draft. The paper is explicit that "we do not make any assumption on the joint distribution of the $n+1$ points" — $Z^i$ is defined by permuting indices of *whatever* joint distribution the $n+1$ points already have. Constructing $Z_i$ = (operator regime $i$, image, measurement, score) as a random element and $Z^i$ as the index-swapped sequence is unproblematic in exactly the same way it is for a time series or any other non-i.i.d. sequence — no covariate-shift-like symmetry is required to *define* it.
- Any assumption about *why* the sequence is non-exchangeable, or a parametric model of the shift.
- The weights to be "correct" in any sense — the bound holds for any fixed weights; better weights only make the bound tighter, they are not a correctness condition.
- Covariate shift, or any other specific structural assumption about what changed.

This last point is the one that makes [3] usable here at all: the project does not need to first prove operator shift *is* a form of covariate shift (it isn't, per §1 above) before the machinery applies.

**What actually is hard**, now that the swap itself is confirmed trivial to define: finding a computable operator-distance measure that **bounds $d_{TV}(R(Z), R(Z^i))$**, the residual/score-level distance — not defining $Z^i$, and not merely bounding the looser raw-data distance $d_{TV}(Z,Z^i)$. §5 and §6 below are revised accordingly.

## 4. Where [2] still matters, as a special case

[2]'s exact-coverage result is a special case of [3]'s bound: under true covariate shift, with weights set to the exact likelihood ratio, the swap-distance terms vanish and the bound tightens to exact coverage. This matters for T3.2/T3.3 as a **sanity check**, not as the operating regime: whatever operator-distance-based weighting scheme T1's weighted recalibration (D4) uses, if it is fed a degenerate case that genuinely is covariate shift, the derived bound should reduce to [2]'s exact result. If it doesn't, something in the operator-shift-specific derivation is wrong.

**One more reason to anchor on [3] rather than [2], confirmed by the source and not obvious in advance:** [3]'s own related-work section (§ Background, "Further related work") points out an asymmetry that favors the fixed-weight framework specifically. [2]'s covariate-shift weights are *data-dependent* (a function of $X_i$, via the likelihood ratio) and required to be exactly correct or well-approximated; if the likelihood ratio is misspecified, or if there turns out to be no shift at all and the data is actually exchangeable, [2]'s weighted method carries **no coverage guarantee**. [3]'s fixed-weight method, by contrast, **retains exact coverage under true exchangeability regardless of what weights were chosen** (§3.3 point 2 above) and degrades gracefully, bounded by the TV-distance sum, when it is not. Given that this project cannot fully rule out corner cases where the "operator shift" turns out to be negligible for a given pair of regimes, this graceful-degradation property is a direct argument for building on [3] rather than attempting a bespoke likelihood-ratio correction in the style of [2].

## 6. Open questions handed to T3.2 / T3.3

- **Does a null-space-based operator distance actually bound $d_{TV}(R(Z), R(Z^i))$ (the score-level distance), or only the looser $d_{TV}(Z, Z^i)$?** This is now the precise question, not a vaguer "is the swap well-defined" one (§3.4 resolved that). T3.2 evaluates several candidate distances (null-space projection, principal angles, operator norms, sampling-support difference, spectral divergence); the eventual choice must be defensible as *bounding movement in the score distribution specifically*, or the resulting "bound" is empirical curve-fitting rather than a proof. The paper's own covariate-time-series example (§3.3) is a cautionary case where a raw-data-level distance stayed large while the residual-level distance vanished — the analogous risk here is an operator distance that looks large (e.g. very different sampling masks) while barely moving the actual score distribution, or vice versa.
- **Does the fixed-weight assumption survive contact with group-conditional calibration (D3/T1.2)?** Mondrian calibration effectively partitions the calibration set by regime before any weighting is applied; the source's own related-work section explicitly treats Mondrian conformal prediction as a *separate* idea from its own framework ("These works involve very different ideas from those presented in the current paper" — § Background, "Further related work"), so [3] does not already answer whether its bound composes with group-conditional partitioning. This is confirmed as genuinely open, not just unexamined, and should be flagged to T1.2/T3.3 jointly.
- **L3 needs its own argument, not a citation.** Per §5's last row, [3] explicitly leaves data-dependent/estimated weights as future work and only gestures at a conditional-TV bound. Before T3.3 attempts L3 ("known versus estimated weights, effective sample size, finite-sample deficit"), decide whether to (a) adapt the paper's conditional-TV sketch, (b) restrict L3's claims to the *known*-weight case only (which is this project's actual operating regime per D4, so may be sufficient — estimated weights may not even be needed if masks are always experimenter-chosen), or (c) treat estimated weights as a stretch item consistent with L3's "advanced" billing in the blueprint's ladder.
