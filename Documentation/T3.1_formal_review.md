## Objective

Lay out, precisely enough for T3.2/T3.3 to build on without re-deriving it, what Barber–Candès–Ramdas–Tibshirani's "Conformal Prediction Beyond Exchangeability" [3] actually proves, what it assumes, and — critically — where it does and does not already cover operator shift. This task produces no new theorem. It produces the inventory of assumptions T3.3 (the L1 stability result) has to either inherit, adapt, or explicitly break.

---

## 1. Why [3] and not the covariate-shift paper [2]

The obvious starting point is weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019) — reweight calibration points by the likelihood ratio between deployment and calibration covariate distributions, get back exact coverage. The blueprint's own honest-scope framing (§6.1) rules this out as the primary anchor: covariate shift moves the distribution of X while holding the conditional relationship Y | X fixed, and operator shift does not do that. Changing the forward operator changes the measurement process itself — the reconstruction and the score computed from it are different *functions* of the true image under A_cal versus A_deploy, not just different draws of the same function's input. There is no single shared "Y | X" for a likelihood-ratio correction to preserve.

[3] is the right anchor because it does not assume covariate shift at all. It bounds the coverage gap for *arbitrary* non-exchangeability, with covariate-shift weighting recovered as one special case where the bound happens to be exact. That generality is what a non-covariate-shift problem like operator shift needs.

---

## 2. Split conformal, as the baseline being relaxed

Standard split conformal: a nonconformity score function is fixed (trained on data disjoint from calibration). Calibration scores S_1, ..., S_n and a test score S_{n+1} are computed. If (S_1, ..., S_n, S_{n+1}) are **exchangeable** — their joint law is invariant under permutation — then the rank of S_{n+1} among all n+1 scores is uniform on {1, ..., n+1}, which is the entire proof. Coverage is exact and distribution-free; no assumption on the *shape* of the score distribution is needed, only exchangeability of the sequence.

Everything in this project's problem sits downstream of one fact: calibration scores come from acquisitions under A_cal, the deployment score comes from A_deploy, and if A_cal ≠ A_deploy the sequence is not exchangeable. That is the entire mechanism of the failure described in blueprint §6 — nothing more exotic is going on than a broken permutation-invariance.

## 3. The non-exchangeable framework [3]

### 3.1 Setup

Fixed, non-negative weights w_1, ..., w_n, w_{n+1} are assigned to the n calibration points and the test point — fixed in the sense of not depending on the calibration data itself, chosen in advance from side information about how the sequence is expected to be non-exchangeable. (Weights derived from covariate-shift likelihood ratios are the motivating example in [2]; [3] allows any fixed choice.)

### 3.2 Weighted quantile

The prediction set is built from the weighted empirical quantile of the calibration scores, weight w_{n+1} assigned to the test point exactly as in ordinary weighted split conformal. Setting all weights equal recovers standard split conformal.

### 3.3 The coverage-gap bound

The headline result bounds the shortfall from nominal coverage by a term built from **total variation distances between the true joint law of the sequence and its "swapped" counterparts** — the distribution obtained by exchanging calibration point i with the test point, for each i. Informally: the more the joint distribution changes when calibration point i is swapped for the test point, the more that point's non-exchangeability can leak into the coverage guarantee, and the weighted sum of these per-point TV distances upper-bounds the total coverage loss. When the sequence is genuinely exchangeable every swap distance is zero and the bound recovers exact coverage; the framework is a strict generalization, not a replacement.

**Action item before T3.3 uses this:** verify the exact theorem statement, the precise weighting normalization, and the constant in front of the TV-distance sum directly against [3] (Annals of Statistics, 2023) — the description above is the structural shape of the result, and the derivation in T3.3 needs the literal statement, not a paraphrase.

### 3.4 What the bound needs, and what it does not

**Needs:**
- Weights fixed in advance (not fit on the calibration data being used for the guarantee).
- A well-defined notion of "swapping" the test point with each calibration point — this requires the sequence to still be jointly defined on a common probability space, which is not automatic once acquisitions differ in their own operator.
- The nonconformity score function itself must be fixed independently of calibration data, exactly as in ordinary split conformal (no new requirement here).

**Does not need:**
- Any assumption about *why* the sequence is non-exchangeable, or a parametric model of the shift.
- The weights to be "correct" in any sense — the bound holds for any fixed weights; better weights (i.e., weights that track the true source of non-exchangeability) only make the bound tighter, they are not a correctness condition.
- Covariate shift, or any other specific structural assumption about what changed.

This last point is the one that makes [3] usable here at all: the project does not need to first prove operator shift *is* a form of covariate shift (it isn't, per §1 above) before the machinery applies.

## 4. Where [2] still matters, as a special case

[2]'s exact-coverage result is a special case of [3]'s bound: under true covariate shift, with weights set to the exact likelihood ratio, the swap-distance terms vanish and the bound tightens to exact coverage. This matters for T3.2/T3.3 as a **sanity check**, not as the operating regime: whatever operator-distance-based weighting scheme T1's weighted recalibration (D4) uses, if it is fed a degenerate case that genuinely is covariate shift, the derived bound should reduce to [2]'s exact result. If it doesn't, something in the operator-shift-specific derivation is wrong.

## 5. Assumption inventory carried into T3.2 / T3.3

| Assumption in [3] | Status for operator shift |
|---|---|
| Weights fixed in advance, not data-dependent | **Holds cleanly.** Blueprint §9 Tool 2: retrospective masks/projection geometries are experimenter-chosen, so operator-distance-based weights are known in advance, not estimated. This is the best-case setting [3] supports. |
| Score function fixed independent of calibration data | **Holds**, inherited unchanged from standard split conformal — T1's score (T1.1) is fixed before calibration runs. |
| A common probability space on which calibration and test points, and their swaps, are jointly defined | **Needs an explicit construction.** In [2]/[3]'s covariate-shift picture this is "same Y\|X, different X-marginal." Here there is no shared Y\|X — the swap has to be defined at the level of (operator, image, measurement, score) tuples. This is the first thing T3.3 must state formally before the L1 bound can even be written down; it is not automatic from [3] as written. |
| Coverage gap bounded by a *distributional* (TV) distance | **Abstract, not yet computable.** This is exactly the quantity blueprint §12 wants replaced by an *operator*-distance measure — T3.2's job, not T3.1's. T3.1's contribution is confirming that the object to be replaced is a TV distance between swapped joint laws, which constrains what a valid operator-distance measure has to dominate or approximate. |

## 6. Open questions handed to T3.2 / T3.3

- **What does "swapping" a calibration point and the test point mean when the two were acquired under different operators?** In the covariate-shift case, swapping two draws with the same Y\|X is well-defined by symmetry of the joint law. Under operator shift the analogous symmetry has to be built from the shared true-image distribution plus the *known* operators A_cal, A_deploy applied to it — i.e. the randomness being swapped is over images, not over (operator, image) pairs, with the operator sequence held fixed as a design choice. This needs to be nailed down before T3.3 can write a coverage-gap statement that means what T1 needs it to mean.
- **Is the TV-distance term dominated by a null-space-based operator distance, or only correlated with it?** T3.2 evaluates several candidate distances (null-space projection, principal angles, operator norms, sampling-support difference, spectral divergence); this document does not pick one, but §5's last row says the eventual choice must be defensible as *bounding*, not merely tracking, the TV-distance term, or the resulting "bound" is empirical curve-fitting rather than a proof.
- **Does the fixed-weight assumption survive contact with group-conditional calibration (D3/T1.2)?** Mondrian calibration effectively partitions the calibration set by regime before any weighting is applied; whether the [3] bound composes cleanly with that partitioning, or needs its own argument, is untouched by this review and should be flagged to T1.2/T3.3 jointly rather than assumed.
