## 0. What this cut is, and why it is narrow

Blueprint §23 plans a coordinated pair: a preprint carrying the empirical core and the
guaranteed backbone, then a primary paper with the method and cross-modality result, then a
separate analytical paper for the bound. This is the first of those, and D11 has called it

**Three things are deliberately excluded, each because it is not ready or not true.**

- **Tool 2, distance-weighted recalibration.** Valid, and flags no less area than the

What remains is a demonstrated failure, a demonstrated fix, in two modalities whose forward
operators are structurally different, with the failure graded by a quantity computable from

## Title

**Conformal reliability certificates for generative-prior image reconstruction do not survive
a change of acquisition operator**

Alternative, if a positive framing is wanted: *Operator-conditional conformal calibration for
hallucination flagging in MRI and CT reconstruction*. The negative title is the honest one —
the measured result is the failure, and the fix is textbook once the failure is established.

## Abstract

Generative priors reconstruct images from incomplete measurements by filling what the
acquisition operator does not see, and they sometimes fill it with structure that is not
there. Conformal prediction offers a distribution-free way to flag such regions with a
guaranteed miss rate, and a growing literature applies it to reconstruction. That guarantee
assumes the calibration and deployment data are exchangeable. **We show it fails when the
acquisition operator changes between them — a different undersampling mask, a different view
count, a narrower angular arc — and that the failure is graded, not incidental.**

On sparse-view and limited-angle CT, a threshold calibrated at 180 views and transferred
loses coverage monotonically as views thin (0.890 at 120 views to 0.693 at 30) and as the arc
narrows (0.888 at 150° to 0.796 at 90°), falling below the nominal 0.90 on 13 of 13 subject
splits at the four most severe operators. On accelerated MRI the same failure appears at 10 to
13 of 13 splits depending on the mask family. **Group-conditional (Mondrian) calibration
restores nominal coverage at every operator in both modalities** — 0.902 to 0.923 on CT, 0.884
to 0.901 on MRI — at a measured cost in flagged area and region-level false discovery rate.

We further show the coverage gap is **ordered by a signed operator distance computable from
the two forward operators alone**, with no ground truth and no reconstructions: rank
correlation +0.976 on CT. On MRI the same distance orders the gap only weakly (+0.316), and we
explain why — MRI's worst failures occur at *matched* acceleration across different mask
families, where any scalar operator distance is near zero. We therefore do **not** claim the
two modalities' coverage curves collapse onto one; we claim the failure and the fix are common
to both, and the distance ordering holds where the distance is a genuine scalar.

Finally, we report a negative result about evaluation practice that applies beyond this work.
Writing the null-space reconstruction error through an exact identity,
`err² = s² + c² − 2sc·cos θ` for reconstruction content `s`, true hidden content `c` and their
alignment `θ`, we find that under a mismatched prior `cos θ ≈ 0`, so any hallucination
criterion thresholding an error *magnitude* becomes approximately a function of the score
being evaluated. Measured Spearman correlation 0.99. **Detection AUROCs of 0.95–1.00 obtained
this way measure the identity, not the detector**, and we show a direction-based criterion is
the available alternative.

## 1. Introduction

The contribution, in the order a reader needs it:

1. **Operator shift breaks conformal coverage, and is not a covariate shift.** A change of `A`
   alters the measurement *process*, not the distribution of inputs to a fixed process, so the
   standard weighted-conformal correction for covariate shift does not apply. §3.3.
2. **The failure is measured and graded in two modalities** with structurally different
   operators — a diagonal k-space mask and a non-diagonal projection geometry. §6.1.
3. **The guaranteed fix is exact in the deployment regime that matters.** When the operator set
   is known and finite — which is the real case, since sampling patterns and geometries are
   *chosen* — group-conditional calibration restores exact finite-sample coverage. §4.4, §6.2.
4. **The gap is ordered by a quantity available before deployment.** §4.5, §6.3.
5. **A caution for the field's evaluation practice.** §6.4.

(5) is not in the original plan. It emerged from trying four hallucination criteria and
measuring why each failed, and it is probably the most transferable thing here.

## 2. Related work

Three strands, and the gap between them is where this sits.

**Conformal prediction** gives distribution-free, finite-sample validity under exchangeability
(Vovk et al.). Mondrian/group-conditional variants condition on a discrete taxonomy. Weighted
conformal prediction (Tibshirani et al., 2019) handles covariate shift with known likelihood
ratios; non-exchangeable conformal prediction (Barber et al., 2023) bounds the coverage loss
under general departures. **We use the first two as given and show the third's framing is the
right one for operator shift, but that the covariate-shift correction specifically does not
transfer.**

**Uncertainty for reconstruction** — posterior sampling with diffusion and flow priors (DPS,
DDNM, FlowDPS), and conformal intervals on reconstructed pixels. These calibrate *at one
operator*. The literature that applies CP to reconstruction does not, to our knowledge,
examine what the certificate does when the operator changes, which is the assumption we test.

**Hallucination detection** in reconstruction — posterior dispersion and related
spread-based detectors, and hallucination indices. §6.4's identity is a caution about how this
strand is evaluated.

## 3. Problem setting

### 3.1 Reconstruction and regions

Recover `x` from `y = Ax + ε` with `A` a **known** linear forward operator. Known is not a
convenience assumption: it holds for retrospective MRI undersampling and for CT projection
geometry, where the sampling pattern is chosen. The method does not apply where `A` must be
estimated.

A reconstruction method returns draws `x̂₁…x̂ₙ` from a posterior under a pretrained generative
prior. The audit question is not whether `x̂` is accurate but whether a **region** of it
contains structure the prior invented. Regions are a fixed grid partition; the deployment
setting supplies `A`, `y`, and the draws, and never `x`.

### 3.2 Hallucination as data-consistent invention

Write `P_R = A⁺A` and `P_N = I − P_R`. Any reconstruction decomposes as
`x̂ = P_R x̂ + P_N x̂`. The range part is pinned by the measurements; the null part is the
prior's to choose, and is invisible to any data-consistency check. **Hallucination is
structure in `P_N x̂` that is not in `P_N x`.** This is what makes it hard: it is consistent
with every measurement taken.

Note the asymmetry the criterion must respect. A region can be wrong because the prior
invented structure *or* because it smoothed structure away, and only the first is
hallucination. Restricting attention to the null component is what separates them.

### 3.3 Operator shift, and why it is not covariate shift

**Operator shift** is a change of `A` between calibration and deployment. Under covariate
shift the map from input to output is fixed and the input distribution moves, so reweighting
calibration points by a likelihood ratio restores validity. Here the *map* changes: a
different `A` changes `P_N`, hence which structure is invisible, hence the distribution of
every score — and it changes the ground truth of what counts as hidden. There is no
likelihood ratio on a fixed space to reweight by. §4.4's remedy is therefore conditioning, not
weighting, and we claim no exact guarantee outside the known-finite-operator regime.

## 4. Method

### 4.1 Non-conformity score

Three components per region, each a norm scaled by a shared reference intensity:

- `s_free = ‖P_N x̂‖` — null-space content the reconstruction produced;
- `s_disagree` — spread of `P_N x̂ᵢ` across posterior draws;
- `s_residual = ‖y − A x̂‖` — measurement residual.

Combined by a standardised affine rule with scales estimated on the **calibration operator and
then frozen**. Freezing matters: re-estimating per deployment operator would pull the score
distributions towards one another and shrink the very coverage gap being measured.

Component weights are **equal by default and were not selected** — the design split cannot
separate any weighting from any other. §6.5 shows this costs the result nothing, which is a
stronger position than a fitted weight.

### 4.2 An exact identity

For any region, with `s = s_free`, `c = ‖P_N x‖` the truth's own hidden content, and
`err = ‖P_N(x̂ − x)‖`:

> **`err² = s² + c² − 2·s·c·cos θ`**

where `θ` is the angle between the reconstruction's hidden content and the truth's. It is the
law of cosines, exact whenever the three quantities share one normalisation, and it is
verified on our banks to a maximum relative residual of 0.

It is worth stating because it is diagnostic. `cos θ → 1` means the prior *recovered* hidden
anatomy, and a flag firing there is a false positive by the definition in §3.2; `cos θ → 0`
means its hidden content is orthogonal to the truth's, which is invention; `cos θ < 0` means
it invented structure where the truth had the opposite. Two regions with identical `err` can
sit at either extreme, so **magnitude alone cannot distinguish recovery from invention.** §6.4
is the consequence.

### 4.3 Ground-truth criterion

A region is labelled hallucinated when `err_free` exceeds a threshold fixed as a quantile of
`err_free` on a held-out **design** split of patients, disjoint from both calibration and
evaluation. A quantile rather than a constant: error magnitudes transfer across neither
modalities nor substrates, and a fixed constant flagged every region in our first attempt.

**This criterion has a defect we report rather than resolve** (§6.4), and §6.6 gives the
direction-based alternative.

### 4.4 Calibration

**Split conformal.** Take the `k`-th smallest score among hallucinated calibration regions
with `k = ⌊α(n+1)⌋`. For exchangeable scores the new positive's rank is uniform on `1…n+1`, so
the region-level miss rate is `k/(n+1) ≤ α` exactly. Flooring rather than rounding up: a
ceiling returns a threshold from as few as one positive and reports a miss rate of ½ as
certified.

**Naive transfer** fits the threshold at one operator and applies it at another. This is the
arm under test, and it is what a user does by default.

**Group-conditional (Mondrian)** fits a separate threshold per operator. When the operator set
is known and finite, each group is internally exchangeable and the guarantee is **exact per
group** — no asymptotics, no bound. A group with too few positives to certify is flagged
entirely, which costs efficiency and never validity.

### 4.5 Operator distance

A distance between two operators must be computable from the operators alone, monotone in
something a radiographer would recognise, and defined identically for a k-space mask and a
projection geometry. We use a **signed** null-space measure: positive when the deployment
operator hides more than the calibration operator, negative when less.

Signed, not symmetric, and this is load-bearing. The coverage failure is directional —
deploying towards sparser sampling loses coverage, deploying towards denser over-flags — and a
symmetric metric maps both to the same positive distance, collapsing two opposite failures
onto one point. On CT the signed measure attains ρ = +0.976 against +0.762 for the symmetric
alternatives.

## 5. Experimental design

**Crossed design.** This is retrospective undersampling: one acquisition, every operator
applied to it. So every operator sees every patient, and the patient budget is *shared* across
operators rather than divided among them. Checked on each bank rather than assumed.

**Paired estimand.** The headline quantity is a coverage *difference*, and because the same
patient is reconstructed at both operators it is a **within-patient paired difference**. The
dominant variance term — how hard that patient's anatomy is — is shared and cancels.
Estimating the gap by subtracting two independently bootstrapped coverages discards that
cancellation and reports an interval far wider than the design supports. Measured variance
reduction on real data: 1.29–1.46×.

**Clustered intervals.** Regions within a volume are not independent. Every interval is a
cluster bootstrap over patients; an interval over regions would treat ~9,000 correlated
regions as independent and come back perhaps thirty times too narrow.

**Splits.** Design (criterion thresholds), calibration (conformal thresholds), test
(everything reported) — disjoint on patients. Results are means over 13 subject splits.

**Both directions.** Ascending, calibrated at the least aggressive operator, and descending,
from the most. One direction alone either understates the risk or cherry-picks: ascending
fails by under-covering, descending by over-flagging.

**Grids.** MRI: mask families × acceleration. CT: view count (180 down to 30) and angular
range (180° down to 90°). Solver held fixed within any coverage curve.

## 6. Results

> **All numbers in this section are development output.** The generative prior is a
> natural-image model applied to medical anatomy (D-016). The *structure* of every result —
> monotone loss, restoration at every operator, the distance ordering — is what we report;
> the magnitudes will move with medical weights. No number here should be cited as a
> measurement of clinical performance.

### 6.1 Naive transfer loses coverage, monotonically, in both modalities

CT, calibrated at 180 views, 13-split means, nominal 0.90:

| deployed at | naive coverage | below 0.90 in |
|---|---|---|
| 120 views | 0.890 | — |
| 90 views | 0.847 | 13/13 |
| 60 views | 0.770 | 13/13 |
| 45 views | 0.728 | 13/13 |
| 30 views | **0.693** | 13/13 |
| 150° arc | 0.888 | — |
| 120° arc | 0.837 | — |
| 90° arc | 0.796 | 13/13 |

Monotone along both axes, and robust: at the four most severe operators the guarantee fails on
every one of 13 splits. On MRI the same label leaves fewer robust failures — 13/13 for
`equispaced1d R2`, 11/13 for `random1d R8`, 10/13 for `equispaced1d R8` — which §6.3 explains.

In the descending direction the failure inverts: coverage holds at 1.000 at 7 of 8 CT
operators by flagging ever more as the shift grows, flag area 0.565 → 1.000 and region FDR
0.547 → 0.924. **Coverage is the wrong thing to read in that direction; flag area is.**

### 6.2 Group-conditional calibration restores it, at a stated cost

13-split means: **0.902–0.923 at every CT operator**, 0.884–0.901 on MRI wherever positives
exist. The trend against operator distance vanishes, as a fix should (ρ = +0.267 on CT, gaps
−0.02 to +0.045).

The cost, which we report rather than bury (single split):

| operator | flag area | region FDR |
|---|---|---|
| 30 views | 0.220 → 0.313 | 0.029 → 0.106 |
| 90° arc | 0.308 → 0.423 | 0.084 → 0.202 |

Naive transfer's near-zero FDR at the sparse operators is **not precision**. It flags too
little, mostly in regions that are hallucinated, and misses a quarter to a third of them.

One efficiency finding worth stating because it looks like a defect and is a consequence of
§4.1: at 180 views, group-conditional calibration flags 86% of regions when the score scales
are frozen at 30 views, against 12% when frozen at 180, with coverage the same (0.897, 0.888).
Freezing at the calibration operator is required so as not to shrink the measured gap; the
cost appears as inefficiency at remote operators.

### 6.3 The gap is ordered by operator distance on CT, and only weakly on MRI

CT, over the calibration operator's eight pairs: **ρ = +0.976** with the signed distance,
against +0.762 for symmetric alternatives. The five candidate distances, which coincide on
MRI, genuinely differ on CT.

MRI: ρ = +0.316 from `random1d R2` and +0.556 from R8. **We report this as a limitation with a
mechanism, not as noise.** MRI's worst failures sit at *matched* acceleration across different
mask families — equispaced versus random at the same R — where any scalar operator distance is
near zero. The correct MRI presentation is therefore one curve per mask family; a single curve
is a line fitted through two clusters.

**Consequently we do not claim curve collapse.** The strong form of a cross-domain claim would
be that both modalities' gap-versus-distance curves fall on one curve, making the gap a
function of operator movement and not of modality. Our own test (nested pooled-versus-separate
fits, with the verdict resting on magnitude against the patient-split noise floor rather than
on an F-test, which rejects on differences of 0.0004) says the strong form fails. The claim is
the weak form: **the same failure and the same fix in both modalities, with the distance
ordering demonstrated where the distance is a genuine scalar.**

### 6.4 A caution about evaluating hallucination detectors

Our detection AUROCs are 0.95–1.00. **They are not a result, and the reason generalises.**

By §4.2's identity, at `cos θ ≈ 0` — which is what a mismatched prior produces, since its
invented content is near-orthogonal to the truth's — `err ≈ √(s² + c²)`. Measured on our bank,
`spearman(err_free, hypot(s_free, free_content)) = +0.971`, and the full criterion correlates
with the score at Spearman 0.99. **The criterion is approximately a function of the score it is
grading.** An AUROC computed this way measures the identity.

This is not a defect of one criterion. We tried four — an absolute error threshold, a ratio to
true hidden content, that ratio under a candidacy gate, and an absolute threshold at a design
quantile — and **all four threshold a magnitude**, so all four inherit it. Moving the threshold
cannot help.

Two further cautions we hit directly and that any such evaluation should check:

- **A confound can beat the score.** Our anatomy control — the truth's own hidden content,
  which no detector should need — reaches AUROC 0.946 against the magnitude criterion at the
  milder operator, beating the score's 0.572. A detection number must be reported against such
  a control, per operator, and direction-agnostically: a fixed sign scores the control as
  `1 − truth` for half the cases and flips the verdict.
- **Never pool across operators.** Pooled, our anatomy control scored 0.499 — "exactly chance"
  — which was two opposite per-operator biases cancelling. Within one operator it was 0.662.

### 6.5 The result does not depend on the component weighting

Because §4.1's weights could not be selected, we checked whether the result needs them.
Sweeping `w_free` across the full grid and recomputing the paired coverage restoration at each
point, the claim is reported as weight-insensitive only when it holds at every weighting **and**
the spread across the grid is smaller than a single weighting's own interval — a flat curve is
evidence of insensitivity only if one point is determined better than the curve's spread.

### 6.6 A direction-based criterion is the available alternative

Since the identity's other coordinate is scale-free, a criterion on `cos θ` is not
near-determined by the score: measured `spearman(cos θ, s_free) = −0.435` against the
magnitude criterion's +0.971. It also encodes §3.2's distinction directly — anti-aligned
hidden content is invention, aligned is recovery — which no magnitude can.

Preliminary, on a small bank and reported as such: it removes the saturation, with the score's
AUROC falling from 0.881 to a believable 0.615, and halves the anatomy control's advantage at
the milder operator (excess −0.374 → −0.152). It does **not** by itself establish a detection
claim; at the milder operator the control still wins. We report the construction and the
caution, not a detection result.

One trap worth recording: restoring a magnitude factor — labelling on invented *mass*
`s·sin θ` rather than direction — reinstates the circularity exactly, because `sin θ ≈ 1`
wherever `cos θ ≈ 0`, so the product collapses back to `s_free`.

## 7. Limitations

1. **The prior is a natural-image model, not a medical one.** Gates every magnitude, and also
   the criterion's validity: at `cos θ ≈ 0` the criterion is approximately the score.
2. **No external validation of the criterion.** The one published alternative disagrees with
   ours at κ ≈ 0 and mostly flags background.
3. **One solver family carries the results.** The solver used replaces the range component
   structurally and has already confined posterior spread to the null space, so the
   decomposition cannot be shown to contribute on it; a gradient-steered solver, where the
   null-error share is 0.82–0.85 rather than 0.99, is the test and is outstanding.
4. **Patient budget.** Per-operator coverage is determined to about ±0.12 whatever the grid
   size; the paired contrasts are what the design supports, and we lead with them.
5. **Volume, not patient, is the clustering unit on MRI**, so MRI intervals are optimistic by
   roughly the volumes-per-patient factor.
6. **The CT forward operator is ours**, not a scanner's, and its pseudo-inverse is approximate.
7. **Single-coil MRI** via root-sum-of-squares reduction.
8. **No claim of exact coverage under continuous operator change.** Only the known-finite-set
   regime is exact.

## 8. Conclusion

Conformal reliability certificates for generative-prior reconstruction are calibrated against
one acquisition operator and silently lose their guarantee when that operator changes. We
measured the loss in two modalities with structurally different forward operators, showed it
is graded by a signed operator distance computable before deployment, and showed that
conditioning on a known finite operator set restores exact coverage at a quantified cost in
flagged area and false discovery rate. We also found that the standard way of grading such
detectors can be circular under a mismatched prior, and gave the identity that explains when.
