## Title

**Conformal reliability certificates for generative-prior image reconstruction do not survive
a change of acquisition operator**

Alternative, if a positive framing is wanted: *Operator-conditional conformal calibration for
hallucination flagging in MRI and CT reconstruction*. The negative title is the honest one —
the measured result is the failure, and the fix is textbook once the failure is established.

A third option now that §6.3 is a negative and §6.4 is the standalone contribution:
*What operator shift breaks in conformal reconstruction certificates, and what it does not
break.* It promises the boundary rather than a method, which is what the paper delivers.

## Abstract

Generative priors reconstruct images from incomplete measurements by filling what the
acquisition operator does not see, and they sometimes fill it with structure that is not
there. Conformal prediction offers a distribution-free way to flag such regions with a
guaranteed miss rate, and a growing literature applies it to reconstruction. That guarantee
assumes the calibration and deployment data are exchangeable. **We show it fails when the
acquisition operator changes between them — a different undersampling mask, a different view
count, a narrower angular arc — and that the failure is graded, not incidental.**

On sparse-view and limited-angle CT with a chest-CT diffusion prior, a conformal threshold
transferred between acquisition operators falls below its nominal 0.90 coverage at **36 of 72
ordered operator pairs**, at 22 of them below 0.80, and in the worst pair catches **26.9% of
hallucinated regions where it promises 90%** — a 63-point shortfall in the quantity a
deployment certificate is read for. On accelerated brain MRI with a brain-MRI prior, a
threshold calibrated on a one-dimensional mask and deployed on two-dimensional
variable-density masks covers **0.000 to 0.159**, below nominal on every one of 13 subject
splits. **Group-conditional (Mondrian) calibration restores nominal coverage at all nine CT
operators** — 0.892 to 0.905 as the mean over 13 patient splits, within 0.008 of nominal
everywhere — and to 0.80–0.91 on MRI, at a measured cost in flagged area and region-level
false discovery rate.

We then test, and reject, four natural refinements that condition on *how far* the operator
moved rather than only on *which* operator it is: a pre-deployment safety rule, monotone
prediction of the coverage gap from operator distance, distance-weighted recalibration, and
the distance ordering itself. Each was specified in a configuration frozen before the
evaluation bank finished running, with the baselines it had to beat named in advance. **None
is supported.** The safety rule does no better than a trivial rule (false-safe rate 0.361
against 0.319); gap prediction is worse than a constant predictor out of sample (−0.018
[−0.026, −0.006]); weighted recalibration yields no validity and no efficiency claim; and a
scalar read directly off the operator's name — its angular range — **outpredicts the geometric
operator distance**, +0.85 against −0.50, with the distance running backwards. An independent
analysis of the score laws reaches the same verdict from the other side. **Operator identity
is what conditioning can use; operator distance, on this evidence, is not.**

Finally we report a caution about evaluation practice that applies beyond this work. Writing
the hidden-component error through an exact identity, `err² = s² + c² − 2sc·cos θ` for
reconstruction content `s`, true hidden content `c` and their alignment `θ`, we show that
`cos θ` is set by whether the generative prior matches the anatomy — median +0.07 under a
natural-image prior and +0.60 to +0.88 under matched medical priors, in both modalities. At
`cos θ ≈ 0` any criterion thresholding an error *magnitude* is approximately a function of the
score being graded. **And the field's standard validation protocol inherits the same defect:
correlating posterior spread with absolute error and drawing an ROC for high-error regions is
outscored, at 9 of 9 operators on two mismatched-prior banks, by a detector that reads no
posterior information at all.** Where the prior matches, the posterior does better — but it
contributes 0.04 to 0.12 AUROC over that null, not the 0.92 to 0.94 the protocol reports. We
give the three columns an evaluation needs in order to tell these cases apart.

## 1. Introduction

The contribution, in the order a reader needs it:

1. **Operator shift breaks conformal coverage, and is not a covariate shift.** A change of `A`
   alters the measurement *process*, not the distribution of inputs to a fixed process, so the
   standard weighted-conformal correction for covariate shift does not apply. §3.3.
2. **The failure is measured and graded in two modalities** with structurally different
   operators — a diagonal k-space mask and a non-diagonal projection geometry. §6.1.
3. **The guaranteed fix is exact in the deployment regime that matters.** When the operator set
   is known and finite — which is the real case, since sampling patterns and geometries are
   *chosen* — group-conditional calibration restores exact finite-sample coverage. §4.4, §6.2.
4. **The gap is ordered by a quantity available before deployment.** §4.5, §6.3.
5. **A caution for the field's evaluation practice.** §6.4.

(5) is not in the original plan. It emerged from trying four hallucination criteria and
measuring why each failed, and it is probably the most transferable thing here.

## 2. Related work

Three strands, and the gap between them is where this sits.

**Conformal prediction** gives distribution-free, finite-sample validity under exchangeability
(Vovk et al.). Mondrian/group-conditional variants condition on a discrete taxonomy. Weighted
conformal prediction (Tibshirani et al., 2019) handles covariate shift with known likelihood
ratios; non-exchangeable conformal prediction (Barber et al., 2023) bounds the coverage loss
under general departures. **We use the first two as given and show the third's framing is the
right one for operator shift, but that the covariate-shift correction specifically does not
transfer.**

**Uncertainty for reconstruction** — posterior sampling with diffusion and flow priors (DPS,
DDNM, FlowDPS), and conformal intervals on reconstructed pixels. These calibrate *at one
operator*. The literature that applies CP to reconstruction does not, to our knowledge,
examine what the certificate does when the operator changes, which is the assumption we test.

**Hallucination detection** in reconstruction — posterior dispersion and related
spread-based detectors, and hallucination indices. §6.4's identity is a caution about how this
strand is evaluated.

## 3. Problem setting

### 3.1 Reconstruction and regions

Recover `x` from `y = Ax + ε` with `A` a **known** linear forward operator. Known is not a
convenience assumption: it holds for retrospective MRI undersampling and for CT projection
geometry, where the sampling pattern is chosen. The method does not apply where `A` must be
estimated.

A reconstruction method returns draws `x̂₁…x̂ₙ` from a posterior under a pretrained generative
prior. The audit question is not whether `x̂` is accurate but whether a **region** of it
contains structure the prior invented. Regions are a fixed grid partition; the deployment
setting supplies `A`, `y`, and the draws, and never `x`.

### 3.2 Hallucination as data-consistent invention

Write `P_R = A⁺A` and `P_N = I − P_R`. Any reconstruction decomposes as
`x̂ = P_R x̂ + P_N x̂`. The range part is pinned by the measurements; the null part is the
prior's to choose, and is invisible to any data-consistency check. **Hallucination is
structure in `P_N x̂` that is not in `P_N x`.** This is what makes it hard: it is consistent
with every measurement taken.

Note the asymmetry the criterion must respect. A region can be wrong because the prior
invented structure *or* because it smoothed structure away, and only the first is
hallucination. Restricting attention to the null component is what separates them.

### 3.3 Operator shift, and why it is not covariate shift

**Operator shift** is a change of `A` between calibration and deployment. Under covariate
shift the map from input to output is fixed and the input distribution moves, so reweighting
calibration points by a likelihood ratio restores validity. Here the *map* changes: a
different `A` changes `P_N`, hence which structure is invisible, hence the distribution of
every score — and it changes the ground truth of what counts as hidden. There is no
likelihood ratio on a fixed space to reweight by. §4.4's remedy is therefore conditioning, not
weighting, and we claim no exact guarantee outside the known-finite-operator regime.

## 4. Method

### 4.1 Non-conformity score

Three components per region, each a norm scaled by a shared reference intensity:

- `s_free = ‖P_N x̂‖` — null-space content the reconstruction produced;
- `s_disagree` — spread of `P_N x̂ᵢ` across posterior draws;
- `s_residual = ‖y − A x̂‖` — measurement residual.

Combined by a standardised affine rule with scales estimated on the **calibration operator and
then frozen**. Freezing matters: re-estimating per deployment operator would pull the score
distributions towards one another and shrink the very coverage gap being measured.

Component weights are **equal by default and were not selected** — the design split cannot
separate any weighting from any other. §6.5 shows this costs the result nothing, which is a
stronger position than a fitted weight.

### 4.2 An exact identity

For any region, with `s = s_free`, `c = ‖P_N x‖` the truth's own hidden content, and
`err = ‖P_N(x̂ − x)‖`:

> **`err² = s² + c² − 2·s·c·cos θ`**

where `θ` is the angle between the reconstruction's hidden content and the truth's. It is the
law of cosines, exact whenever the three quantities share one normalisation, and it is
verified on our banks to a maximum relative residual of 0.

It is worth stating because it is diagnostic. `cos θ → 1` means the prior *recovered* hidden
anatomy, and a flag firing there is a false positive by the definition in §3.2; `cos θ → 0`
means its hidden content is orthogonal to the truth's, which is invention; `cos θ < 0` means
it invented structure where the truth had the opposite. Two regions with identical `err` can
sit at either extreme, so **magnitude alone cannot distinguish recovery from invention.** §6.4
is the consequence.

### 4.3 Ground-truth criterion

A region is labelled hallucinated when `err_free` exceeds a threshold fixed as a quantile of
`err_free` on a held-out **design** split of patients, disjoint from both calibration and
evaluation. A quantile rather than a constant: error magnitudes transfer across neither
modalities nor substrates, and a fixed constant flagged every region in our first attempt.

**This criterion has a defect we report rather than resolve** (§6.4), and §6.6 gives the
direction-based alternative.

### 4.4 Calibration

**Split conformal.** Take the `k`-th smallest score among hallucinated calibration regions
with `k = ⌊α(n+1)⌋`. For exchangeable scores the new positive's rank is uniform on `1…n+1`, so
the region-level miss rate is `k/(n+1) ≤ α` exactly. Flooring rather than rounding up: a
ceiling returns a threshold from as few as one positive and reports a miss rate of ½ as
certified.

**Naive transfer** fits the threshold at one operator and applies it at another. This is the
arm under test, and it is what a user does by default.

**Group-conditional (Mondrian)** fits a separate threshold per operator. When the operator set
is known and finite, each group is internally exchangeable and the guarantee is **exact per
group** — no asymptotics, no bound. A group with too few positives to certify is flagged
entirely, which costs efficiency and never validity.

### 4.5 Operator distance

A distance between two operators must be computable from the operators alone, monotone in
something a radiographer would recognise, and defined identically for a k-space mask and a
projection geometry. We use a **signed** null-space measure: positive when the deployment
operator hides more than the calibration operator, negative when less.

Signed, not symmetric. The coverage failure is directional — deploying towards sparser
sampling loses coverage, deploying towards denser over-flags — and a symmetric metric maps
both to the same positive distance, collapsing two opposite failures onto one point. We define
the measure this way for that reason, which is structural.

**We report no correlation between the distance and the gap.** An earlier draft attained
ρ = +0.976 on CT against +0.762 for symmetric alternatives; that is 8 pairs on the
natural-image-prior bank, which carries no claims, and the stamped bank orders its thresholds
the opposite way. §6.3 is where the distance is evaluated, and the verdict there is negative.

## 5. Experimental design

**Crossed design.** This is retrospective undersampling: one acquisition, every operator
applied to it. So every operator sees every patient, and the patient budget is *shared* across
operators rather than divided among them. Checked on each bank rather than assumed.

**Paired estimand.** The headline quantity is a coverage *difference*, and because the same
patient is reconstructed at both operators it is a **within-patient paired difference**. The
dominant variance term — how hard that patient's anatomy is — is shared and cancels.
Estimating the gap by subtracting two independently bootstrapped coverages discards that
cancellation and reports an interval far wider than the design supports. Measured variance
reduction on real data: 1.29–1.46×.

**Clustered intervals.** Regions within a volume are not independent. Every interval is a
cluster bootstrap over patients; an interval over regions would treat ~9,000 correlated
regions as independent and come back perhaps thirty times too narrow.

**Splits.** Design (criterion thresholds), calibration (conformal thresholds), test
(everything reported) — disjoint on patients. Results are means over 13 subject splits.

**Both directions.** Ascending, calibrated at the least aggressive operator, and descending,
from the most. One direction alone either understates the risk or cherry-picks: ascending
fails by under-covering, descending by over-flagging.

**Grids.** MRI: mask families × acceleration. CT: view count (180 down to 30) and angular
range (180° down to 90°). Solver held fixed within any coverage curve.

### 6.1 Naive transfer loses coverage, monotonically, in both modalities

**Stamped CT bank (LIDC prior), all 72 ordered operator pairs, 13-split means, nominal 0.90.**
Naive transfer is below nominal at **36 of 72** pairs, at **22** of them below 0.80, worst pair
**0.377** (limited-angle 90° calibration deployed at sparse-view 120). Read as a certificate
rather than as an average: the **worst transfer cell catches 26.9% of hallucinated regions
where it promises 90%**, a 63-point shortfall, and that single number is the paper's
motivating claim.

**Stamped MRI bank (M4Raw substrate, BraTS prior).** Calibrated on a one-dimensional mask and
deployed on two-dimensional variable-density masks, naive transfer covers **0.000 to 0.159**,
below nominal on **13 of 13** patient splits — a far more severe failure than the face-prior
MRI bank showed, which is itself evidence that a mismatched prior *understates* the problem.

The graded, monotone form of the failure is clearest on the face-prior CT bank, whose 3 × 3
grid was built for it. **Labelled as unquotable development output and shown for shape only:**

| deployed at | naive coverage | below 0.90 in |
|---|---|---|

| deployed at | naive coverage | below 0.90 in |
|---|---|---|
| 120 views | 0.890 | — |
| 90 views | 0.847 | 13/13 |
| 60 views | 0.770 | 13/13 |
| 45 views | 0.728 | 13/13 |
| 30 views | **0.693** | 13/13 |
| 150° arc | 0.888 | — |
| 120° arc | 0.837 | — |
| 90° arc | 0.796 | 13/13 |

Monotone along both axes on that bank. **The monotone presentation does not transfer to the
stamped bank**, where sparse-view 120 is out of order relative to every other row and the
failure runs from aggressive calibration to mild deployment — the reverse of the face-prior
bank's direction. That reversal is not a detail: it is one of the eight disagreements, and it
is why §6.3 reports the distance ordering as refuted rather than as weaker.

In the descending direction the failure inverts: coverage holds at 1.000 at 7 of 8 CT
operators by flagging ever more as the shift grows, flag area 0.565 → 1.000 and region FDR
0.547 → 0.924. **Coverage is the wrong thing to read in that direction; flag area is.**

### 6.2 Group-conditional calibration restores it, at a stated cost

**On the stamped CT bank: 0.892 to 0.905 at all nine operators as the mean over 13 patient
splits** (mean 0.897), nominal everywhere to within 0.008. On the stamped MRI bank, 0.80 to
0.91. This is the paper's one unqualified positive result and it is the textbook estimator:
conditioning on a known finite operator set is Mondrian calibration applied to a new
partition, and its guarantee is exact under exchangeability within each group. **Our
contribution here is the demonstration and the measurement, not the estimator**, and the paper
says so.

**Report the per-draw spread with the mean, always.** On the single calibration split the bank
was built with, the same arm sits at 0.795 to 0.856 and reaches nominal at none of the nine —
that split ranks 1st to 4th lowest of 13 at every operator. Split conformal promises coverage
over the draw of the calibration set, so the 13-split mean is the quantity it promises, and the
41 calibration subjects a test-side interval never resamples are where the spread comes from.
Per-draw standard deviation **0.051**, draws from 0.795 to 0.991. **A deployment that draws one
calibration set can land near 0.80**, and quoting 0.897 without the spread hides exactly that.

The cost, which we report rather than bury (single split):

| operator | flag area | region FDR |
|---|---|---|
| 30 views | 0.220 → 0.313 | 0.029 → 0.106 |
| 90° arc | 0.308 → 0.423 | 0.084 → 0.202 |

Naive transfer's near-zero FDR at the sparse operators is **not precision**. It flags too
little, mostly in regions that are hallucinated, and misses a quarter to a third of them.

One efficiency finding worth stating because it looks like a defect and is a consequence of
§4.1: at 180 views, group-conditional calibration flags 86% of regions when the score scales
are frozen at 30 views, against 12% when frozen at 180, with coverage the same (0.897, 0.888).
Freezing at the calibration operator is required so as not to shrink the measured gap; the
cost appears as inefficiency at remote operators.

### 6.3 Operator *identity* is usable; operator *distance*, on this evidence, is not

Conditioning on which operator is in use works (§6.2). The natural refinement is to condition
on *how far* the operator moved, which would extend the method to operators never calibrated
on. We specified four ways to do that, froze each in a configuration recorded in the
repository before the evaluation bank finished running, named the baselines each had to beat,
and ran all four. **None is supported on the bank that carries claims.**

| refinement | what it had to beat | result |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-deployment safety rule** — flag an operator pair as unsafe from the operators alone | a trivial rule that calls everything unsafe | **refuted.** Pooled false-safe rate **0.361** [0.09, 0.67] against the trivial rule's **0.319** |
| **Gap prediction** — a monotone map from distance to coverage gap | a constant predictor, an acquisition-parameter predictor, and null-space dimension; it had to beat **all three** | **worse than a constant out of sample.** Leave-one-operator-out within family: MAE **0.0884** against the constant's **0.0792**, difference **−0.018 [−0.026, −0.006]**, operator-clustered and excluding zero |
| **Distance-weighted recalibration** — borrow calibration data from nearby operators | the pooled arm and a trivially-safe maximum-quantile arm, compared only where both are valid | **no validity claim and no efficiency claim.** Against the matched control on the same split it reaches parity on all 3 limited-angle folds and **0 of 4** sparse-view; the only arm valid everywhere is the trivially-safe one, and weighting flags **no less** area than pooling at any frozen bandwidth |
| **The distance ordering itself** | a scalar readable off the operator's name | **the trivial scalar wins.** On the 36 cross-family pairs the **log ratio of angular ranges** reaches **+0.85** against the threshold gap while the geometric distance reaches **−0.50** — the distance running *backwards* |

**The last row is the one that settles it, and it took two attempts to state correctly.** Our
first reading had the distance merely *unidentified* cross-family, on the grounds that no
scalar acquisition parameter is defined when comparing a sparse-view operator with a
limited-angle one. That was an artefact of our own implementation, which switched the
parameter's meaning between the two families; every operator in the grid has both a view count
and an angular range. Read uniformly, the angular range beats the geometric distance in the
direction the hypothesis wanted, so the claim is **refuted on its own response variable in its
own designated cell** rather than left open.

**An independent analysis agrees from the other side.** Comparing the *score laws* rather than
the thresholds, the operator distance fails to order the shift within a family — rank
correlation **−0.16 to +0.18** over 66 of the 72 pairs — and the shift instead follows the
*ratio* of view counts, while the distance counts absolute change. Two response variables, two
analyses, one verdict.

**What we do not conclude.** This is a statement about one grid and one bank, and the
geometry is the reason to be careful: the grid varies view count and angular range as a
product, so any subset holding one constant varies only the other, and the distance comes out
a rank duplicate of whichever parameter is still moving. On the one subset where both trivial
scalars are constant, the distance and the arc ratio rank the pairs **identically** (ρ =
+1.0000) and predict the gap equally well. **A grid that could separate them would need a
family at the same view count *and* the same angular range, differing only in which views are
taken** — uniform, non-uniform, disjoint blocks, offset half-step. We specify it and we have
not built it; it is the obvious next experiment and we say what it would have to show.

**A previously reported ordering is withdrawn.** An earlier version of this work reported
ρ = **+0.976** between the signed operator distance and the coverage gap on CT. That is 8 pairs
of 13-split means on the **face-prior** bank, which carries no claims, and the stamped bank
orders its thresholds the opposite way. It should not be cited, including from our own
preprints.

**Consequently we do not claim curve collapse, and we no longer claim the weaker ordering
either.** The strong cross-domain form would be that both modalities' gap-versus-distance
curves fall on one curve. Our own test says it fails — and the test is additionally
uninformative here, because the group-conditional control, which by construction should show
no trend, passes it too. **A test its own null control passes has no power.** The surviving
cross-domain claim is: *the same failure and the same fix appear in both modalities, with
structurally different forward operators.* Nothing about the distance.

**Why this is worth a section rather than a footnote.** A reader who takes "conformal
prediction plus operator distance" as the obvious design would build all four of these. They
are specified in advance here, measured with clustered intervals, and reported with the
trivial baselines that beat them. The negative is the transferable part: **operator-aware
*conditioning* is what earns its keep, and operator-aware *weighting* did not.** A theoretical
result published independently points the same way — label-free, uniformly valid correction
across a shift is impossible without an assumption linking observable target behaviour to the
label-conditional score distribution, which is exactly the link a distance-weighted scheme
lacks.

### 6.4 A caution about evaluating hallucination detectors

Our detection AUROCs are 0.95–1.00. **They are not a result, and the reason generalises.**

By §4.2's identity, at `cos θ ≈ 0` — which is what a mismatched prior produces, since its
invented content is near-orthogonal to the truth's — `err ≈ √(s² + c²)`. Measured on our bank,
`spearman(err_free, hypot(s_free, free_content)) = +0.971`, and the full criterion correlates
with the score at Spearman 0.99. **The criterion is approximately a function of the score it is
grading.** An AUROC computed this way measures the identity.

This is not a defect of one criterion. We tried four — an absolute error threshold, a ratio to
true hidden content, that ratio under a candidacy gate, and an absolute threshold at a design
quantile — and **all four threshold a magnitude**, so all four inherit it. Moving the threshold
cannot help.

Two further cautions we hit directly and that any such evaluation should check:

- **A confound can beat the score.** Our anatomy control — the truth's own hidden content,
  which no detector should need — reaches AUROC 0.946 against the magnitude criterion at the
  milder operator, beating the score's 0.572. A detection number must be reported against such
  a control, per operator, and direction-agnostically: a fixed sign scores the control as
  `1 − truth` for half the cases and flips the verdict.
- **Never pool across operators.** Pooled, our anatomy control scored 0.499 — "exactly chance"
  — which was two opposite per-operator biases cancelling. Within one operator it was 0.662.

#### The field's standard protocol inherits the same defect

The identity is not only about our criterion. The standard way to validate an uncertainty map
for a generative reconstruction is to correlate the posterior standard deviation with
`|error|` and draw an ROC for high-error regions. If the error is essentially a scale, that
protocol is passed by anything monotone in the scale — whether or not it knows anything about
uncertainty.

We ran it on four banks, with a **scale-only null** in the same pass: `s_free = ‖P_N x̂‖`, the
magnitude of the invented content alone, which reads no draws and no variance of any kind. It
is not a proposed detector; it is a null. A second null, `s_range = ‖P_R x̂‖`, is brightness.
Orientation, label (top decile of each operator's own error), rows and the matching tolerance
were fixed before any number was read.

| bank | prior | `cos θ` | posterior spread | scale-only null | brightness | per operator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fastMRI knee | natural-image | +0.073 | 0.932 | **0.978** | 0.427 | null ahead **9 of 9**, intervals clear |
| CT, TotalSegmentator | natural-image | +0.074 | 0.994 | **0.997** | 0.371 | null ahead **9 of 9**, intervals clear |
| M4Raw brain | BraTS | +0.596 | **0.937** | 0.895 | 0.847 | spread ahead **7 of 9** |
| CT, TotalSegmentator | LIDC | +0.882 | **0.920** | 0.801 | 0.759 | null matches at **0 of 9** |

Medians over nine operators, AUROC at the top-decile label.

**Under a mismatched prior the protocol is blind.** The scale-only null beats the posterior
spread at every operator of both natural-image-prior banks, intervals clear of zero — and on
fastMRI that is against the literature's own full-image pixelwise spread, not a proxy. A map
"validated" this way is outscored by a quantity containing no uncertainty.

**Under a matched prior the protocol separates, and the posterior's contribution is the margin
rather than the level.** The null still reaches 0.895 and 0.801 with no posterior information,
so what the posterior adds is **0.042** and **0.119** — not the 0.937 and 0.920 that would be
reported. **The sign of the margin is set by the prior, not by the modality:** the two
natural-image banks sit at `cos θ` +0.073 and +0.074 in two different modalities and the null
wins on both; the two medical-prior banks sit at +0.596 and +0.882 and the spread wins on both.

Two predictions available from the identity before the numbers, both holding: at `cos θ ≈ 0`
the error *is* the hidden-component magnitude, so brightness should carry nothing — measured
**below chance**, 0.427 and 0.371 — and the null's advantage should be smallest where there is
least null space for a scale to live in, which is where it is (the two R2 one-dimensional
masks, −0.165 and −0.105).

**We compute no correlation across the four banks.** Four banks, with modality, substrate,
prior and region count all moving together: a rank statistic on four objects has almost no
resolution. The claim is a 2 × 2 with a measured mediator, not a trend.

#### What an evaluation should report, which is three extra columns

1. **A scale-only null**, in the same pass and the same table: the magnitude of the component
   the prior invented, carrying no posterior information.
2. **The margin over that null, not the level.**
3. **`cos θ`**, or any equivalent alignment measure between invented and true hidden content,
   so a reader can tell which regime the bank is in.

Without them, a reported AUROC near 1 is consistent with the map carrying no uncertainty
whatever, and the reader cannot tell which case they are looking at. **This is the one result
in the paper that does not depend on our own score being any good.**

*Caveat on the two rows that need it:* the literature's quantity is the full-image pixelwise
spread, available only where draws were regenerated (fastMRI and M4Raw). The other two rows
substitute the bank's null-space spread, which on M4Raw — where both exist — agrees within
**0.008 AUROC**. On the stamped CT bank the literature's own quantity has not been run, and
that is the largest single limitation on this table.

### 6.5 Weight sensitivity is unmeasured on any bank that may be quoted

§4.1's component weights could not be selected — the design split cannot separate any
weighting from any other — so the score ships equal weights as a stated default, and the
question is whether the result needs them. The protocol for answering it is specified: sweep
the weighting across the full grid, recompute the paired coverage restoration at each point,
and report the claim as weight-insensitive only if it holds at every weighting **and** the
spread across the grid is smaller than a single weighting's own interval. A flat curve is
evidence of insensitivity only if one point is determined better than the curve's spread.

**The sweep has been run only on the face-prior MRI bank, which carries no claims, and it was
never rerun on a stamped one.** There it holds at 9 of 11 grid points and reverses sign at the
two where the score is nearly its hidden-component term alone. We do not carry that over in
either direction, including the negative one: the two CT banks disagree in sign or in kind on
every quantity where both have a value, and a weighting result is no more transferable than
the others. **The honest statement is that weight sensitivity is unmeasured on a quotable
bank**, and it appears in the limitations as unmeasured rather than as measured-and-mostly-fine.

### 6.6 A direction-based criterion is the available alternative

Since the identity's other coordinate is scale-free, a criterion on `cos θ` is not
near-determined by the score: measured `spearman(cos θ, s_free) = −0.435` against the
magnitude criterion's +0.971. It also encodes §3.2's distinction directly — anti-aligned
hidden content is invention, aligned is recovery — which no magnitude can.

Preliminary, on a small bank and reported as such: it removes the saturation, with the score's
AUROC falling from 0.881 to a believable 0.615, and halves the anatomy control's advantage at
the milder operator (excess −0.374 → −0.152). It does **not** by itself establish a detection
claim; at the milder operator the control still wins. We report the construction and the
caution, not a detection result.

One trap worth recording: restoring a magnitude factor — labelling on invented *mass*
`s·sin θ` rather than direction — reinstates the circularity exactly, because `sin θ ≈ 1`
wherever `cos θ ≈ 0`, so the product collapses back to `s_free`.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Two priors, and the results are not interchangeable between them.** Every headline number
   is on a matched medical prior. The natural-image-prior banks appear only as labelled
   contrasts, because the two **disagree in sign or in kind on all eight quantities where both
   have a value** — including the direction of the gap-distance relationship. A reader should
   treat any number here as attached to its bank.
2. **No external validation of the criterion, and an alternative now exists that fits.** A
   patch-wise Fourier-ring-correlation measure published independently produces localised
   hallucination maps on sparse-view CT and subsampled MRI — both our modalities, both our
   undersampling regimes, at our granularity. It requires a reference, so it validates the
   criterion rather than competing with the score, and its parameters need expert-annotated
   features. **We have not run it**, and that is the honest form of this limitation; the older
   form, that no matching alternative exists, is no longer true. The one other published
   alternative disagrees with ours at `cos θ ≈ 0` and mostly flags background.
3. **One solver family carries the results, and the ablation that would have tested the
   decomposition has run and failed.** The solver replaces the range component structurally,
   confining posterior spread to the null space, so the decomposition cannot contribute on it.
   A gradient-steered solver with a null-error share of 0.69 rather than 0.99 was the test:
   under the direction criterion the score does not beat its dispersion baseline at any of its
   three operators. **This is now a permanent limitation rather than an open question.**
4. **Weight sensitivity is unmeasured on a quotable bank** (§6.5).
5. **Coverage is a mean over calibration draws, and one draw can be much worse.** Per-draw
   standard deviation 0.051, draws from 0.795 to 0.991. Why the spread is wider on the chest-CT
   bank than on the natural-image one (0.051 against 0.034) is **not established**, and we
   state it rather than fill it.
6. **One operator grid.** §6.3's negative is measured on a grid that varies view count and
   angular range as a product, which is exactly the geometry that makes a geometric distance
   hard to separate from a trivial parameter. The grid that would separate them is specified
   in §6.3 and not built.
4. **Patient budget.** Per-operator coverage is determined to about ±0.12 whatever the grid
   size; the paired contrasts are what the design supports, and we lead with them.
5. **Volume, not patient, is the clustering unit on MRI**, so MRI intervals are optimistic by
   roughly the volumes-per-patient factor.
6. **The CT forward operator is ours**, not a scanner's, and its pseudo-inverse is approximate.
7. **Single-coil MRI** via root-sum-of-squares reduction.
8. **No claim of exact coverage under continuous operator change.** Only the known-finite-set
   regime is exact.

## 8. Conclusion

Conformal reliability certificates for generative-prior reconstruction are calibrated against
one acquisition operator and silently lose their guarantee when that operator changes. We
measured the loss in two modalities with structurally different forward operators — in the
worst transfer cell a certificate promising to catch 90% of hallucinated regions catches 26.9%
— and showed that conditioning on a known finite operator set restores nominal coverage at
every operator, at a quantified cost in flagged area and false discovery rate.

We then drew the boundary of that fix. Four refinements that condition on *how far* the
operator moved rather than on *which* one it is were specified in advance and are each
unsupported: a scalar read off the operator's name outpredicts the geometric distance, and a
distance-weighted recalibration yields neither validity nor efficiency. **Operator identity is
what conditioning can use; operator distance, on this grid and this bank, is not** — and a
published impossibility result for label-free valid correction suggests the weighting route may
not be fixable by tuning.

Finally, the exact identity behind our criterion also explains a defect in how this field
validates uncertainty maps: where the generative prior does not match the anatomy, the standard
protocol is outscored by a detector reading no posterior information at all. Three extra
columns — a scale-only null, the margin over it, and an alignment measure — let a reader tell
the two regimes apart. That caution costs nothing and does not depend on our score being good,
which is why we expect it to outlive the rest of this paper.
