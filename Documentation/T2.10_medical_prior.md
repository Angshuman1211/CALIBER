## Objective

Every number the project has produced so far rests on a face model, `ddpm-celebahq-256`, so none is reportable (D-016). D79 found that the reason for deferring a medical prior since Week 1 was a misreading. Song, Shen, Xing & Ermon (ICLR 2022) trained their MRI prior on BraTS 2021, not fastMRI, so the NYU agreement does not reach it. ## 1. The checkpoint

Song et al. publish three NCSN++ checkpoints on Google Drive (`github.com/yang-song/score_inverse_problems`): BraTS 2021 brain MRI at 240², LIDC lung CT at 320², and LDCT at 512². Only the BraTS one was downloaded (`exp/brats_ncsnpp_ve/checkpoints/checkpoint_26`, 42.9 MB, sha256 `ba255793…7c93`, training step 1,300,001). It sits under `checkpoints/`, which is gitignored. The test images in the same folder were **not** downloaded: they are BraTS data, which comes with its own registration terms, and the port is checked on our own M4Raw brain scans instead.

**What it was trained on** matters for how its output is read (`brats/brats.py`, `configs/ve/brats_ncsnpp_continuous.py`):

- **All four BraTS contrasts**: T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR. M4Raw has T1, T2 and FLAIR.
- **Skull-stripped**, cropped to the brain, clipped to mean ± 6σ, and min-max scaled to [0, 1] per volume. M4Raw is not skull-stripped.
- **A continuous VE-SDE**, σ from 0.01 to 128.
- **NCSN++**: 32 base channels, `ch_mult (1, 1, 2, 2, 2)`, attention at 30², BigGAN blocks with FIR resampling. 2,676,069 parameters.

The file is a Flax `msgpack`. It is read with `msgpack` and numpy only. Unlike `torch.load` on a pickle, nothing in it can execute code, and the converted output is loaded with `weights_only=True`.

## 2. The port, and how it was verified

**`solvers/ncsnpp.py`** is NCSN++ in PyTorch, adapted from Song's own `score_sde_pytorch` (Apache-2.0, notice retained). It is cut down to the configuration all three checkpoints share: Fourier noise embedding, BigGAN blocks, `output_skip` / `input_skip` pyramids summed, skip rescaling, output scaled by 1/σ. The compiled CUDA `upfirdn2d` kernel is replaced by the pure-PyTorch fallback the reference already uses on the CPU, which needs no compiler.

**`scripts/convert_song_checkpoint.py`** maps the Flax weights onto it. Flax names a layer `<Class>_<n>` by its call order, and the port builds its modules in the same forward order, so the mapping is per class by position. Three layout changes are needed: Flax convolutions are `(kh, kw, in, out)` against torch's `(out, in, kh, kw)`; Flax dense kernels are transposed; Flax stores GroupNorm parameters as `(1, 1, 1, C)`. The conversion refuses to finish if any Flax layer is left over or any torch tensor is left unfilled. On the BraTS checkpoint: **473 tensors, 2,676,069 values, none left over.**

**Verified against Song et al.'s own model.** Their Flax NCSN++ (`models/` of `score_inverse_problems`) was run in a throwaway JAX 0.6.2 environment, with their weights, on fixed random inputs. That needed two shims, neither touching the network: a two-function stand-in for `models/utils.py`, whose other imports pull in checkpointing and a second network; and the GroupNorm reshape that today's Flax expects. The port was then run on the same inputs:

| σ | max \|torch − flax\| | relative to max \|score\| | correlation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 7.9×10⁻⁴ | 1.0×10⁻⁵ | 1.00000000 |
| 1.0 | 1.4×10⁻⁵ | 4.8×10⁻⁶ | 1.00000000 |
| 50 | 6.7×10⁻⁷ | 1.1×10⁻⁵ | 1.00000000 |

**That is float32 rounding.** The layer mapping, the three layout changes and the FIR resampling are all right. JAX is not a project dependency, so `tests/test_ncsnpp.py` keeps the check alive without it: on any machine with the converted checkpoint, it compares the port against values recorded from this Flax run.

## 3. The adapter: a score model behind a noise-prediction interface

The solvers (DDNM, DPS, the flow sampler) ask a prior for ε in DDPM's VP form, x_t = √a·x₀ + √(1−a)·ε. A VE score model returns s(x, σ) = ∇ log p_σ(x) for x = x₀ + σz. The two are one change of variables apart, and it is exact:

σ = √((1−a)/a),  x_VE = x_t / √a,  ε = −σ · s(x_VE, σ)  (Tweedie)

`SongVEScoreDenoiser` implements that. It carries **the prior's own schedule**: a = 1/(1+σ²) on the log-uniform σ grid from 0.01 to 128 that the model was trained and sampled on. The DDPM linear schedule would ask it for σ ≈ 157 at the first step, beyond its training range. `build_denoiser` chooses the adapter from what the checkpoint directory holds, so a config names the prior the same way as before (`checkpoint: checkpoints/song_inverse_problems/brats_ncsnpp_ve`), and the face-prior path is unchanged.

The algebra is tested against a prior where the answer is known in closed form. For x₀ ~ N(μ, τ²), the exact VE score gives, through the adapter, the optimal E[ε | x_t] = √(1−a)(x_t − √a·μ)/(aτ² + 1 − a) at every timestep tested. So the adapter is checked against the answer rather than against itself.

## 4. Is it a prior for our data?

The BraTS model has never seen M4Raw. Before building a bank on it, a direct check: add Gaussian noise of known σ to M4Raw slices (6 volumes, centre-cropped to 240²), apply the model's own denoiser x + σ²·s(x, σ), and compare errors.

| σ | per-slice max normalisation: PSNR noisy → denoised | gain | p99 normalisation: gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 34.0 → 35.4 dB | 1.4× | 1.0× |
| 0.05 | 26.0 → 31.9 dB | 3.9× | 2.3× |
| 0.10 | 20.0 → 30.0 dB | 9.9× | 5.3× |
| 0.20 | 14.0 → 27.7 dB | 23.2× | 12.5× |
| 0.50 | 6.0 → 23.3 dB | 54.0× | 37.8× |

**It denoises M4Raw brains strongly, 10× at σ = 0.1.** It does so only when the images are in the range it was trained on. Under the project's usual p99 normalisation, 1% of pixels sit above 1 (up to 1.9) and the gain roughly halves. The M4Raw bank therefore uses `normalisation: max`: per slice, where BraTS normalised per volume.

## 5. A first look at D79's prediction, before the bank

D79 wrote a prediction down before any run: a medical prior should *raise* cos θ, the alignment between the reconstruction's hidden content and the truth's, and so weaken the collapse that has made every magnitude label a function of the score (D67). On a smoke bank (2 volumes × the 9 MRI operators, DDNM, 24 steps), set beside the face prior on **the same M4Raw substrate** (Track 1's `banks/w02_solver_smoke`):

| | median cos θ | sd | Spearman(`err_free`, hypot(`s_free`, `free_content`)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| face prior, M4Raw (`w02_solver_smoke`, 128², 12 steps) | +0.073 | 0.089 | +0.971 |
| face prior, fastMRI (bank of record) | +0.064 | 0.118 | +0.990 |
| **BraTS prior, M4Raw** (smoke, 240², 24 steps) | **+0.674** | 0.275 | **+0.633** |

**The prediction holds on the smoke bank.** The face prior's invented content is orthogonal to the truth (cos θ ≈ 0), which is exactly the condition under which err_free ≈ hypot(s_free, free_content). The BraTS prior's points the same way as the truth's, and the magnitude label falls from near-determined by the score (0.97) to moderately related (0.63). Resolution and step count differ between the two M4Raw banks, and this is 18 pairs; the full bank (§6) is the measurement. DDNM still behaves as before: the median null-error share is 0.994, and the identity holds to 6×10⁻¹⁴.

## 6. The first medical-prior bank

**The bank.** All 240 M4Raw validation volumes (30 patients) × the 9 MRI operators: 2,160 pairs, 486,000 regions (15 × 15 per slice). It ran on the BraTS prior with DDNM, 24 steps, 4 draws, at about 3.5 s/pair, in 2.3 h. The run stopped twice and resumed from its checkpoint both times (§9). The label is D-061's, valued on the bank's own design split: ε = 0.193.

### 6a. D79's prediction, on the full bank

| | median cos θ | sd | Spearman(`err_free`, hypot(`s_free`, `free_content`)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| face prior, M4Raw (`w02_solver_smoke`) | +0.073 | 0.089 | +0.971 |
| **BraTS prior, M4Raw, full bank** | **+0.596** | 0.264 | **+0.647** |

**The prediction holds.** The medical prior's invented content points the same way as the truth's, and the magnitude label stops being the score's own identity. cos θ's own dependence on `s_free` is moderate (Spearman +0.480), so the direction label is not independent of the score either. D74 said as much.

### 6b. The coverage failure is far stronger, and the fix holds

Naive transfer against group-conditional calibration, both directions, intervals clustered by patient (D-027):

| Deployed at | Asc. (from `random1d R2`): naive | flag area | group-cond. | Desc. (from `random1d R8`): naive | flag area | group-cond. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `equispaced1d R4` | 0.964 | 0.428 | 0.903 | 0.990 | 0.557 | 0.898 |
| `equispaced1d R8` | 0.921 | 0.429 | 0.894 | 0.902 | 0.424 | 0.893 |
| `random1d R2` | *(calib.)* 0.868 | 0.223 | 0.868 | 0.943 | 0.403 | 0.853 |
| `random1d R4` | 0.963 | 0.432 | 0.909 | 0.982 | 0.504 | 0.905 |
| `random1d R8` | 0.917 | 0.425 | 0.895 | *(calib.)* 0.889 | 0.415 | 0.889 |
| `variable_density2d R2` | **0.000** | 0.000 | 0.857 | **0.000** | 0.000 | 0.800 |
| `variable_density2d R4` | **0.016** | 0.002 | 0.890 | **0.013** | 0.002 | 0.901 |
| `variable_density2d R8` | **0.118** | 0.032 | 0.901 | **0.159** | 0.043 | 0.907 |

`equispaced1d R2` has **no positive regions** under ε (0 on the test side), so coverage is undefined there. DDNM reconstructs it too well for any region to cross the threshold.

**Calibrated on a 1-D mask and deployed on a 2-D variable-density mask, naive transfer flags almost nothing.** Flag area falls from ~0.4 to 0.000–0.043, and coverage to **0.000–0.159**. On the face prior, the worst naive cell on fastMRI was 0.62 (T2.7). Group-conditional calibration restores 0.80–0.91 at the same cells.

**Robust over 13 patient splits** (`split_noise_floor.py`, ε re-valued on each split's own design side, 0.183–0.196):

- Naive transfer to `variable_density2d` misses nominal in **13 of 13 splits at R2, R4 and R8, in both directions**. Its split means are 0.000, 0.017 and 0.125 ascending, and 0.000, 0.013 and 0.157 descending.
- Group-conditional calibration has split means of 0.882–0.896 at those cells, and 0.893 over every cell (sd 0.018, range 0.854–0.923).

**The gap is not ordered by the scalar distance on MRI.** `random1d R2` → `variable_density2d R2` changes the mask family at the same acceleration, so `null_space_growth` there is **0.000**, and it is the worst failure in the bank. Naive gap against signed distance has Spearman −0.23 ascending and −0.09 descending (n = 7, the no-positive cell excluded). T2.8 found MRI's worst failures sat at matched acceleration across families; on the medical prior that pattern is no longer a weak trend, it is the whole effect.

### 6c. Detection: the medical prior does not rescue it

`label_comparison.py` (D74), per operator, direction-agnostic AUCs against the anatomy control `free_content`:

| label | score AUC | anatomy AUC | operators where the score beats anatomy by ≥ 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| magnitude (`err_free > 0.193`) | 0.921–0.997 | 0.878–0.999 | **1 of 8** |
| direction (cos θ < 0.404, the design q25) | 0.526–0.731 | 0.556–0.754 | **1 of 8** (`variable_density2d R8`, 50 positives) |

D79 predicted that cos θ would rise (it does, §6a). The hope behind the prediction was that the score would then show real detection on the direction label. **It does not.** Under the direction label the score ranks *below* the anatomy control at all five 1-D-mask operators (excess −0.09 to −0.12). The magnitude label no longer saturates, but the anatomy control rises with it: once cos θ moves off zero, `err_free` depends on `free_content` directly.

Stratified by `free_content` tercile within operator (`stratified_detection.py`), the 1-D operators read "invention-driven", with score AUC 0.94–0.96 against anatomy 0.77–0.85. But **`s_disagree` alone does better than the combined score at every one of them (0.90–0.99)**, and `s_free` alone is weak (0.49–0.69). On this prior the posterior spread carries the detection, not the null-space magnitude the score is built on. At the 2-D operators everything is anatomy-confounded.

### 6d. The baseline on the coverage axis, and the weights

- **D73 against `s_disagree`.** Both detectors lose most of their coverage going to the 2-D masks (penalties 0.73–0.94). Two-sided, over 14 interpretable cells: `s_disagree` transfers better at 5, CALIBER at 6, and 3 are within 0.02. **No consistent winner.** Unlike on the face prior, neither detector's transferred threshold inflates its flag area: the failures are under-coverage, not over-flagging.
- **D76.** The R8 → R2 restoration is established at **8 of 11** weightings (w_free 0–0.7); at equal weights it is **+0.107 [+0.092, +0.126]**. It reverses where the score is nearly `s_free` alone (w_free ≥ 0.9). From R2 → R8 there is nothing to restore: the transferred threshold over-covers at every weighting but w_free = 1.

## 7. DPS on the medical prior: no admissible guidance at the DDNM budget

D68's ablation needs a DPS arm on this prior, and D-031 requires DPS's guidance strength ζ to be measured afresh for a new prior. ζ = 5 came from the face prior and means nothing here. The sweep ran on **design patients only** (`w11_m4raw_brats_dps_sweep.yaml`, `keep_splits: [design]`): 8 volumes × the three `random1d` operators, 24 steps, 4 draws (`stage2_decomposition.py`).

| ζ | null-error share | range error | null error | data-consistency error | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.094 | 0.90–0.93 | 0.25–0.32 | 0.955 | fails D-033 |
| 5 | 0.233 | 0.52–0.57 | 0.24–0.32 | 0.566 | fails D-033 |
| 20 | 0.954 | 0.10–3.15 | 0.36–**64** | 1.269 | **diverged** at R2 |
| 50 | 1.000 | 0.23–0.44 | **294–801** | 0.319 | **diverged** |

(Errors are relative to the truth's norm, so 1 is the error of reconstructing zeros.)

**At 24 steps DPS either barely uses the data or blows up.** Below ζ = 20 most of its error is in the range, where the measurements pin the image; that is D-033's "unconverged fit". From ζ = 20 its null-space error grows to tens or hundreds of times the image.

**The selection rule chose ζ = 50, and that was a bug, now fixed.** `select_guidance.py` applied D-033's gate, null-error share > 0.5, and then picked the best data fit. A diverged sampler has an enormous null-space error, which drives its *share* to 1.000, so it passed the gate. The DPS bank built on ζ = 50 was stopped within a minute, before it produced anything. The rule now also excludes any setting whose relative error exceeds 1, i.e. worse than zeros. On this sweep it refuses all four values. A regression test pins the real case (share 1.000, null error 801).

A second sweep at **100 steps** (ζ = 1, 2, 5, 10, design patients) is how to find out whether DPS can be made admissible on this prior at all. *Pending: running.* If it can, the DPS arm costs ~4× the DDNM budget, which the ablation would have to state.

## 8. The CT prior: LIDC, ported and verified

The same procedure as BraTS. Song et al.'s LIDC checkpoint (`exp/ct2d_320_ncsnpp_ve/checkpoints/checkpoint_101`, 66 MB, step 1,000,000) has 4,119,015 parameters at 320², with seven resolution levels and attention at 20². The first conversion **refused**, on parameter-free `Downsample_*` / `Upsample_*` layers the BraTS file did not have. These are the pyramids' FIR resamplers, which this older Flax version wrote as empty dicts. The converter now skips layers with no tensors, and still refuses any leftover layer that has some (one more test). Converted: 645 tensors, none left over.

**Against Song et al.'s Flax model at 320²:**

| σ | max \|torch − flax\| | relative | correlation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 7.4×10⁻⁴ | 1.3×10⁻⁵ | 1.00000000 |
| 1.0 | 4.0×10⁻⁵ | 1.4×10⁻⁵ | 1.00000000 |
| 50 | 2.2×10⁻⁷ | 4.6×10⁻⁶ | 1.00000000 |

LIDC was trained on chest CT, min-max normalised to [0, 1] per volume. The project's CT pipeline maps a fixed HU window to [0, 1] instead, and TotalSegmentator slices span the body.

**D79's prediction on CT, smoke size** (`w11_ct_lidc_smoke_gpu.yaml`: 2 TotalSegmentator subjects × the 9 CT operators at 320², DDNM, 24 steps; 18 pairs, 7,200 regions, 83 s/pair, 25 min):

| | median cos θ | sd | Spearman(`err_free`, hypot(`s_free`, `free_content`)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| face prior, CT (bank of record, 900 pairs) | +0.074 | 0.162 | +0.991 |
| **LIDC prior, CT** (smoke, 18 pairs) | **+0.976** | 0.119 | **+0.486** |
| face prior, MRI (M4Raw) | +0.073 | 0.089 | +0.971 |
| **BraTS prior, MRI** (M4Raw, full) | **+0.596** | 0.264 | **+0.647** |

**The same in both modalities, and stronger on CT.** A medical prior's invented content points the way the truth's does. The magnitude label stops being the score's own identity: 0.99 → 0.49 on CT, 0.97 → 0.65 on MRI. On CT this is two subjects, so it is a direction, not yet a measurement. A full CT bank on LIDC at 320² is ~100 subjects × 9 operators at ~80 s/pair, about 20 h on this GPU.

## 10. What Track 1 has to rule

The prior's domain is recorded as **`mri_brain`**, and no modality admits it yet (`AWAITING_RULING`, D-067). Every bank on it is stamped not reportable. That is deliberate:

1. **Is an anatomy-matched medical prior admissible?** Recorded as `mri`, the current table would admit the brain prior for every MRI bank, fastMRI *knees* included. That is cross-anatomy, which D79 itself distinguishes.
2. **Can M4Raw carry a reportable coverage claim?** D-015 made it a development substrate: 0.3 T, and no pathology annotations, so it cannot close the annotation-linked evaluation (Sec. 14). Coverage under operator shift does not use annotations. Whether that is enough is Track 1's call.

If both are yes, the change is one line: move `mri_brain` from `AWAITING_RULING` into `ADMISSIBLE_DOMAINS["mri"]`, and set M4Raw's `reportable`.

## Honest limitations

- **Not reportable,** pending the two rulings of §10.
- **30 patients.** This machine holds M4Raw's validation split only: 240 volumes from 30 subjects, each scanned 2–3 times per contrast. That leaves 3 design patients for ε. The 2-D-mask failure is far outside its intervals, but the subtler cells are not. M4Raw's full 183 subjects would need ~15 GB that D: does not have.
- **`equispaced1d R2` has no positives** under ε, so one cell of the grid is undefined, and it is dropped from every rank correlation.
- **BraTS is skull-stripped; M4Raw is not.** Skull, scalp and background are outside the prior's training distribution, and its reconstructions of them are extrapolation.
- **The grid is fixed by the network** at 240², so 15 × 15 regions, not the fastMRI bank's 16 × 16. No per-region number carries over between the two.
- **The weights have no stated licence** (D79). That is ordinary for a cited preprint and a real question for the O7 release.
- **Two of three checkpoints.** BraTS and LIDC are converted and verified; LDCT (512²) is not downloaded. `CONFIGS` in the converter already holds its settings.
- **No DPS arm yet.** D68 on the medical prior waits on the 100-step sweep (§7).
