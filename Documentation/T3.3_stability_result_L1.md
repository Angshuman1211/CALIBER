## 1. Setting, made precise

$X_1,\dots,X_{n+1} \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} P_X$ — true images from a shared population. This is not a convenience assumption here: it is literally how the experiment is built (D8 — fastMRI is *retrospectively* undersampled from the same acquired scans at different rates, so calibration and deployment draws genuinely come from the same underlying image population by construction).

For $i=1,\dots,n$ (calibration): $Z_i = (A_{\text{cal}}, X_i, Y_i, R_i)$, with $Y_i = A_{\text{cal}}X_i + \varepsilon_i$ and $R_i = s(A_{\text{cal}}, X_i, Y_i)$, where $s$ is T1.1's fixed hallucination score.
For $i=n+1$ (test/deployment): $Z_{n+1} = (A_{\text{deploy}}, X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1}, R_{n+1})$, defined analogously.

Two structural facts, both already established and load-bearing here:
- **$s$ is fixed, not refit on calibration data** (T1.1 design; confirmed as the split-conformal case in T3.1 §3.3/§5). This means $R_i$ depends only on $(A_{\text{cal or deploy}}, X_i, Y_i)$ — a coordinatewise function of $Z_i$ alone, not of the rest of the batch.
- **$Z_1,\dots,Z_{n+1}$ are mutually independent** — $X_i$ iid, $\varepsilon_i$ independent noise, and the operator assigned to index $i$ is deterministic (not random) given whether $i\leq n$ or $i=n+1$.

This is exactly the setting the risk register (blueprint §21) calls "the failure" and the first line of the experimental protocol (§16) — no group-conditional partitioning yet, just the basic naive-calibration-transfer case.

## 2. Step 1 — collapse the coverage gap to a single distributional distance

**This step is a direct, checked consequence of the results verified in T3.1. No new assumption is introduced here.**

Because $R$ is coordinatewise (§1), the score sequence $(R_1,\dots,R_{n+1})$ is itself an independent sequence, and swapping indices $i$ and $n+1$ in the *raw* data $Z \to Z^i$ produces exactly the same index-swap in the *score* sequence: $R(Z^i)$ is $(R_1,\dots,R_{n+1})$ with entries $i$ and $n+1$ exchanged. This means Lemma 1 from [3] (verified in T3.1 §3.3, "Lemma `dtv_swap`") — stated for any independent sequence — applies **directly to the score sequence itself**, not only to the raw data:

$$d_{TV}\big(R(Z), R(Z^i)\big) \;\leq\; 2\, d_{TV}(R_i, R_{n+1}) \;=\; 2\, d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}), \quad i=1,\dots,n,$$

where $Q_{\text{cal}}$ is the law of $s(A_{\text{cal}}, X, A_{\text{cal}}X+\varepsilon)$ for $X\sim P_X$, and $Q_{\text{deploy}}$ is defined analogously with $A_{\text{deploy}}$. The right-hand side is the **same quantity for every $i$**, because every calibration point shares the same operator $A_{\text{cal}}$ — a simplification specific to this project's two-regime structure that is not present in [3]'s fully general treatment (which allows a different distance for every index).

Substituting into Theorem 4 (T3.1 §3.3) and using $\sum_{i=1}^n \tilde{w}_i < 1$ (immediate from the normalized-weight definition, §3.3):

$$\boxed{\ \text{Coverage gap} \;\leq\; 2\, d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}})\ }$$

This already matches blueprint L1's billing ("a stability argument bounding the change in the score distribution... by the operator distance") in form — the coverage gap is bounded by a distance between two score *distributions*. What remains is replacing that abstract distributional distance with something computable from the operators alone (blueprint §12's actual ask), which is where new project-specific content is required.

## 2b. Step 1 restated — Kolmogorov, label-conditional, per image (T3.5 §2, §6)

§2 above is a correct consequence of [3]'s Theorem 4, but it is looser than needed and is stated on the wrong law and the wrong independence unit. Three changes, each checked numerically in `T3.5_numerical_verification.md`.

**(a) A threshold event needs only the Kolmogorov distance.** Let $S_1,\dots,S_n \sim Q_{\text{cal}}$ be independent, $\hat q$ the conformal quantile (a function of them alone), and $S \sim Q_{\text{deploy}}$ independent of them. Then $P(S \le \hat q \mid S_{1:n}) = F_{\text{dep}}(\hat q)$. Ordinary split-conformal validity gives $\mathbb{E}[F_{\text{cal}}(\hat q)] \ge 1-\alpha$, and $|F_{\text{dep}} - F_{\text{cal}}| \le d_K$ pointwise, so
$$\mathbb{E}\,[F_{\text{dep}}(\hat q)] \;\ge\; 1-\alpha - d_K(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}), \qquad d_K = \sup_t |F_{\text{cal}}(t) - F_{\text{dep}}(t)|.$$
Mirrored to the repo's lower-tail rule (flag when score $\ge \tau$, $\tau$ the $k$-th smallest positive, $k=\lfloor \alpha(n+1)\rfloor$): miss rate at deployment $\le \alpha + d_K$. Since $d_K \le d_{TV}$ this is tighter than §2's boxed $2\,d_{TV}$, has no factor 2, and does not use Theorem 4.

**(b) The law is the label-conditional one.** The risk T1.2 controls is the miss rate among hallucinated regions, and `split_threshold` fits on positives only. So $Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}$ in (a) are the laws of the score **given `label = 1`** at each operator. These are not the unconditional laws §2 bounds: on the prior harness conditioning raises $d_K$ from ≈0.40 to 0.56–0.62, and the label-conditional under-coverage gap (+0.52) exceeds the unconditional $d_K$ (0.42) in the worst cell (T3.5 §6.1). With each gap compared to the KS of its own law, the bound holds in all twelve cells tested.

**(c) The independence unit is the image.** (a) needs independent calibration scores. Regions of one image are not independent. **Proposition (cluster version).** Calibrate on $m$ independent images with $r$ regions each ($n = mr$ pooled scores), let $Q_{\text{cal}}$ be the law of the score of a uniformly chosen region of a random calibration image, and let the test region come from a fresh deployment image. Then for every $\varepsilon>0$ and integer $K\ge 1$,
$$\mathbb{E}\,[F_{\text{dep}}(\hat q)] \;\ge\; 1-\alpha - d_K(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) - \varepsilon - \tfrac1K - 2K e^{-2m\varepsilon^2}.$$
*Proof.* Write the pooled empirical CDF as $\hat F = \frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m H_j$ with $H_j(t) = \frac1r\sum_{\text{regions of image } j}\mathbf 1\{S\le t\}$. The $H_j$ are i.i.d. $[0,1]$-valued non-decreasing processes with $\mathbb{E}H_j = F_{\text{cal}}$. Fix grid points $t_1<\dots<t_K$ with $F_{\text{cal}}(t_k)=k/K$ (or the nearest atoms). Hoeffding at each grid point plus a union bound gives $P(\max_k|\hat F(t_k)-F_{\text{cal}}(t_k)|>\varepsilon)\le 2Ke^{-2m\varepsilon^2}$; monotonicity of both CDFs between grid points adds $1/K$, so off that event $\sup_t|\hat F-F_{\text{cal}}|\le\varepsilon+1/K$. The conformal quantile has $\hat F(\hat q)\ge\lceil(1-\alpha)(n+1)\rceil/n\ge 1-\alpha$, hence $F_{\text{cal}}(\hat q)\ge 1-\alpha-\varepsilon-1/K$ off the bad event, and (a)'s pointwise step finishes the proof. $\square$

The rate is $1/\sqrt m$ in **images**, not regions. With $m=100$ the penalty is ≈0.26 (loose; observed no-shift deficit ≈0.002) and for $m<50$ it is vacuous, which is the honest reading of T3.5 §6.2's 0.765 coverage at $m=2$. It needs equal region counts per image; the label-conditional version has a random number of positives per image and uses the ratio-of-sums form of the Hoeffding step, proved next.

**(d) The label-conditional, cluster-level proposition.** Now let the calibrator use only positives, as `split_threshold` does. Item $j$ has regions $(S_{jk}, \Lambda_{jk})$, $k\le r$, and the items are i.i.d. Put $P_j = \frac1r\sum_k \Lambda_{jk}$ (positive fraction), $A_j(t) = \frac1r\sum_k \Lambda_{jk}\mathbf 1\{S_{jk}\le t\}$, $\bar p = \mathbb{E}P_j > 0$, $A = \mathbb{E}A_j$, and define the positive-region law $F^+_{\text{cal}} = A/\bar p$ (the score of a positive region drawn with probability proportional to positives; $F^+_{\text{dep}}$ likewise). The pooled empirical positive CDF is the **ratio of sums** $\hat F^+ = \bar A/\hat p$ with $\bar A = \frac1m\sum_j A_j$, $\hat p = \frac1m\sum_j P_j$.

**Proposition.** For every $\eta\in(0,\bar p)$ and integer $K\ge1$, with $\varepsilon = 2\eta/(\bar p-\eta)$, the miss rate at deployment satisfies
$$\mathbb{E}\,[\text{miss}_{\text{dep}}] \;\le\; \alpha + d_K\big(Q^+_{\text{cal}}, Q^+_{\text{deploy}}\big) + \varepsilon + \tfrac1K + 2(K+1)\,e^{-2m\eta^2}.$$
*Proof.* (1) *Algebra.* $\frac{\bar A}{\hat p}-\frac{A}{\bar p} = \frac{(\bar A - A) + F^+_{\text{cal}}(\bar p - \hat p)}{\hat p}$, and $F^+_{\text{cal}}\le1$, so $\big|\hat F^+(t) - F^+_{\text{cal}}(t)\big| \le \dfrac{|\bar A(t) - A(t)| + |\hat p - \bar p|}{\hat p}$ (pinned in `test_ratio_of_sums_lemma_is_exact_algebra`). (2) *Concentration.* $A_j(t)\in[0,1]$ and $P_j\in[0,1]$ are i.i.d., so Hoeffding gives $P(|\bar A(t)-A(t)|>\eta)\le 2e^{-2m\eta^2}$ at each of $K$ grid points $t_k$ with $F^+_{\text{cal}}(t_k)=k/K$, and $P(|\hat p-\bar p|>\eta)\le 2e^{-2m\eta^2}$. A union bound over these $K+1$ events fails with probability $\le 2(K+1)e^{-2m\eta^2}$; off it, $\hat p\ge\bar p-\eta$ and step (1) gives $|\hat F^+-F^+_{\text{cal}}|\le\varepsilon$ at every grid point. (3) *Grid.* $\hat F^+$ and $F^+_{\text{cal}}$ are non-decreasing, so for $t\in[t_k,t_{k+1}]$: $\hat F^+(t)\le\hat F^+(t_{k+1})\le F^+_{\text{cal}}(t_{k+1})+\varepsilon\le F^+_{\text{cal}}(t)+\tfrac1K+\varepsilon$; hence $\sup_t(\hat F^+-F^+_{\text{cal}})\le\varepsilon+\frac1K$. (4) *Conformal step.* With $N$ pooled positives and $k=\lfloor\alpha(N+1)\rfloor$, the threshold $\tau$ is the $k$-th smallest positive score, so $\hat F^+(\tau^-)=(k-1)/N\le\alpha$ (if $k<1$ the rule flags everything and the miss rate is 0). Then $F^+_{\text{cal}}(\tau^-)\le\alpha+\varepsilon+\frac1K$, and for a fresh positive region at deployment $P(S<\tau)=F^+_{\text{dep}}(\tau^-)\le F^+_{\text{cal}}(\tau^-)+d_K^+$. On the bad event bound the miss rate by 1. $\square$

It is loose in the same way as (c), and the positive fraction enters twice: $\varepsilon$ blows up as $\bar p\to0$ (rare hallucinations make the image-level penalty large), which is the cluster-level form of D3's thin-group warning. Grid check of the Hoeffding events: `test_ratio_of_sums_hoeffding_events_for_variable_positives_per_image`.

**What stays from §2.** The Theorem-4 route remains a correct, weaker statement for the unconditional law and exchangeable-up-to-TV swaps. It is no longer the headline.

## 3. Step 2 — relate $d_K(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}})$ to a computable operator distance (sketch, two flagged assumptions; **unconditional law, fixed solver**)

**This step is new derivation, not something [3] supplies. Treat it as a sketch of the right shape of argument, not a proof.** Scope after T3.5: it controls the **unconditional** score law, and only for a **fixed solver** (the score is a function of the reconstruction, which depends on $A$ through the solver — T1.1's "Lipschitz conditional on the reconstruction"). The coupling in (i) cannot be run on the label-conditional laws, because under a common $X$ the label itself changes with $A$; for those laws Step 2 has no derivation and $d_K^{\text{cond}}$ is measured directly (T3.5 §6.1, §7; T3.6).

$Q_{\text{cal}}$ and $Q_{\text{deploy}}$ are laws of the same score function evaluated on independent draws from the same $P_X$, under different operators. A direct TV bound between two continuous-valued marginals via a "how far apart are typical values" argument runs into a standard problem: coupling $X$ across the two regimes bounds a *smoother* distance (Wasserstein-1) for free, but converting that into total variation needs an extra regularity condition. Concretely:

**(i) Coupling step (uncontroversial).** Since both $Q_{\text{cal}}$ and $Q_{\text{deploy}}$ are driven by the same $P_X$, use the identical-$X$ coupling: draw one $X\sim P_X$ and evaluate the score under both operators. This gives, by a standard Kantorovich argument,
$$W_1(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}) \;\leq\; \mathbb{E}_X\Big[\big| s(A_{\text{deploy}}, X, \cdot) - s(A_{\text{cal}}, X, \cdot)\big|\Big].$$

**(ii) Flagged assumption 1 — Lipschitz score. Revised after reading T1.1's actual score code and running a numerical check.**

*If* $s(A, X, \cdot)$ is $L$-Lipschitz in $A$ with respect to $d_{\text{op}}$ (the aggregate null-space projection distance, meanwhile ratified project-wide in T1.3), for $X$ in the typical region under $P_X$, then $\mathbb{E}_X[\,\cdot\,] \leq L \cdot d_{\text{op}}(A_{\text{cal}}, A_{\text{deploy}})$.

**A favorable structural fact, found in `src/caliber/scoring/score.py`, not assumed here:** T1.1's combination rule (D25) is deliberately an affine, non-negative weighted sum of the three components, specifically *because* "any non-negative weighted sum of Lipschitz components is Lipschitz with $L=\sum_i w_i L_i$" — the code cites this task by name. A ratio-based combination was rejected for exactly this reason (it diverges as the denominator vanishes, which is not Lipschitz). This means assumption (ii) is not one monolithic claim about the whole score — it reduces to three separate, more tractable claims, one per component ($s_{\text{free}}$, $s_{\text{disagree}}$, $s_{\text{residual}}$), each of which only needs to be Lipschitz on its own for the combination to inherit the property automatically, with $L$ a known function of the per-component constants and the (frozen, per D27) combination weights.

**Numerical check run: `scripts/t3_lipschitz_synthetic_check.py`.** This does *not* call T1.1's actual `compute_score_components` — it is a self-contained synthetic proxy (nested 1D masked-sampling operators, for which $d_{\text{op}}$ has the exact closed form used in `operators/distance.py`; a smoothness-informed reference score combining null-space energy, cross-sample disagreement, and measurement residual via interpolation-based prior fills rather than pure noise, so the score depends on actual signal content, not just randomness). The result, run with a fixed calibration operator ($R=4$) against a nested sweep of deployment operators ($R=5,\dots,64$):

| R | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $d_{\text{op}}$ | 0.000 | 0.225 | 0.286 | 0.354 | 0.410 | 0.433 | 0.446 | 0.468 | 0.484 |
| $\mathbb{E}_X[\lvert\Delta\text{score}\rvert]/d_{\text{op}}$ | — | 0.374 | 0.391 | 0.443 | 0.469 | 0.492 | 0.529 | 0.676 | 0.838 |

$d_{\text{op}}$ itself behaves exactly as T1.3 reported (graded, non-saturating, growth slowing as $R$ increases — consistent with their table, a useful cross-check that the closed-form formula is implemented consistently between the two tasks). **The ratio is not constant. It more than doubles across the sweep, and a finite-difference local-slope estimate grows roughly tenfold from the small-$R$ end to the large-$R$ end.** A single global Lipschitz constant $L$ taken as the worst-case observed ratio would still make §3's final bound technically valid over the range actually tested (any function's steepest observed local slope is a valid, if loose, global Lipschitz bound on a bounded range) — but treating $L$ as one fixed, small number that applies uniformly, which is what assumption (ii) originally implied, is not supported by this evidence.

**Re-run against T1.1's actual score code: `scripts/t3_lipschitz_real_score_check.py`. The picture changes, and not in the direction the proxy suggested.** This calls the real `compute_score_components`/`combine_components`, the real `MRIUndersamplingOperator` (128×128 Cartesian k-space masks, matching T1.3's own $R=2$ calibration choice for direct comparability), and the real `PhantomDataset` — the module Track 2 built with a stated second purpose of serving exactly this task. Scales were estimated once from the calibration operator only and frozen before touching any deployment operator, per D27's explicit requirement (re-estimating them per operator would have hidden the very effect being measured). Result, pooled over 16 regions × 120 phantom draws per $R$:

| $d_{\text{op}}$ | 0.000 | 0.405 | 0.500 | 0.580 | 0.612 | 0.644 | 0.661 |
| $\mathbb{E}_X[\lvert\Delta\text{score}\rvert]/d_{\text{op}}$ | — | 0.137 | 0.167 | 0.198 | 0.183 | 0.186 | 0.184 |

($d_{\text{op}}$ here matches T1.3's own table to three decimal places at every shared $R$ — a strong independent cross-check that both tasks compute the same registered distance consistently.)

**The ratio rises from $R=3$ to $R=6$, then plateaus around 0.18–0.20 through $R=16$ — it does not keep growing.** This is a materially different, more favorable finding than the synthetic proxy gave, on the actual score the project will actually use. The most likely explanation is that the proxy's crude linear-interpolation fill was a poor stand-in for a real projector-based null/range decomposition — it is not that the earlier finding was fabricated or wrong on its own terms, it is that it was explicitly flagged as untested against the real thing (§3(ii)'s original caveat), and now that it has been tested, the real thing behaves better.

**What this means, revised from the earlier draft of this section:** the case for treating $L$ as a genuinely free, ill-posedness-dependent function (rather than a single practical constant) is weaker than the proxy suggested — over the range actually exercised by the experimental protocol ($R=2$ to $16$, one mask family, phantom images), the ratio looks bounded, not runaway. §4's connection to L2 still has a real target (explaining the *rise* between $R=3$ and $R=6$, and behavior beyond $R=16$ or under other mask families remains untested), but "an operator that hides more permits weaker guarantees" is no longer the most urgent framing — **"the ratio appears to plateau, but only three mask configurations beyond the rise have been tested, and only on synthetic phantoms, not real fastMRI data"** is the honest one. **Caveats that still apply:** one mask family (`random1d`), one image type (synthetic phantoms, not real anatomy), one region size, default equal-thirds weights, and $R$ capped at 16 because `center_fraction=0.04` saturates the mask beyond that — none of this validates the bound for CT, for real data, or for more extreme acceleration.

**(iii) Density-regularity condition — corrected. The earlier claim in this document ($d_{TV}\le M\cdot W_1$, linear) was imprecise and is replaced below by a derived inequality, worked out from scratch here rather than cited from anywhere.**

The score is real-valued (a scalar per region, per T1.1), so both $Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}$ are 1-D distributions, and two exact identities apply: writing $G(t) = F_{Q_{\text{cal}}}(t) - F_{Q_{\text{deploy}}}(t)$ for the difference of their CDFs,
$$d_K(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) = \sup_t |G(t)|, \qquad W_1(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) = \int |G(t)|\,dt.$$
(An earlier draft wrote the first as $d_{TV}$; it is the Kolmogorov distance. See the note after the boxed inequality.)
These are two *different norms of the same function* $G$ — sup-norm and $L^1$-norm — so bounding one by the other in general is impossible without control on how fast $G$ can change, i.e. on $G' = q_{\text{cal}} - q_{\text{deploy}}$ (the density difference), which is where a regularity condition genuinely has to enter.

**Elementary derivation.** Suppose $\sup_t|G'(t)| \leq D$ (a bound on the density *difference*, not on either density alone) and $G(t)\to 0$ as $t\to\pm\infty$ (true for any two CDFs). Let $S=d_K=\sup_t|G(t)|$, attained at $t_0$. Since $|G'|\le D$, $|G(t)-G(t_0)|\le D|t-t_0|$, so $|G(t)|\ge S-D|t-t_0|$ for $t$ within $S/D$ of $t_0$ — a "tent" of base $2S/D$ and height $S$ sits entirely under $|G|$ there, contributing at least $S^2/D$ (the tent's area) to $\int|G|$. Hence $W_1 \ge S^2/D$, i.e.
$$\boxed{\ d_{K}(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) \;\leq\; \sqrt{D \cdot W_1(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}})}\ }$$
where $D=\sup_t|q_{\text{cal}}(t)-q_{\text{deploy}}(t)|$ (bounded by, e.g., $2M$ if both densities are pointwise bounded by $M$). This is a genuine square-root relationship, not the linear one originally guessed — a function with a bounded rate of change cannot have a tall spike without also having real area under it, and that trade-off is what the derivation above makes precise.

> **Correction (T3.5 §2).** The first identity above was previously written with $d_{TV}$; it is the Kolmogorov distance, $d_K \le d_{TV}$. The tent argument proves the $d_K$ bound only. $d_{TV} \le \sqrt{D\,W_1}$ is **false** in general: for $q_a = 1$, $q_b = 1+\tfrac12\sin(2\pi k x)$ on $[0,1]$, $D=\tfrac12$ and $d_{TV}=0.159$ for every $k$ while $d_K$ and $W_1$ shrink like $1/k$ (T3.5 §2 table; pinned in `test_total_variation_can_exceed_sqrt_d_w1`). Nothing downstream needs the TV version: §2b(a) bounds the gap by $d_K$ directly. **$D$ is a KDE quantity** — it moves by a factor ≈2 across a 16× change of bandwidth (T3.5 §3) — so it is a smoothing-scale-dependent constant and the quantities to carry into T3.6 are the exact empirical $d_K$ and $W_1$.

**Combining (i), (ii) and the corrected (iii):**

$$\text{Coverage gap} \;\leq\; d_K(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) \;\leq\; \sqrt{D\cdot W_1(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}})} \;\leq\; \sqrt{D L}\cdot\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}(A_{\text{cal}}, A_{\text{deploy}})},$$
valid for the unconditional law and a fixed solver; for the label-conditional law only the first inequality applies, with $d_K^{\text{cond}}$ measured (for independent calibration scores; §2b(c) adds $\varepsilon + 1/K + 2Ke^{-2m\varepsilon^2}$ at the image level). The factor 2 of the earlier draft is gone.

**This changes the form of the L1 bound, not just its constant: the coverage gap scales like $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$, not linearly in $d_{\text{op}}$, once the $d_K$/$W_1$ conversion is done honestly.** (T1.3 has been corrected to quote this form.) §3(ii)'s *synthetic-proxy* finding originally suggested the raw score-movement term itself might grow faster than linearly, which would have made the composition with this square root a genuinely open puzzle. The **real-score re-run (§3(ii), revised) found a plateauing ratio instead**, over the range tested — so the more urgent open composition question is not "how do a growing $L$ and a square root combine." **T3.5 ran the confirmation and the plateau did not survive it (§4.1–4.2 there):** the plateau at 0.18–0.20 is a property of the three-component score (it is the cancellation T3.4 §3 identified, and `s_residual` has since left the score, D42). The current two-component score's ratio keeps rising (0.42 → 0.75 over $R=3$–$16$ on the white-noise harness), stays bounded beyond $R=16$, and on a solver whose fill depends on the operator reaches ≈1.3–1.4 with 100%+ spread across mask seeds and a family dependence (variable-density masks give ≈1.2 at matched acceleration). So $L$ is not a single small constant: it is **per solver, per mask family, and seed-noisy**, and the 0.57 ceiling of D-046 is one operator pair on one bank, not a supremum.

## 3b. Step 2 for the label-conditional law (T3.5 §9)

Step 1 (§2b) needs $d_K^+ = d_K(Q^+_{\text{cal}}, Q^+_{\text{deploy}})$, the Kolmogorov distance between score laws **given `label = 1`**. The coupling in §3(i) cannot be applied to $Q^+$ directly: under a common $X$ the label itself changes with $A$, so the two conditional laws are laws of different subpopulations. The route below handles that by splitting off the label flip.

**Setting.** Couple the two operators on the exogenous randomness (image, measurement noise, solver seed) — the paired evaluation the harness already uses. Write $S_A, S_B$ for the scores, $\Lambda_A,\Lambda_B\in\{0,1\}$ for the labels, $\pi_A = P(\Lambda_A=1)>0$, $G_A(t) = P(S_A\le t,\Lambda_A=1)$ (a sub-CDF), so $F^+_A = G_A/\pi_A$.

**Step (a) — exact algebra.**
$$F^+_A-F^+_B = \frac{G_A-G_B}{\pi_A} - F^+_B\,\frac{\pi_A-\pi_B}{\pi_A}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_K^+ \;\le\; \frac{\sup_t|G_A(t)-G_B(t)| + |\pi_A-\pi_B|}{\pi_A}.$$

**Step (b) — coupling.** Pointwise, $\big|\mathbf 1\{S_A\le t\}\Lambda_A-\mathbf 1\{S_B\le t\}\Lambda_B\big|\le \mathbf 1\{\Lambda_A\ne\Lambda_B\}+\mathbf 1\{t \text{ lies between } S_A \text{ and } S_B\}$. Taking expectations, with $P_{\text{flip}}=P(\Lambda_A\ne\Lambda_B)$ and $P_{\times}(t)=P(t\text{ between }S_A,S_B)$:
$$\sup_t|G_A-G_B|\le P_{\text{flip}}+\sup_tP_\times(t),\qquad |\pi_A-\pi_B|\le P_{\text{flip}},\qquad\text{so}\qquad d_K^+\le\frac{2P_{\text{flip}}+\sup_tP_\times(t)}{\pi_A}.$$

**Step (c) — rates.** Assume (i) $\mathbb{E}|S_A-S_B|\le L_S\,d_{\text{op}}$ and $S_A$ has density $\le M_S$ (this is §3(ii), unchanged); (ii) the label statistic $\rho$ (the relative ratio $\text{err\_free}/\text{free\_content}$, T1.1/D21, evaluated on candidate regions) satisfies $\mathbb{E}|\rho_A-\rho_B|\le L_\rho\,d_{\text{op}}$, and $\rho_A$ has density $\le M_\rho$ near the threshold $\tau$ (a margin condition). By Markov, for any $\delta>0$: $P_\times(t)\le P(|S_A-S_B|>\delta)+P(S_A\in(t-\delta,t+\delta])\le L_Sd_{\text{op}}/\delta+2M_S\delta$; minimising over $\delta$ gives $2\sqrt{2M_SL_S\,d_{\text{op}}}$. The same argument on $\rho$ against $\tau$ gives $P_{\text{flip}}\le 2\sqrt{2M_\rho L_\rho\,d_{\text{op}}}$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\;\text{gap}\;\le\;d_K^+\;\le\;\frac{4\sqrt{2M_\rho L_\rho}+2\sqrt{2M_SL_S}}{\pi_A}\;\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}(A_{\text{cal}},A_{\text{deploy}})}\;}$$
the same $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$ rate as the unconditional chain, now for the quantity the calibrator controls.

**What this costs, stated plainly.**
1. **Four new constants**, none measured: $L_\rho$, $M_\rho$ (a margin condition on the label statistic at $\tau$), $M_S$, and the calibration positive rate $\pi_A$ in the denominator. The bound degrades as $1/\pi_A$ — rare hallucinations again.
2. **$\rho$ is not Lipschitz where `free_content` is small** (the denominator). The candidacy gate of D60 (regions below a design-split quantile of `free_content` are never positive) makes $\rho$ bounded on candidates, but the gate is itself defined from $A$ (`free_content` $=\|P_N x_{\text{true}}\|$), so regions can cross it when the operator changes. Those crossings add to $P_{\text{flip}}$ and are **not** controlled by (ii); either bound them separately or fix the candidate set from the calibration operator.
3. **The labels use ground truth**, so (ii) is an assumption about a quantity the deployment-time score never sees.
4. It uses a pairing (same $X$, same noise seed) that the experiment provides and a deployment never does; that is fine for a bound stated over the coupling, but it means $L_S, L_\rho$ are properties of the paired experiment.

**Numerical check** (`scripts/t3_5_conditional_link.py`, T3.5 §9): step (a) holds in every cell tested and is tight on the structured-prior harness (e.g. 0.569 vs measured 0.563 at R8); $P_{\text{flip}}$ is 0.23–0.28 there (saturating, not growing like $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$ over the range), and $d_K^+/\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$ is 0.69–0.88 across $R=3$–$16$. The constants of step (c) were not estimated; the rate is *consistent with*, not demonstrated by, four deployment operators.

## 5. Answering T1.3's re-raised question: does the proof need the operator-norm form anywhere?

T1.3 (`Documentation/T1.3_operator_distance_formulation.md`, "What this hands to Track 3") explicitly flagged that ratifying the aggregate/Frobenius form as the *plotted* distance does not automatically settle whether the *proved* distance needs to be the same object, and asked T3.3 to check this against its own proof technique rather than inherit the plotted quantity silently. Checked, by re-reading §2 and §3 above rather than re-deriving anything new:

- **Step 1 (§2) never references any operator-distance measure at all.** It bounds the coverage gap by $d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}})$ directly, via [3]'s Theorem 4 and Lemma 1 applied to the score sequence — no operator distance of any kind enters at this stage.
- **Step 2 (§3) introduces $d_{\text{op}}$ exactly once**, in assumption (ii), stated generically as "$s$ is $L$-Lipschitz in $A$ with respect to $d_{\text{op}}$" for whichever measure $d_{\text{op}}$ denotes. The derivation was never specific to the operator-norm (gap-metric) form — it was written to accept any valid distance, with the *choice* of which one deferred to T3.2/T1.3.

**Answer: no, this proof does not need the operator-norm form anywhere, at any step.** Since T1.3 has now ratified the aggregate/Frobenius form as $d_{\text{op}}$ project-wide, that is simply the object substituted into assumption (ii), and it is the only distance appearing anywhere in this derivation — plotted and proved coincide here, by construction rather than by coincidence, because the proof was never written against the saturating form to begin with. The caution T1.3 raised was well-founded in general (plotted and proved quantities need not match), but it does not apply to this specific derivation.
