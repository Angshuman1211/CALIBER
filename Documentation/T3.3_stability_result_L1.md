## 1. Setting, made precise

$X_1,\dots,X_{n+1} \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} P_X$ — true images from a shared population. This is not a convenience assumption here: it is literally how the experiment is built (D8 — fastMRI is *retrospectively* undersampled from the same acquired scans at different rates, so calibration and deployment draws genuinely come from the same underlying image population by construction).

For $i=1,\dots,n$ (calibration): $Z_i = (A_{\text{cal}}, X_i, Y_i, R_i)$, with $Y_i = A_{\text{cal}}X_i + \varepsilon_i$ and $R_i = s(A_{\text{cal}}, X_i, Y_i)$, where $s$ is T1.1's fixed hallucination score.
For $i=n+1$ (test/deployment): $Z_{n+1} = (A_{\text{deploy}}, X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1}, R_{n+1})$, defined analogously.

Two structural facts, both already established and load-bearing here:
- **$s$ is fixed, not refit on calibration data** (T1.1 design; confirmed as the split-conformal case in T3.1 §3.3/§5). This means $R_i$ depends only on $(A_{\text{cal or deploy}}, X_i, Y_i)$ — a coordinatewise function of $Z_i$ alone, not of the rest of the batch.
- **$Z_1,\dots,Z_{n+1}$ are mutually independent** — $X_i$ iid, $\varepsilon_i$ independent noise, and the operator assigned to index $i$ is deterministic (not random) given whether $i\leq n$ or $i=n+1$.

This is exactly the setting the risk register (blueprint §21) calls "the failure" and the first line of the experimental protocol (§16) — no group-conditional partitioning yet, just the basic naive-calibration-transfer case.

## 2. Step 1 — collapse the coverage gap to a single distributional distance

**This step is a direct, checked consequence of the results verified in T3.1. No new assumption is introduced here.**

Because $R$ is coordinatewise (§1), the score sequence $(R_1,\dots,R_{n+1})$ is itself an independent sequence, and swapping indices $i$ and $n+1$ in the *raw* data $Z \to Z^i$ produces exactly the same index-swap in the *score* sequence: $R(Z^i)$ is $(R_1,\dots,R_{n+1})$ with entries $i$ and $n+1$ exchanged. This means Lemma 1 from [3] (verified in T3.1 §3.3, "Lemma `dtv_swap`") — stated for any independent sequence — applies **directly to the score sequence itself**, not only to the raw data:

$$d_{TV}\big(R(Z), R(Z^i)\big) \;\leq\; 2\, d_{TV}(R_i, R_{n+1}) \;=\; 2\, d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}), \quad i=1,\dots,n,$$

where $Q_{\text{cal}}$ is the law of $s(A_{\text{cal}}, X, A_{\text{cal}}X+\varepsilon)$ for $X\sim P_X$, and $Q_{\text{deploy}}$ is defined analogously with $A_{\text{deploy}}$. The right-hand side is the **same quantity for every $i$**, because every calibration point shares the same operator $A_{\text{cal}}$ — a simplification specific to this project's two-regime structure that is not present in [3]'s fully general treatment (which allows a different distance for every index).

Substituting into Theorem 4 (T3.1 §3.3) and using $\sum_{i=1}^n \tilde{w}_i < 1$ (immediate from the normalized-weight definition, §3.3):

$$\boxed{\ \text{Coverage gap} \;\leq\; 2\, d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}})\ }$$

This already matches blueprint L1's billing ("a stability argument bounding the change in the score distribution... by the operator distance") in form — the coverage gap is bounded by a distance between two score *distributions*. What remains is replacing that abstract distributional distance with something computable from the operators alone (blueprint §12's actual ask), which is where new project-specific content is required.

## 3. Step 2 — relate $d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}})$ to a computable operator distance (sketch, two flagged assumptions)

**This step is new derivation, not something [3] supplies. Treat it as a sketch of the right shape of argument, not a proof.**

$Q_{\text{cal}}$ and $Q_{\text{deploy}}$ are laws of the same score function evaluated on independent draws from the same $P_X$, under different operators. A direct TV bound between two continuous-valued marginals via a "how far apart are typical values" argument runs into a standard problem: coupling $X$ across the two regimes bounds a *smoother* distance (Wasserstein-1) for free, but converting that into total variation needs an extra regularity condition. Concretely:

**(i) Coupling step (uncontroversial).** Since both $Q_{\text{cal}}$ and $Q_{\text{deploy}}$ are driven by the same $P_X$, use the identical-$X$ coupling: draw one $X\sim P_X$ and evaluate the score under both operators. This gives, by a standard Kantorovich argument,
$$W_1(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}) \;\leq\; \mathbb{E}_X\Big[\big| s(A_{\text{deploy}}, X, \cdot) - s(A_{\text{cal}}, X, \cdot)\big|\Big].$$

**(ii) Flagged assumption 1 — Lipschitz score. Revised after reading T1.1's actual score code and running a numerical check.**

*If* $s(A, X, \cdot)$ is $L$-Lipschitz in $A$ with respect to $d_{\text{op}}$ (the aggregate null-space projection distance, meanwhile ratified project-wide in T1.3), for $X$ in the typical region under $P_X$, then $\mathbb{E}_X[\,\cdot\,] \leq L \cdot d_{\text{op}}(A_{\text{cal}}, A_{\text{deploy}})$.

**A favorable structural fact, found in `src/caliber/scoring/score.py`, not assumed here:** T1.1's combination rule (D25) is deliberately an affine, non-negative weighted sum of the three components, specifically *because* "any non-negative weighted sum of Lipschitz components is Lipschitz with $L=\sum_i w_i L_i$" — the code cites this task by name. A ratio-based combination was rejected for exactly this reason (it diverges as the denominator vanishes, which is not Lipschitz). This means assumption (ii) is not one monolithic claim about the whole score — it reduces to three separate, more tractable claims, one per component ($s_{\text{free}}$, $s_{\text{disagree}}$, $s_{\text{residual}}$), each of which only needs to be Lipschitz on its own for the combination to inherit the property automatically, with $L$ a known function of the per-component constants and the (frozen, per D27) combination weights.

**Numerical check run: `scripts/t3_lipschitz_synthetic_check.py`.** This does *not* call T1.1's actual `compute_score_components` — it is a self-contained synthetic proxy (nested 1D masked-sampling operators, for which $d_{\text{op}}$ has the exact closed form used in `operators/distance.py`; a smoothness-informed reference score combining null-space energy, cross-sample disagreement, and measurement residual via interpolation-based prior fills rather than pure noise, so the score depends on actual signal content, not just randomness). The result, run with a fixed calibration operator ($R=4$) against a nested sweep of deployment operators ($R=5,\dots,64$):

| R | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $d_{\text{op}}$ | 0.000 | 0.225 | 0.286 | 0.354 | 0.410 | 0.433 | 0.446 | 0.468 | 0.484 |
| $\mathbb{E}_X[\lvert\Delta\text{score}\rvert]/d_{\text{op}}$ | — | 0.374 | 0.391 | 0.443 | 0.469 | 0.492 | 0.529 | 0.676 | 0.838 |

$d_{\text{op}}$ itself behaves exactly as T1.3 reported (graded, non-saturating, growth slowing as $R$ increases — consistent with their table, a useful cross-check that the closed-form formula is implemented consistently between the two tasks). **The ratio is not constant. It more than doubles across the sweep, and a finite-difference local-slope estimate grows roughly tenfold from the small-$R$ end to the large-$R$ end.** A single global Lipschitz constant $L$ taken as the worst-case observed ratio would still make §3's final bound technically valid over the range actually tested (any function's steepest observed local slope is a valid, if loose, global Lipschitz bound on a bounded range) — but treating $L$ as one fixed, small number that applies uniformly, which is what assumption (ii) originally implied, is not supported by this evidence.

**Re-run against T1.1's actual score code: `scripts/t3_lipschitz_real_score_check.py`. The picture changes, and not in the direction the proxy suggested.** This calls the real `compute_score_components`/`combine_components`, the real `MRIUndersamplingOperator` (128×128 Cartesian k-space masks, matching T1.3's own $R=2$ calibration choice for direct comparability), and the real `PhantomDataset` — the module Track 2 built with a stated second purpose of serving exactly this task. Scales were estimated once from the calibration operator only and frozen before touching any deployment operator, per D27's explicit requirement (re-estimating them per operator would have hidden the very effect being measured). Result, pooled over 16 regions × 120 phantom draws per $R$:

| R | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $d_{\text{op}}$ | 0.000 | 0.405 | 0.500 | 0.580 | 0.612 | 0.644 | 0.661 |
| $\mathbb{E}_X[\lvert\Delta\text{score}\rvert]/d_{\text{op}}$ | — | 0.137 | 0.167 | 0.198 | 0.183 | 0.186 | 0.184 |

($d_{\text{op}}$ here matches T1.3's own table to three decimal places at every shared $R$ — a strong independent cross-check that both tasks compute the same registered distance consistently.)

**The ratio rises from $R=3$ to $R=6$, then plateaus around 0.18–0.20 through $R=16$ — it does not keep growing.** This is a materially different, more favorable finding than the synthetic proxy gave, on the actual score the project will actually use. The most likely explanation is that the proxy's crude linear-interpolation fill was a poor stand-in for a real projector-based null/range decomposition — it is not that the earlier finding was fabricated or wrong on its own terms, it is that it was explicitly flagged as untested against the real thing (§3(ii)'s original caveat), and now that it has been tested, the real thing behaves better.

**What this means, revised from the earlier draft of this section:** the case for treating $L$ as a genuinely free, ill-posedness-dependent function (rather than a single practical constant) is weaker than the proxy suggested — over the range actually exercised by the experimental protocol ($R=2$ to $16$, one mask family, phantom images), the ratio looks bounded, not runaway. §4's connection to L2 still has a real target (explaining the *rise* between $R=3$ and $R=6$, and behavior beyond $R=16$ or under other mask families remains untested), but "an operator that hides more permits weaker guarantees" is no longer the most urgent framing — **"the ratio appears to plateau, but only three mask configurations beyond the rise have been tested, and only on synthetic phantoms, not real fastMRI data"** is the honest one. **Caveats that still apply:** one mask family (`random1d`), one image type (synthetic phantoms, not real anatomy), one region size, default equal-thirds weights, and $R$ capped at 16 because `center_fraction=0.04` saturates the mask beyond that — none of this validates the bound for CT, for real data, or for more extreme acceleration.

**(iii) Density-regularity condition — corrected. The earlier claim in this document ($d_{TV}\le M\cdot W_1$, linear) was imprecise and is replaced below by a derived inequality, worked out from scratch here rather than cited from anywhere.**

The score is real-valued (a scalar per region, per T1.1), so both $Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{deploy}}$ are 1-D distributions, and two exact identities apply: writing $G(t) = F_{Q_{\text{cal}}}(t) - F_{Q_{\text{deploy}}}(t)$ for the difference of their CDFs,
$$d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) = \sup_t |G(t)|, \qquad W_1(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) = \int |G(t)|\,dt.$$
These are two *different norms of the same function* $G$ — sup-norm and $L^1$-norm — so bounding one by the other in general is impossible without control on how fast $G$ can change, i.e. on $G' = q_{\text{cal}} - q_{\text{deploy}}$ (the density difference), which is where a regularity condition genuinely has to enter.

**Elementary derivation.** Suppose $\sup_t|G'(t)| \leq D$ (a bound on the density *difference*, not on either density alone) and $G(t)\to 0$ as $t\to\pm\infty$ (true for any two CDFs). Let $S=\sup_t|G(t)|$, attained at $t_0$. Since $|G'|\le D$, $|G(t)-G(t_0)|\le D|t-t_0|$, so $|G(t)|\ge S-D|t-t_0|$ for $t$ within $S/D$ of $t_0$ — a "tent" of base $2S/D$ and height $S$ sits entirely under $|G|$ there, contributing at least $S^2/D$ (the tent's area) to $\int|G|$. Hence $W_1 \ge S^2/D$, i.e.
$$\boxed{\ d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}}) \;\leq\; \sqrt{D \cdot W_1(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}})}\ }$$
where $D=\sup_t|q_{\text{cal}}(t)-q_{\text{deploy}}(t)|$ (bounded by, e.g., $2M$ if both densities are pointwise bounded by $M$). This is a genuine square-root relationship, not the linear one originally guessed — a function with a bounded rate of change cannot have a tall spike without also having real area under it, and that trade-off is what the derivation above makes precise.

> **Correction (T3.5, `T3.5_numerical_verification.md` §2).** The identity $d_{TV} = \sup_t |G(t)|$ above is the **Kolmogorov** distance $d_K$, not total variation: $d_K \le d_{TV}$, with equality only in special families. The tent argument is correct and proves $d_K \le \sqrt{D\cdot W_1}$. It does **not** prove $d_{TV} \le \sqrt{D \cdot W_1}$, and that inequality is false in general (T3.5 builds a family with $d_{TV}$ fixed while $d_K$ and $W_1$ shrink). The L1 chain is repaired without needing it: for split conformal with independent calibration scores and a *threshold* event, $	ext{coverage}_{	ext{dep}} \ge 1-lpha - d_K(Q_{	ext{cal}}, Q_{	ext{deploy}})$ directly (T3.5 §2), so the boxed Step 1 bound can be stated with $d_K$ in place of $2\,d_{TV}$ (tighter, and exactly the quantity the tent argument controls). Read the combined bound below with $d_K$ for $d_{TV}$ and without the factor 2: gap $\le \sqrt{D W_1} \le \sqrt{D L}\,\sqrt{d_{	ext{op}}}$.

**Combining (i), (ii) and the corrected (iii):**

$$\text{Coverage gap} \;\leq\; 2\sqrt{D\cdot W_1(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}})} \;\leq\; 2\sqrt{DL}\cdot\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}(A_{\text{cal}}, A_{\text{deploy}})}.$$

**This changes the form of the L1 bound, not just its constant: the coverage gap scales like $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$, not linearly in $d_{\text{op}}$, once the TV/W1 conversion is done honestly.** §3(ii)'s *synthetic-proxy* finding originally suggested the raw score-movement term itself might grow faster than linearly, which would have made the composition with this square root a genuinely open puzzle. The **real-score re-run (§3(ii), revised) found a plateauing ratio instead**, over the range tested — so the more urgent open composition question is not "how do a growing $L$ and a square root combine," it is simply confirming the plateau holds beyond $R=16$ and beyond one mask family, which is a numerical task (T3.5), not a further analytical puzzle.

## 4. Why this connects to L2 — a narrower, better-evidenced claim than it first looked

$L$ (the per-component Lipschitz constants, now that D25's affine combination reduces the score's constant to a weighted sum of them) and $D$ (the density-difference bound from the corrected TV/W1 conversion) are exactly the kind of quantities blueprint §12.2's **L2** stage wants to relate to "null-space dimension and spectral decay." The story here changed across the two numerical passes in §3(ii), and the honest version is the later one: the synthetic proxy suggested $L$ grows without bound as ill-posedness increases; the real-score re-run instead found the ratio **rising then plateauing**, over the range actually tested. That leaves a real but *narrower* job for L2 — explain the rise between $R=3$ and $R=6$ and where the plateau comes from, rather than explain unbounded growth, which the better evidence does not actually show. L2 should treat "does the plateau hold outside the tested range (higher $R$, other mask families, CT)" as an open empirical question to fold into its own scope, not assume the answer either way.

## 4b. Gaps between this bound and what the experiments measure (flagged after reading the Track 1/2 harness)

Three mismatches between the quantity bounded above and the quantity the banks measure. None invalidates Step 1; each must be closed before T3.6 compares bound to measurement.

1. **Conditioning on the label.** Step 1 bounds $d_{TV}(Q_{	ext{cal}}, Q_{	ext{deploy}})$ for the *unconditional* score law. The harness controls the miss rate **among hallucinated regions** (T1.2, D9), so the exchangeability that matters is for scores *given label = 1*. The label depends on the operator: the positive rate moves from 0.40 to 0.77 across R=2→8 (P-21). The conditional laws can differ far more than the unconditional ones, and the Lipschitz argument in §3 (a statement about $s(A, X, \cdot)$ for typical $X$) does not control a selection on a label that is itself a function of the reconstruction and the ground truth. Needed: either bound $d_{TV}$ of the label-conditional laws directly, or state the bound for the unconditional (all-regions) risk and report the conditional gap as a separate empirical quantity.
2. **Independence unit.** §1 treats $Z_1,\dots,Z_{n+1}$ as independent, one per image. The calibrated unit is a *region*, 256 per image, many per patient (about two volumes per patient in fastMRI, D-027). Regions inside an image are dependent, so Lemma 1's independence hypothesis fails as stated. Needed: restate with the cluster (patient) as the unit, or with a within-cluster dependence term.
3. **Solver dependence.** $s$ is a function of the reconstruction $\hat x$, which depends on $A$ through the black-box solver (T1.1, "what is handed back to T3.3"). §3(ii) is Lipschitz *conditional on $\hat x$*; the dependence of $\hat x$ on $A$ is not bounded here. The harness used for the plateau check replaces the solver with `pinv` plus injected noise, which sidesteps it.

**Direction.** Theorem 4 is a one-sided *lower* bound on coverage, and $d_{TV}$ is symmetric, but the measured gap is not: for the same operator pair the naive-transfer gap is about −0.10 in one direction and +0.90 in the other (D47). So the bound can only be tested per direction, and only the under-coverage direction (positive gap) is what it speaks to. The over-coverage direction (flag area → 1) is an efficiency failure the bound does not describe. T3.6 should report $d_{TV}$ (computable from the bank's label-conditional score samples) next to the measured gap, per direction, as the check of Step 1 before testing Step 2.

## 5. Answering T1.3's re-raised question: does the proof need the operator-norm form anywhere?

T1.3 (`Documentation/T1.3_operator_distance_formulation.md`, "What this hands to Track 3") explicitly flagged that ratifying the aggregate/Frobenius form as the *plotted* distance does not automatically settle whether the *proved* distance needs to be the same object, and asked T3.3 to check this against its own proof technique rather than inherit the plotted quantity silently. Checked, by re-reading §2 and §3 above rather than re-deriving anything new:

- **Step 1 (§2) never references any operator-distance measure at all.** It bounds the coverage gap by $d_{TV}(Q_{\text{cal}},Q_{\text{deploy}})$ directly, via [3]'s Theorem 4 and Lemma 1 applied to the score sequence — no operator distance of any kind enters at this stage.
- **Step 2 (§3) introduces $d_{\text{op}}$ exactly once**, in assumption (ii), stated generically as "$s$ is $L$-Lipschitz in $A$ with respect to $d_{\text{op}}$" for whichever measure $d_{\text{op}}$ denotes. The derivation was never specific to the operator-norm (gap-metric) form — it was written to accept any valid distance, with the *choice* of which one deferred to T3.2/T1.3.

**Answer: no, this proof does not need the operator-norm form anywhere, at any step.** Since T1.3 has now ratified the aggregate/Frobenius form as $d_{\text{op}}$ project-wide, that is simply the object substituted into assumption (ii), and it is the only distance appearing anywhere in this derivation — plotted and proved coincide here, by construction rather than by coincidence, because the proof was never written against the saturating form to begin with. The caution T1.3 raised was well-founded in general (plotted and proved quantities need not match), but it does not apply to this specific derivation.
