## 1. T2-A: the LIDC-prior CT bank

`configs/experiments/w12_ct_lidc_gpu.yaml`. The substrate and the grid are the CT bank of record's (`w07_ct_onboarding`): all 100 extracted TotalSegmentator subjects × six sparse-view densities (180 to 30 views) and three limited-angle arcs (150° to 90°). That is 900 pairs and 360,000 regions (20 × 20 of 16 px). The prior is Song et al.'s LIDC NCSN++ at its fixed 320², with DDNM at 24 steps and 4 draws, as in the smoke run.

### 1a. Splits

`split: auto` with the bank of record's fractions. The split registry hashes the subject id, so the sides are the same people as in `w07_ct_onboarding`: 8 design, 41 calibration and 51 test subjects. One subject is one slice here, so there is no slice-level split to get wrong, and `tests/test_totalsegmentator.py` pins that a subject cannot straddle.

### 1b. The HU mapping (D-071)

The pre-registration says "HU mapping fixed to the prior's training normalisation before the run". The training code states the rule. Song et al.'s builder (`ct2d_320/ct2d_320.py`, read from their repository) does this to each volume before slicing it:

```python
img = img - np.min(img)
factor = 1. / np.max(img)
img = img * factor
```

So the bank uses `intensity: volume_minmax`, a new option on the TotalSegmentator loader: each subject's volume is scaled from its own minimum to its own maximum, with no clip and no percentile, because neither is in the training code. D-013's fixed window `[-1000, 1000] HU` stays the default, so every earlier CT bank keeps its config hash and its meaning.

What that does to these 100 subjects, measured on the volumes (ground truth only):

| | minimum over subjects | median | maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| volume minimum (HU) | −1799 | −1222 | −1025 |
| volume maximum (HU) | 1101 | 3148 | 4094 |
| soft tissue (40 HU) after min-max | 0.25 | 0.31 | 0.50 |

Under the fixed window soft tissue is 0.52 in every item. Under min-max it moves with each volume's extremes: the scanner's padding value at the bottom (below −1024 HU in all 100) and bone or metal at the top (above 3000 HU in 56). The training volumes had the same property, so this is the prior's own input range. It is also the cost D-013 chose the window to avoid, and it is listed under limitations.

The crop pads with zeros, and zero is now the volume's minimum, not air. Padding is therefore filled with the volume's own air value (−1000 HU after scaling), so a body narrower than the grid is not framed by an edge that is not in the scan. Two tests pin the rule: it scales by the volume and not the slice, and nothing in an item is darker than air.

**A check that the mapping works, which did not choose it.** On the 8 design subjects, noise was added to the ground-truth slice and the prior's one-step denoised estimate compared with the truth (gain = MSE before / MSE after):

| σ | fixed window: median (worst) | volume min-max: median (worst) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 19.8 (4.1) | **25.4** (9.1) |
| 0.1 | 33.3 (9.3) | **43.5** (21.4) |
| 0.3 | 79.0 (35.3) | **121.1** (67.6) |
| 1.0 | 211.4 (143.0) | **318.7** (233.5) |

The prior denoises better in its training range at every noise level and in the worst subject. This reads no reconstruction, score or label. Had it gone the other way the rule would still have been the training rule, and the result would be a limitation to report.

### 1c. The control that decides whether the bank is read

The plan: "a matched-operator control must return ≈ 0.90, or the bank is wrong and nothing else is read". When the bank finishes this is the first command run, before cos θ and before any transfer number:

```bash
python scripts/coverage_by_patient.py runs/w12_ct_lidc_gpu-<hash> --cluster sample_id \
    --low ct.sparse_view.v180.a180.dfull --high ct.sparse_view.v30.a180.dfull
```

Its group-conditional arm calibrates and tests on the same operator, on disjoint subjects. The pass rule was written with 20 of the 900 pairs built and no coverage computed:

- the arm's 95% subject-clustered interval contains 0.90 at **every** one of the 9 operators, and
- its mean over the operators is within 0.03 of 0.90.

Anything else is a fail, and the bank is not read. The rule is no tighter than 51 test subjects allow: on the face-prior CT bank the same arm's intervals are about ±0.06 wide (T2.7), and that bank passes it (9 of 9, mean 0.893). A script waits for the bank and applies the rule before anything else runs.

Then, only on a pass, T2-C: cos θ on the full bank (`label_comparison.py`), read **before** any score result, because a chest prior on whole-body slices may lower it from the smoke bank's +0.976 and bring D67's identity back.

**`label_comparison.py` refuses this bank, and the refusal is a finding** (P-45). Its identity check wants every region to satisfy `err_free² = s_free² + free_content² − 2·s_free·free_content·cos θ` with |cos θ| ≤ 1. At 1,744 of 360,000 regions (0.48%) the stored columns imply |cos θ| > 1, by a median of 0.006 and at most 0.31. They are regions where the error is small beside the content (median `err_free` 0.011 against content 0.09), so cos θ sits at the boundary and a small inconsistency crosses it. They concentrate at the two most ill-posed operators (limited-angle 90°: 709; sparse-view 30: 329). The face-prior bank has 11 such regions, and the smoke bank 5. The number above was therefore computed directly, with cos θ clipped; dropping the violating regions does not change the median (+0.882). The likely cause is the CT null-space projection, which is a CG solve and not exactly linear at its tolerance. That is not confirmed.

**The ruling.** D86 reverses D85's reverting clause: T2-A1 was run correctly and fired correctly, and the condition was the defective part. It added one pooled calibration term to nine cells whose terms differ, and it fails a sound bank whenever any one of nine intervals misses. The ruling rests on §1e's 13-split table as it stands: nominal at all nine operators, with the registry split a common displacement of every cell. The registry split stays the reporting split, with the both-sides interval. T1-A's unsafe label moves to the across-split mean (T2-A3). Two checks could still overturn it, and both were run before anything was read.

D86 said that if these concentrate at the three failing cells the bank goes back. **They do concentrate there.** The three failing cells average 400 violations against 91 at the six that passed, 4.4 times as many, and an exact one-sided rank-sum over the nine cells gives W = 8 against an expected 15, p = 0.048. The share by mass (69%) flatters this: limited-angle 90° alone holds 41% of all violations, and with it set aside the failing cells hold 485 against 545.

**What settles it (D87).** The reconstructions and the scores are computed once. Re-cutting the subjects into calibration and test recomputes nothing, so whatever the solver's tolerance does to the scores is the same in all 13 splits. §1e's nine 13-split means are all at nominal (0.892 to 0.905), and limited-angle 90°, the worst T2-A1 cell and the holder of 41% of the violations, has the highest of the nine. A defect that is present in every split cannot explain a shortfall that appears on one split and is gone in the mean of thirteen. The concentration is a fact about which operators are numerically hardest. The transfer table says the same thing from the other side: sparse-view 120 has no violations at all and is the operator most out of order.

**A bound, not evidence.** With every violating region dropped, matched coverage moves by at most 0.0014. That could never have convicted the bank, so it does not acquit it: on the test split the violating positives number 9, 58 and 1 at the three cells (of 6,655, 12,236 and 6,770; about 10,760 positives per operator over all splits), so dropping them can move coverage by at most +0.0012, +0.0040 and +0.0001, which is 3%, 8% and 0% of shortfalls of 0.044 to 0.053. And |cos θ| > 1 is a threshold on a continuous error: the 1,744 are where the inconsistency crossed 1, not every region it reached. Track 2 led with this bound in its first account of the check. D87 is right that it should not have.

**Check 2: Track 1's own function.** `clustered_coverage` (10,000 replicates) gives [0.8045, 0.8967], [0.8012, 0.8885] and [0.7851, 0.8993] at the three cells. T2-A1's numbers were [0.805, 0.897], [0.801, 0.888] and [0.785, 0.899]. The two implementations agree.

**Asked for in D86, and what came back.**

- *The 13 splits at a fixed ε.* The spread does not fall: mean sd 0.051, as with ε re-valued (0.051). At the three cells it is 0.027, 0.023 and 0.038 against bootstrap standard errors of 0.024, 0.022 and 0.029. Re-valuing ε is not where the spread comes from.
- *T2-A2, dynamic range.* The per-volume HU range runs from 2,126 to 5,546 over the 100 subjects, but the calibration half's mean range barely moves between splits (3,913 to 4,156), and it does not order τ: Spearman −0.05 to +0.36 over the nine operators, median +0.08. Within a subject there is something: a wider range goes with lower scores among its positives (−0.19 to −0.44 at eight operators). So the per-volume scale does reach the score, and it is not what moves τ between these splits. What does is still not identified.

### 1h. D87: the null for the threshold, the solver's residual, and the safety rule (21 Nov)

**T2-A4: the threshold's spread is an order statistic's.** The null: a calibration half is a random set of subjects and τ is the `floor(α(n+1))`-th smallest positive score among them, and nothing else. The observed sd of τ over the 13 splits (label fixed, so only membership moves it) against 2,000 sets of 13 random halves of the same sizes from the same 100 subjects:

| | LIDC bank | face-prior bank |
|---|---|---|
| observed sd inside the null's central 95% | 8 of 9 operators | 9 of 9 |
| observed / null median, median over operators | 1.25 | 0.93 |
| null sd of τ as a share of τ (median) | 6.1% | 2.2% |

The null reproduces it. The one exception is limited-angle 150° on the LIDC bank (0.099 against an upper bound of 0.097). The null is also wider on the LIDC bank by about the same factor as the observed spread, so the contrast between the two banks (0.051 against 0.034 in coverage) is the shape of the positives' lower tail and not something about how these 13 splits fell. There is nothing further to explain, and D-071 is not the cause of the spread. One thing short of a clean pass: on the LIDC bank the observed sd is above the null's median at all nine operators.

**T2-A5: the CT range/null split, measured.** `P_R x = pinv_cg(A x)` is conjugate gradients on the normal equations, stopped at 20 iterations or a relative residual of 10⁻⁶. On the 8 design subjects' slices:

| operator | CG residual at the stop | idempotence defect | additivity defect (2% perturbation) | violating regions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sparse-view 180 | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.41 | 0.088 | 176 |
| sparse-view 120 | 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.23 | 0.055 | 0 |
| sparse-view 90 | 0.9 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.19 | 0.055 | 76 |
| sparse-view 60 | 0.8 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.16 | 0.048 | 0 |
| sparse-view 45 | 0.7 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.12 | 0.030 | 63 |
| sparse-view 30 | 0.8 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.09 | 0.017 | 329 |
| limited-angle 150° | 2.1 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.35 | 0.036 | 230 |
| limited-angle 120° | 2.6 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.26 | 0.038 | 156 |
| limited-angle 90° | 5.6 × 10⁻⁴ | 0.30 | 0.122 | 714 |

- **The solver never converges to its tolerance.** It uses all 20 iterations at every operator and stops at a residual 70 to 560 times the 10⁻⁶ it asks for. Limited-angle residuals are two to seven times the sparse-view ones, as D87 expected.
- **The split is not a projector.** Applying the null projection twice changes the result by 9% to 41%, and `P_N(a) − P_N(b)` differs from `P_N(a − b)` by 2% to 12%. A stopped CG is not a linear map, because its Krylov space depends on its right-hand side. D38's identity needs that difference to be zero.
- **More iterations do not repair it.** At ten times the budget the residual falls to 1–3 × 10⁻⁵ and idempotence improves (0.03 to 0.17), but additivity gets much worse (0.24 to 2.4): the solver then stops by its divergence guard at a different iteration for each input. The 20-iteration setting is the more linear of the two.
- **The violation count does not track the defect**, as D87 said it could not. Sparse-view 30 has the smallest additivity defect and the second most violations; sparse-view 180 has the largest idempotence defect and few.

What it means for the bank: `s_free`, `free_content` and `err_free` are each computed with the same stopped solver, so they are consistent as a regularised decomposition, and cos θ is recoverable to a few percent, not exactly. `label_comparison.py`'s guard asks for 10⁻³ of `err_free²`, a hundred times tighter than the solver's own additivity. The guard is unchanged here. What its tolerance should be is Track 1's to set, from this table.

## 3. T2-D: the literature's protocol, on our banks

`scripts/protocol_replication.py`. Papers that propose an uncertainty map for a generative reconstruction validate it one way: correlate the posterior standard deviation with the absolute error, and draw an ROC for how well it picks out high-error regions. D-070 (point 5) asks what that protocol can tell apart. The test is a detector with **no posterior information**: if it scores as well, a pass on the protocol is not evidence about uncertainty.

**Fixed before any number was read**, as the pre-registration requires for the circularity analysis:

- **Orientation.** A larger detector value predicts a larger error. An AUROC below 0.5 is reported as it falls.
- **Label.** A region is high-error when its `err_total` is at or above the 0.90 quantile of its own operator. 0.75 is reported beside it.
- **Rows.** Test patients.
- **The control is run in the same pass**, on the same rows: `s_free` = ‖P_N x̂‖ per region, the null-space content of the mean reconstruction (Track 1's scale-only control), and `s_range` = ‖P_R x̂‖, the measured content. Neither reads the draws.
- **Reading.** A control within 0.05 AUROC of the posterior spread "matches". Intervals resample whole patients and re-value the label inside every resample.

**The posterior spread.** On the fastMRI bank it is the full-image pixelwise standard deviation across draws (`dispersion_calib_test.parquet`, T2.9), which is the literature's quantity. The other two banks have no regenerated draws, so the bank's `s_disagree` stands in: the same spread, measured in the null space. On DDNM the two agree closely, because DDNM pins the range component (within 0.012 AUROC on fastMRI, T2.6).

### 3a. Results

Median over the 9 operators; AUROC is for the q90 label.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| fastMRI knee, 209,664 regions, 44 patients | face | 0.767 / 0.932 (full-image) | **0.895 / 0.978** | 0.124 / 0.427 |
| TotalSegmentator CT, 117,504 regions, 51 subjects | face | 0.957 / 0.994 (`s_disagree`) | **0.983 / 0.997** | −0.298 / 0.371 |
| M4Raw brain, 243,000 regions, 15 patients | BraTS | **0.930 / 0.937** (`s_disagree`) | 0.838 / 0.895 | 0.899 / 0.847 |
| TotalSegmentator CT, 183,600 regions, 51 subjects (added 20 Nov) | LIDC | **0.779 / 0.920** (`s_disagree`) | 0.440 / 0.801 | 0.474 / 0.759 |

|---|---|---|---|---|

### 3b. What it says

**On a face prior the protocol is blind.** The scale-only control beats the posterior spread at every operator of both banks, with the interval clear of zero. On fastMRI that is against the literature's own full-image spread. A method "validated" by this protocol on these banks would be outscored by ‖P_N x̂‖, which contains no uncertainty.

**On the medical prior the spread is ahead, and the protocol is still mostly scale.** On M4Raw with the BraTS prior the spread is better than the control at 7 of 9 operators with the interval clear of zero. But the control reaches a median AUROC of 0.895 with no posterior information, and on this bank even brightness (`s_range`) reaches 0.847. The posterior's own contribution on this protocol is the gap between 0.937 and 0.895, not the 0.937. The gap is largest at the two R2 1-D masks (−0.165 at `random1d` R2, −0.105 at `equispaced1d` R2), where there is least null space for a scale to live in.

This is D79's finding seen from the protocol's side. cos θ rose from +0.07 to +0.60 on M4Raw (T2.10 §6a): the medical prior's invented content is no longer orthogonal to the truth's, so the error is no longer a function of scale alone, and the spread starts to carry something. It carries little.

**What this is not.** It is not a detection result for the CALIBER score, and no bank needs to carry detection claims for it: the statement is that the protocol cannot tell the detectors apart. Two of the three banks are face-prior development banks. The medical-prior row is 15 test patients. The LIDC bank is the fourth row when it lands.

## 5. The release question (O7)

The plan's risk register gives Track 2 one item: "Checkpoint licence (O7 release). Fine for a cited preprint; resolve before any code/weights release." T2.10 recorded that the weights have no stated licence. This is what was checked, on 18 Nov, and what follows from it.

| Item | Terms, as stated at the source | In the release? |
|---|---|---|
| Song et al.'s weights (BraTS, LIDC) and our converted `.pt` files | **None.** `yang-song/score_inverse_problems` has no LICENSE file and GitHub reports no licence for it. The weights sit on the authors' Google Drive, linked from the README, with no terms | **No.** With no licence nothing grants redistribution. The release ships `convert_song_checkpoint.py` and the download command, as the T2.10 appendix already does |
| `src/caliber/solvers/ncsnpp.py` | Adapted from `yang-song/score_sde_pytorch`, which is Apache-2.0. The file keeps the original header | Yes. Apache-2.0 also asks for a copy of the licence in the distribution, which this repository does not have yet |
| The rest of the code | **The repository has no LICENSE file**, and `pyproject.toml` names none | Needs a decision before any release (below) |
| `ddpm-celebahq-256` | Apache-2.0 on Hugging Face (T2.1) | Not shipped; fetched by name |
| fastMRI | NYU agreement: no redistribution (T2.1 App. A) | No images, no reconstructions |
| M4Raw | CC BY 4.0 (Zenodo 8056074) | Not shipped; cited |
| TotalSegmentator v2.0.1 | CC BY 4.0 (Zenodo 10047292) | Not shipped; cited |
| LIDC-IDRI, the CT prior's training data | CC BY 3.0, with TCIA's data citation and an acknowledgement of NCI and FNIH | We hold none of it. The paper cites it because the prior was trained on it |
| BraTS 2021, the MRI prior's training data | Its own registration terms | We hold none of it (T2.10 §1). Cited |

**So the checkpoint question has a clean answer: the release contains no weights.** Nothing in the code path needs them to be in the repository. `fetch_data.py` already treats the checkpoints as a manual download, and the converter and its tests reproduce the port from the authors' own files. That is also the ordinary arrangement for a cited preprint, so the preprint is not blocked.

**Nothing in the repository is copied from the unlicensed repository.** `ncsnpp.py` comes from the Apache-2.0 PyTorch repository. The converter is ours; what it takes from `score_inverse_problems` is the names of the Flax layers and the configuration values, which it has to match. The Flax model used to verify the port ran in a throwaway environment and was never committed (T2.10 §2).

**Two things are not Track 2's to settle, and one of them was not on the register** (P-43):

1. **The repository itself has no licence.** Until it has one, the O7 "open-source release" is source that nobody is licensed to use. Apache-2.0 would match the one file that already carries it. The choice belongs to all three tracks.
2. **Whether the banks are released.** A bank is numbers per region plus a few cached reconstructions. The M4Raw and TotalSegmentator banks derive from CC BY 4.0 data, so releasing their `scores.parquet` and manifests is permitted with attribution, and it would let T1-A..D and T3-A be reproduced without a GPU. The fastMRI banks' cached reconstructions are images derived from fastMRI and stay local. Whether numbers alone count as redistribution under the NYU agreement is a reading Track 2 will not make alone.

## Honest limitations

- **The bank is read on a ruling that reversed its own earlier clause** (D86), after the control failed in both written forms. The record keeps all three steps (§1e, §1f, §1g). What the reading rests on is the 13-split table, which was computed after the first failure.
- **The safety-rule numbers come from a run past the script's own gate.** `t1a_safety_rule.py` still stops at limited-angle 90° and needed `--ignore-control`. D86 rules the bank read, and the script's gate has not caught up with the ruling; until it does its output calls itself diagnostic.
- **T1-B's zero is a statement about the frozen direction as much as about the distance.** A non-decreasing fit cannot follow a falling response, so on this bank it reports the training mean. §1i gives the rank correlations beside the verdict for that reason. The mirrored fit quoted there was looked at after the result and is a description.
- **cos θ on CT is good to a few percent, not exactly.** The split it rests on is a stopped CG that is not linear (T2-A5). The same is true of every CT bank the project has built.
- **T2-A4's pass is one-sided in a small way:** the observed spread is above the null's median at all nine LIDC operators.
- **The direction of the transfer failure is reported, not explained.** The distances are now in, and they order the gaps with the opposite sign to the face-prior bank's. Why a medical prior reverses it is not answered here.
- **cos θ on this bank was computed around a guard, not through it.** `label_comparison.py` refuses the bank because 0.48% of regions fail its identity check (P-45). The median does not depend on those regions, but the cause is not confirmed.
- **The diagnosis did not find why this bank's split-to-split spread is wider** than the face-prior bank's (0.051 against 0.034). Two candidate causes were measured and ruled out. Thirteen splits is also a small sample of splits.
- **The naive arm on the registry split was seen** while reading the control, because one script prints both. It is not reported and nothing was decided by it.
- **Min-max makes the intensity scale a property of each volume.** Soft tissue sits anywhere from 0.25 to 0.50. The label threshold ε and every `err_free` are in per-volume units. Coverage is a rank statement within the bank and does not need a common unit, but a region's error is not comparable across subjects as HU.
- **How LIDC's raw volumes were prepared before Song et al.'s builder is not in their repository.** The builder reads pre-binned float volumes (`image_bin_320`) and min-max scales them. Whether those were clipped first is unknown, so "the training normalisation" is the rule the code states, applied to volumes whose extremes may differ from the training set's.
- **Cross-anatomy.** The prior saw chest CT. The slices are organ-bearing slices from head to pelvis at a 480 mm field. The bank carries coverage claims only, and T2-C may find cos θ lower than the smoke bank's.
- **The smoke bank's cos θ (+0.976) was measured under the fixed window**, on 2 subjects. It is not the same measurement as this bank's will be.
- **The bank's first 20 pairs were written by a process that predates the claims code.** The rows are the same either way (the stamp is in the manifest, not the rows), and the resumed process writes the manifest. `restamp_bank.py` is run on the finished bank as a check.
- **On the LIDC CT bank the protocol separates them.** The scale-only control is below the posterior spread at 9 of 9 operators with the interval clear, by 0.068 to 0.160 in AUROC, and matches at none. Across the four banks the control's standing falls as cos θ rises: above the spread at cos θ ≈ 0.07 (both face-prior banks), level with it at 0.60 (M4Raw), well below at 0.88 (this bank). That is D67's mechanism read off the protocol, on four points. The control still reaches 0.80 with no posterior information.
- **The CT protocol rows use `s_disagree`, not the full-image spread.** The M4Raw row now has both (the draws were regenerated on 19 Nov, 1,080 pairs): against the literature's own spread the scale-only control matches at 7 of 9 operators, is below with the interval clear at 6 and above at 2. The reading in §3b does not change.
- **The protocol's "matches" margin (0.05) is a judgement**, fixed in advance and reported with the raw differences so it can be re-read at another margin.
- **The licence table in §5 is what the sources state on 18 Nov 2026**, read from the repository metadata and the dataset pages. It is not legal advice, and BraTS's terms were not re-read: we hold no BraTS data.
- **The laptop has 1–2 GB of commit free while the bank runs** (P-41). Analyses run on the CPU interpreter, and the full test suite was run with the bank paused at a checkpoint.
