**Branch:** `T1.1`

## The measure

$$d_{\text{op}}(A_{\text{cal}}, A_{\text{dep}}) \;=\; \frac{\bigl\lVert P_{N}(A_{\text{cal}}) - P_{N}(A_{\text{dep}}) \bigr\rVert_F}{\sqrt{\dim(\text{ambient})}}$$

In the code: `null_space_projection` with `ambient` normalisation
(`caliber.operators.distance`). Locked for Week 3 and used identically by all
three tracks through the operator registry.

**Why this is the quantity that had to be chosen.** It is the x-axis of the
paper's central figure, it determines the weighting scheme (D4), and Track 3
cannot state a bound "in quantities computable from the operators alone" until
the quantity exists. T1.3 gates T3.3, so it could not be deferred.

---

## Why this measure

Three requirements, all binding, all met:

1. **Computable without ground truth** — a function of the two operators only.
2. **Modality-general** — $P_N$ exists for any linear operator, so the same
   function accepts a k-space mask and a projection-angle set. No dependence on
   MRI's diagonal structure.
3. **Interpretable** — under `ambient` normalisation on Cartesian MRI it reduces
   to $\sqrt{\text{fraction of k-space lines whose sampled status changed}}$.
   That satisfies Q1's requirement that the distance be monotone in something a
   radiographer would recognise as a bigger change to the scan.

It is also the aggregate subspace distance Track 3's T3.2 §5 independently
recommended, so this ruling **ratifies a convergence** between the analytical
and empirical tracks rather than arbitrating between them.

### Verified, not assumed

128², `random1d`, calibrating at R=2, measured across the sweep:

| R | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $d_{\text{op}}$ | 0.000 | 0.405 | 0.500 | 0.580 | 0.612 | 0.644 | 0.650 |

Graded and monotone. **T3.2 §3's saturation failure does not occur**, because
the implemented measure is the *Frobenius* form; the operator-norm form
$\lVert P_1 - P_2 \rVert_{\text{op}} = \sin\theta_{\max}$ pins at exactly 1
whenever the null-space dimensions differ, which is the typical case across an
acceleration sweep. That form is **not implemented and must not be added** under
a name that invites confusion with the primary.

## Rejected, with reasons

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Operator-norm form of family (A) | Saturates at 1 under dimension mismatch (T3.2 §3). Would flatten the entire acceleration sweep to a constant |
| `sampling_support` | Verified **numerically identical** to the primary in MRI — both 0.612372 at R=2→8 — because MRI's operator is diagonal in the Fourier basis, so the two coincide exactly (T3.2 §2). Only a heuristic in CT. Retained as a free MRI-only diagnostic |
| `chordal_subspace` | The same number divided by $\sqrt2$. An alias, not an alternative; no separate experiment |
| `spectral_divergence` | Measures severity of ill-posedness, not direction of change — an L2 modifier, not a distance (T3.2 §1). D-011 also shows it barely separates CT operators (decay exponent 1.688 → 1.676 between 180 and 20 views) |

---

## Why ill-posedness normalisation is deliberately excluded

§11.2's collapse claim and §12.2's L2 stage both involve ill-posedness, and
folding it into $d_{\text{op}}$ would double-count. L1 bounds the gap by
$2ML\cdot d_{\text{op}}$, and **L2's entire job is to express $L$ and $M$ in
terms of null-space dimension and spectral decay**. If $d$ already absorbed
that, L2's scaling would be unidentifiable.

So $d_{\text{op}}$ stays a pure geometric property of the operator pair, and
ill-posedness enters at plot time as a separate, **named** factor. That is what
leaves the Week 9 curve-collapse test something to actually test.

---

## Two consequences that require action elsewhere

**The axis compresses.** The measure is a square root, so R=2→3 moves it 0.405
while R=8→16 moves it 0.037 — the headline plot's most severe points bunch into
a sliver. **T1.4 must design operator grids that spread *distance* evenly rather
than acceleration evenly**, and $d^2$ (the plain fraction of dimensions whose
measured status changed) is the interpretable secondary axis.

**Naming collision.** `operator_norm` in `operators/distance.py` is
$\lVert A - B \rVert_F$ — family (B) — *not* the family-(A) operator norm T3.2
warns against. Two different things with confusable names; worth a comment at
the call site before someone conflates them in a figure caption.

## What this hands to Track 3 (contract S1)

T3.3's L1 derivation was written contingently against "the T3.2-recommended
aggregate subspace distance". That is now the ratified choice, so the
contingency is discharged.

One caveat handed forward with it: T3.2 §3 left open whether the *operator-norm*
form is still needed **inside** the proof, separately from its unsuitability as
the plotted distance. T3.3 must check that against its own proof technique
rather than inheriting the plotted quantity silently — the distance that is
plotted and the distance that is proved need not be the same object.
