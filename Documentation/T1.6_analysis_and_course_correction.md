### The three fronts against the central claim

The claim: *the conformal guarantee fails under operator shift, can be restored, and the residual gap is bounded by a computable operator distance.*

**Front I — empirical core (MRI).** The phenomenon has been observed on real k-space: naive transfer held 0.944 coverage at the calibration operator and collapsed to 0.217 at R=4 and 0.014 at R=8, monotone against operator distance (ρ = +0.829). That is the project's central claim surviving contact with real measurements rather than phantoms. It is **not reportable** — reference sampler, M4Raw substrate, and D-024's confound — but as a feasibility signal at a feasibility checkpoint it is the right evidence.

**Front II — cross-domain (CT).** The pipeline runs end to end on real LoDoPaB data, which is ahead of the Phase-3 schedule. One defect blocks any reported curve: **D-012**, the FBP-based null projector amplifies (‖P_N x‖/‖x‖ = 1.74 at 30 views, where an orthogonal projection cannot exceed 1) and degrades *monotonically in the same direction as operator distance*. A CT coverage curve could therefore come out monotone because the decomposition degrades rather than because reliability does. Confirmed independently during this review. The conjugate-gradient fix must land before Front II produces anything.

**Front III — theory.** T3.1 complete, T3.2 substantially complete, T3.3 first pass with Step 1 rigorous and Step 2 honestly flagged. The front is ahead of schedule and has already fed back into method design: T3.3 §5's smoothness requirement is why the score combination is affine rather than a ratio (D25). That is §19's cross-verification working in the direction it is least expected to — theory constraining engineering.

**No front is lagging in the §19 sense.** The constraint is a single blocked task, not a track falling behind.

### Scoop recheck (Q5) — the finding that changes something

D11 mandates an early preprint for priority; Q5 required the competitive sweep to be repeated before further design work. Done, and it moves one thing.

**The targeted gap is still open.** Nothing found calibrates conformal prediction at one acquisition operator, deploys at another, and restores coverage. The §6 landscape table stands.

**But the adjacent literature has closed in on two sides.**

*"Pixelwise Uncertainty Quantification of Accelerated MRI Reconstruction"* (arXiv 2601.13236, Jan 2026) applies conformalized quantile regression to accelerated MRI across **acceleration factors 2 to 10**, and reports that predictors *calibrated for individual acceleration rates* satisfy coverage at their corresponding rates. That is precisely CALIBER's *matched-operator upper-bound baseline*, published. They have the substrate, the acceleration sweep, and the conformal machinery already assembled; the mismatched cell is one experiment away. **This is the highest-priority competitive risk in the project, and it makes D11's preprint urgent rather than aspirational.**

*CHEM* (arXiv 2512.09806, Dec 2025) is a **conformal** hallucination-estimation metric that localises hallucination-prone regions distribution-free, and its **null-space hallucination map decomposes the reconstruction via SVD of the forward operator**. It compares the prediction's null-space component *against the ground truth's*, so it is an evaluation metric rather than a deployment-time score — closer to CALIBER's `err_free` **label** than to its `s_free` **score**. Two consequences:

1. **Independent convergence on the label.** CHEM arrived at the same ground-truth criterion CALIBER uses in D21. That is corroboration of the criterion's form, and worth citing as such.
2. **The decomposition is not a contribution and must not be written as one.** "Conformal + hallucination localisation + distribution-free" and "null-space/range decomposition" are both now published under a fixed operator. **CALIBER's novelty rests entirely on operator shift.** Any draft claiming the operator-aware score itself as a contribution would be wrong on the record. Logged as D31.

Also noted, adjacent but not competing: Wen, Ahmad and Schniter, *Minimax Multi-Target Conformal Prediction* (TMLR, Nov 2025) — multi-target intervals for imaging inverse problems, including multi-round measurement acquisition. Different problem; the group remains the one to watch.

# Phase 2 Gate — October 2026

## Verdict

**The central claim is demonstrated in both modalities. The secondary claim is not, and cannot be from this prior.** That separation is the gate's main output, and it is sharper than "results are not reportable yet".

Phase 2's job was the empirical core: show the conformal guarantee fails under operator shift and that group-conditional calibration restores it. It is done, on a corrected label, in MRI and CT, with 13-split intervals. Phase 3's cross-domain artefact arrived early with it.

What is *not* established is that the operator-aware score is a good hallucination detector. Two independent measurements say the decomposition is not earning its place, and a third says the detection numbers cannot be read at all under this prior. Those are different problems from D-016 and they need different remedies.

### The three fronts against the central claim

**Front I — holds, and the direction I asserted was wrong.** O3 is demonstrated as the blueprint wrote it. My D50 claim that descending the grid is where coverage collapses does not survive the corrected label (P-30), which I retracted in D61; the retraction is now confirmed on the full bank rather than on phantoms.

**Front II — holds, and it is the stronger of the two.** CT's failure is *graded* in a way MRI's is not: coverage falls monotonically with both view count and arc, below nominal on every split at four operators. MRI's failures are concentrated at matched acceleration across mask families. CT is the better headline.

**Front III — a first pass with a repaired error, and nothing yet about the real project.** T3.5 is honest that its harness is synthetic. L1 remains unvalidated against a real bank.

## The two findings that change what can be claimed

### 1. The decomposition is not earning its place on detection

Two measurements, both Track 2's, pointing the same way:

- **Posterior dispersion ranks hallucinations within 0.001–0.012 AUROC of `s_disagree`** at every operator.
- **The equal-weight combination never beats its best single component**, worse in 7 of 9 operators by 0.002–0.014 AUROC.

Read carefully, these say different things and only one is damaging.

The combination result is **inside the noise**: 0.002–0.014 against a patient-split floor of 0.034–0.048 in coverage units. Consistent in direction in 7 of 9 operators is a sign test at one-sided *p* = 0.090, two-sided 0.180 — suggestive, not established. So the honest statement is "no evidence the combination helps", not "the combination hurts".

The dispersion result is the serious one, and its mechanism is the point: **DDNM replaces the range component structurally, so the posterior spread is already confined to the null space.** Posterior dispersion and `s_disagree` are therefore the same quantity computed two ways, not two methods agreeing. On this solver the operator decomposition *cannot* add anything to `s_disagree`, because the solver has already done the decomposition.

**Scope decision.** The decomposition's value is testable only on a solver that does *not* structurally confine spread to the null space. D-033 measured DPS's null-error share at 0.817–0.852 against DDNM's 0.99. **So the solver ablation must include DPS, and it is no longer an ablation — it is the only configuration in which the decomposition can earn its keep.** Running it on DDNM alone would be measuring the solver's design, not the score's.

### 2. Coverage survives the label problems; detection does not

This separation decides what the midterm may claim, and it has not been stated anywhere else.

**Coverage claims survive.** O3 and O5 are statements about calibration *behaviour* under a fixed label: a threshold transferred across operators loses coverage, and a per-group threshold does not. That holds whatever the label means, because both arms are scored against the same one. The label's defects change the *interpretation* of "hallucination", not the validity of the comparison.

**Detection claims do not survive.** They are statements about the score's merit against a label which is approximately a function of the score. T2.7 measured `err_free ≈ √(s_free² + free_content²)` to Spearman 0.99 — which is D38's identity at `cos θ ≈ 0` — so the stratified AUROCs of 0.95–1.00 are measuring the identity. D65 said this for one bank; it now holds on both modalities.

**So: the midterm reports coverage failure and restoration, in both modalities, with intervals. It does not report detection quality as a result.** Where detection numbers appear they are labelled as what they are — a consequence of the identity under a mismatched prior.

## Course corrections

1. **The MRI coverage-gap-vs-distance curve does not work and should not be forced.** ρ = +0.316 from `random1d R2` against CT's +0.976, because MRI's worst failures sit at matched acceleration across families where the scalar distance is ≈ 0. That is D47's finding confirmed: the family change is categorical, not a coordinate. **Plot MRI as one line per family and let CT carry the curve claim.** A single MRI curve through nine points would be a line fitted through two clusters.
2. **The noise floor is the patient split, not the seed** — 0.034–0.048 against 0.007 and 0.001. This retires D59's emphasis, which asked for a second seed sweep at R8. No ablation effect below ~0.05 coverage is an effect, and that bar should be printed beside every ablation table.
3. **Tool 2 is dominated and [7] is untested.** Tool 2 is valid on 2 of 3 interior folds and beaten by the nearest-operator arm. [7] (D66) fixes the location error but has only ever run on a two-group fold where it reduces to `nearest`. **Run `tool2_holdout.py` on the CT grid**, which has the most interior operators in the project.
4. **T1.7 starts now, and it starts with the preprint.** D11 called it urgent in August on competitive grounds (arXiv 2601.13236). There is now a defensible result to write: a demonstrated failure and fix in two modalities with structurally different operators, which is the paper, with detection explicitly out of scope.
