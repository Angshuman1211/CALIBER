### The three fronts against the central claim

The claim: *the conformal guarantee fails under operator shift, can be restored, and the residual gap is bounded by a computable operator distance.*

**Front I — empirical core (MRI).** The phenomenon has been observed on real k-space: naive transfer held 0.944 coverage at the calibration operator and collapsed to 0.217 at R=4 and 0.014 at R=8, monotone against operator distance (ρ = +0.829). That is the project's central claim surviving contact with real measurements rather than phantoms. It is **not reportable** — reference sampler, M4Raw substrate, and D-024's confound — but as a feasibility signal at a feasibility checkpoint it is the right evidence.

**Front II — cross-domain (CT).** The pipeline runs end to end on real LoDoPaB data, which is ahead of the Phase-3 schedule. One defect blocks any reported curve: **D-012**, the FBP-based null projector amplifies (‖P_N x‖/‖x‖ = 1.74 at 30 views, where an orthogonal projection cannot exceed 1) and degrades *monotonically in the same direction as operator distance*. A CT coverage curve could therefore come out monotone because the decomposition degrades rather than because reliability does. Confirmed independently during this review. The conjugate-gradient fix must land before Front II produces anything.

**Front III — theory.** T3.1 complete, T3.2 substantially complete, T3.3 first pass with Step 1 rigorous and Step 2 honestly flagged. The front is ahead of schedule and has already fed back into method design: T3.3 §5's smoothness requirement is why the score combination is affine rather than a ratio (D25). That is §19's cross-verification working in the direction it is least expected to — theory constraining engineering.

**No front is lagging in the §19 sense.** The constraint is a single blocked task, not a track falling behind.

### Scoop recheck (Q5) — the finding that changes something

D11 mandates an early preprint for priority; Q5 required the competitive sweep to be repeated before further design work. Done, and it moves one thing.

**The targeted gap is still open.** Nothing found calibrates conformal prediction at one acquisition operator, deploys at another, and restores coverage. The §6 landscape table stands.

**But the adjacent literature has closed in on two sides.**

*"Pixelwise Uncertainty Quantification of Accelerated MRI Reconstruction"* (arXiv 2601.13236, Jan 2026) applies conformalized quantile regression to accelerated MRI across **acceleration factors 2 to 10**, and reports that predictors *calibrated for individual acceleration rates* satisfy coverage at their corresponding rates. That is precisely CALIBER's *matched-operator upper-bound baseline*, published. They have the substrate, the acceleration sweep, and the conformal machinery already assembled; the mismatched cell is one experiment away. **This is the highest-priority competitive risk in the project, and it makes D11's preprint urgent rather than aspirational.**

*CHEM* (arXiv 2512.09806, Dec 2025) is a **conformal** hallucination-estimation metric that localises hallucination-prone regions distribution-free, and its **null-space hallucination map decomposes the reconstruction via SVD of the forward operator**. It compares the prediction's null-space component *against the ground truth's*, so it is an evaluation metric rather than a deployment-time score — closer to CALIBER's `err_free` **label** than to its `s_free` **score**. Two consequences:

1. **Independent convergence on the label.** CHEM arrived at the same ground-truth criterion CALIBER uses in D21. That is corroboration of the criterion's form, and worth citing as such.
2. **The decomposition is not a contribution and must not be written as one.** "Conformal + hallucination localisation + distribution-free" and "null-space/range decomposition" are both now published under a fixed operator. **CALIBER's novelty rests entirely on operator shift.** Any draft claiming the operator-aware score itself as a contribution would be wrong on the record. Logged as D31.

Also noted, adjacent but not competing: Wen, Ahmad and Schniter, *Minimax Multi-Target Conformal Prediction* (TMLR, Nov 2025) — multi-target intervals for imaging inverse problems, including multi-round measurement acquisition. Different problem; the group remains the one to watch.
