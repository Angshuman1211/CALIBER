## Objective

T3.3 §6 and T3.4 §5 handed this task a list: does the Lipschitz picture hold beyond R=16, beyond `random1d`, beyond a white-noise solver, on a non-mask operator; what is the density constant D; and do the three mismatches between the bound and the experiments (T3.3 §4b) matter in numbers. This document answers each on small synthetic operators and the committed real-prior bank, and records what each answer changes upstream.

**Code.** `src/caliber/theory/` (`stats.py`: KS, W1, TV, D estimators; `harness.py`: the controlled score generator, generalised from T3.3's script with a mask-family / R / weights / solver axis), `scripts/t3_5_*.py` (one per check), `tests/test_theory_checks.py` (10 gates, including one that executes a script — P-19's lesson).

Neither is a diffusion model; `prior` is the cheapest harness in which the reconstruction genuinely depends on the operator.

## 2. Finding 1 — T3.3's TV step is a Kolmogorov step; the chain is simpler and tighter

**The error.** T3.3 §3(iii) writes two "exact identities" for 1-D laws, with `G = F_cal − F_dep`: `d_TV = sup_t |G(t)|` and `W₁ = ∫|G|`. The second is right. The first is the **Kolmogorov distance** `d_K = sup_t|G(t)|` (events are half-lines); total variation is `½∫|q_cal − q_dep|` (events are all sets). `d_K ≤ d_TV`, strictly in general. The tent argument is correct and proves `d_K ≤ √(D·W₁)` where `D = sup|q_cal − q_dep|`. It does not prove the TV version, which is false:

| k | KS | W₁ | TV | D | √(D·W₁) | KS ≤ | TV ≤ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1592 | 0.0796 | 0.1592 | 0.5 | 0.1995 | True | True |
| 2 | 0.0796 | 0.0398 | 0.1592 | 0.5 | 0.1411 | True | **False** |
| 5 | 0.0318 | 0.0159 | 0.1592 | 0.5 | 0.0892 | True | **False** |
| 20 | 0.0080 | 0.0040 | 0.1592 | 0.5 | 0.0446 | True | **False** |
| 50 | 0.0032 | 0.0016 | 0.1592 | 0.5 | 0.0282 | True | **False** |

(`q_a = 1`, `q_b = 1 + 0.5·sin(2πkx)` on [0,1]: a high-frequency density difference leaves TV fixed while KS and W₁ shrink. Pinned in `test_total_variation_can_exceed_sqrt_d_w1`.)

**The repair does not need TV at all.** For split conformal the event is a threshold event, i.e. a half-line, and the argument is one line. Let `S₁…Sₙ ~ Q_cal` be independent, `q̂` the conformal quantile (a function of them only) and `S ~ Q_dep` independent of them. Conditional on the calibration scores, `P(S ≤ q̂) = F_dep(q̂)`. By ordinary split-conformal validity `E[F_cal(q̂)] ≥ 1 − α`, and `|F_dep − F_cal| ≤ d_K` pointwise, so

$$\mathbb{E}\,[F_{\text{dep}}(\hat q)] \;\ge\; 1-\alpha - d_K(Q_{\text{cal}}, Q_{\text{dep}}).$$

Mirrored to the repo's lower-tail rule (flag when score ≥ τ, τ the k-th smallest positive, k = ⌊α(n+1)⌋): miss rate at deployment ≤ α + `d_K`. Chain:

$$\text{gap} \;\le\; d_K \;\le\; \sqrt{D\cdot W_1} \;\le\; \sqrt{D L}\;\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}.$$

This is tighter than T3.3's boxed `2·d_TV` (no factor 2, and `d_K ≤ d_TV`), and needs no appeal to [3]'s Theorem 4. It requires independent calibration scores (see §6.2 for when that fails). T3.3 has since been rewritten on this chain (§2b there), including a cluster-level proposition with proof; its test is `test_cluster_proposition_grid_step_holds_with_the_stated_probability`.

## 3. Finding 2 — the density constant D, measured

`scripts/t3_5_density_constant.py`. 2-component score (`w_free = w_disagree = ½`, D42), scales frozen on `random1d` R=2 (D27), Gaussian-KDE densities with a Silverman-type bandwidth.

**Noise harness** (n = 4,800 per law):

| dep | KS | W₁ | TV | D | √(D·W₁) | KS ≤ | TV ≤ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R3 | 0.988 | 0.170 | 0.979 | 11.3 | 1.390 | True | True |
| R4 | 1.000 | 0.266 | 0.999 | 10.5 | 1.671 | True | True |
| R8 | 1.000 | 0.409 | 1.000 | 9.1 | 1.925 | True | True |
| R16 | 1.000 | 0.495 | 1.000 | 8.3 | 2.021 | True | True |

The two laws are **essentially disjoint** (KS ≈ 1): with white-noise fill and eight draws the score is a nearly deterministic function of the null fraction, so a different operator moves it by many spreads. Every bound in the chain is then vacuous (`√(D·W₁) > 1`). This is a property of the harness, not of the method — and it is exactly why T3.3's mean-shift ratios looked healthy while the *coverage* was destroyed (§6.3).

**Prior harness** (n = 1,600 per law):

| dep | KS | W₁ | TV | D | √(D·W₁) | KS ≤ | TV ≤ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R3 | 0.344 | 0.126 | 0.332 | 1.74 | 0.469 | True | True |
| R4 | 0.416 | 0.207 | 0.408 | 1.80 | 0.610 | True | True |
| R8 | 0.403 | 0.180 | 0.392 | 1.63 | 0.542 | True | True |
| R16 | 0.406 | 0.189 | 0.396 | 1.63 | 0.554 | True | True |

Here the bound is informative: `√(D·W₁)` is 1.3–1.5× KS. TV ≈ KS because the difference between these two laws is smooth (a shift-like change); the analytic family in §2 is what breaks the TV version.

**D depends on the KDE bandwidth** (prior harness, R=8; KS = 0.403 and W₁ = 0.180 do not move): bandwidth ×0.25 → D = 1.98 (bound 0.596); ×1 → 1.63 (0.542); ×4 → 0.89 (0.401). D is a sup over a smoothed density difference, so it is only meaningful reported with its bandwidth, and any bound quoting a single D should state the smoothing scale — otherwise it is a free parameter. `d_K` and `W₁` are exact empirical quantities and are the safer ones to carry into T3.6.

Three things follow. ### 4.3 The seed noise floor is large

T1.4 §6 names the mask-seed noise floor as the prerequisite for reading anything. On the prior harness the spread across two seeds is 0.08–0.14 on ratios of 0.25–0.31 at the T3.3 range, and 0.4–0.65 on ratios of 0.9–1.4 beyond R=16. A secant measured at one mask seed is not a number; this is the first measurement of that floor in the project, on a toy prior, and it says it is large.

## 5. Finding 4 — the closed form (T3.4 §2b) and CT

T3.4 §2b: under nested masks `d_op² = Δ null_frac`, and if `s_free ∝ √null_frac` then the free ratio follows `(√(a+d²) − √a)/d`.

- **Noise harness, `random1d`:** measured / closed form = 1.41, 1.43, 1.46, 1.46, 1.47, 1.47 (R = 3…16). Pinned in `test_free_component_ratio_follows_the_sqrt_null_frac_shape`.
- **Structured prior:** the quotient is 1.2, 1.5, 1.1, 1.0, 0.9, 0.9 over the same R and 2.5–4.1 beyond R=16, mask-seed-dependent. **The law is the white-noise null hypothesis and the prior breaks it**, as D-046 measured on the real bank (1.19 vs 1.08).
- **CT sparse-view** (`ct.sparse_view`, 32×32, calibration 60 views; `scripts/t3_5_ct_check.py`, 8 trials, one geometry — indicative only). `nf_eff` is the Hutchinson estimate of the effective null fraction, because CT's `null_space_dim` is the dimension-count bound and is **0** here (views × detectors exceeds the pixel count; D-007).

| operator | nf_eff | d_op | growth | 2-comp | free | law quotient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v45 | 0.224 | 0.143 | +0.117 | 0.769 | 1.021 | 1.030 |
| v30 | 0.336 | 0.328 | +0.312 | 1.133 | 1.162 | 1.096 |
| v20 | 0.487 | 0.489 | +0.478 | 1.290 | 1.312 | 1.108 |
| v12 | 0.648 | 0.634 | +0.629 | 1.351 | 1.348 | 1.099 |
| v8 | 0.762 | 0.711 | +0.708 | 1.298 | 1.289 | 1.051 |

  The `√nf_eff` law holds to within 3–11% on sparse-view CT with white-noise fill (quotient ≈ 1.03–1.11). It **fails on limited-angle**: 0.97, 0.87, 0.76, 0.65 for arcs of 150°, 120°, 90°, 60°, with the secant ratio falling from 0.44 to 0.24 as the arc shrinks — the opposite of sparse-view, a plausible reading (not tested here) is that a restricted arc removes directions that carry little image energy first, so the energy-blind null fraction overstates the change. So an energy-blind null-fraction coordinate describes sparse-view CT and not limited-angle CT, a structural difference from MRI that the cross-modality collapse claim (§11.2) will have to confront. Also: `d_op` for CT is a Hutchinson estimate (48 probes), `ambient`-normalised, and tracks the effective null fraction closely (0.143…0.711 against 0.224…0.762), so D19 behaves as a graded, non-saturating distance on CT sparse-view without a closed form — the cross-check T1.3 and T3.2 left open, on a 32×32 operator (the registry's full-size CT is blocked behind D-012 for *reported* curves, not for this synthetic check).

## 6. Finding 5 — the three T3.3 §4b mismatches, in numbers

`scripts/t3_5_conformal_chain.py` (harness, with the repo's own `split_threshold`) and `scripts/t3_5_bank_check.py` (committed bank). `gap = (1 − α) − coverage_dep`; positive = under-coverage; α = 0.1. Label: relative criterion with τ at the 0.6 quantile of the R=2 ratio, as a design split would set it.

### 6.1 Label conditioning (prior harness, 100 trials)

| cal → dep | unconditional gap | KS | label-conditional gap | KS (conditional) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R2 → R4 | −0.085 | 0.416 | −0.099 | 0.619 |
| **R4 → R2** | +0.257 | 0.416 | **+0.521** | 0.619 |
| R2 → R8 | −0.062 | 0.402 | −0.086 | 0.563 |
| **R8 → R2** | +0.137 | 0.402 | **+0.340** | 0.563 |
| R2 → R16 | −0.060 | 0.406 | −0.086 | 0.565 |
| R16 → R2 | +0.131 | 0.406 | +0.338 | 0.565 |

Positive rates barely move on this harness (0.40 / 0.46 / 0.40 / 0.40), yet **conditioning raises KS by 40–50%** (0.40 → 0.56–0.62), and the label-conditional under-coverage gap (+0.52, +0.34) is **larger than the unconditional KS** (0.416, 0.402) in the very cells where it is largest. So a bound proved on the unconditional law would be violated by the quantity the project controls. The corrected chain holds in all twelve cells when each gap is compared with the KS of its own law (unconditional against unconditional, conditional against conditional). **T3.3's bound must be stated on the law of scores given `label = 1`**, which is also the only thing T1.2's calibrator uses. On the committed real-prior bank the same pattern appears (§7).

### 6.2 Independence unit — calibrating on whole images

Calibrating on `m` images (16 dependent regions each), testing on regions of fresh images, **no operator shift**, target 0.90. Noise harness:

| m images | 2 | 5 | 10 | 25 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean coverage | 0.765 | 0.851 | 0.877 | 0.894 | 0.898 |
| sd | 0.198 | 0.125 | 0.090 | 0.059 | 0.042 |
| 5% quantile | 0.361 | 0.606 | 0.694 | 0.786 | 0.826 |

The prior harness shows essentially none of it (mean 0.895–0.900 for m = 2…25; sd 0.07 → 0.025): there, regions of one image are nearly independent. So the penalty is real and substantial **when regions within an image share their score** (the noise harness; the cause was not isolated here, `ref_intensity` and the shared draw noise are the candidates) and absent when they do not. In the real pipeline the dependence is whatever the solver and normalisation induce; it is measurable per bank, and the sd column is what a cluster bootstrap would need to cover. The real-bank answer (and T1.4's patient-level clusters, D-027) is to resample whole items, as `t3_5_bank_check.py` does. Step 1's independence hypothesis should be stated at the cluster level.

### 6.3 Why the mean-shift ratio never saw the problem

On the noise harness the naive-transfer gaps are **exactly +0.900 / −0.100** in the two directions for every pair (R2↔R4, R2↔R8, R2↔R16, unconditional and conditional) — a trivial reproduction of D47's measured +0.902 / −0.097 — while T3.3's mean-shift ratio is a mild 0.14–0.75. The laws are disjoint (KS = 1, §3), so a threshold from one lies wholly outside the other. A *mean-shift* Lipschitz constant says nothing about *coverage* unless the spread is accounted for; the chain needs the CDF distance, not the mean movement.

### 6.4 Direction

Gaps are asymmetric on every harness (table in §6.1; −0.10 against +0.52 for R2/R4). The chain bound is symmetric in the pair, so it holds in both directions but is **tight in neither**: it is a statement about |gap|, and the benign direction (negative gap, over-coverage with exploding flag area, D47) is an efficiency failure it does not describe. T3.6 should report KS per ordered pair next to the gap and keep the two directions on separate axes.

### 6.5 Solver dependence

The noise and prior harnesses give different constants (§4.2) on identical operators, scores and weights. Where the reconstruction depends on the operator through the solver, L is not a property of the score alone. T3.3 §3(ii) should be stated "for a fixed solver family".

## 7. Finding 6 — the corrected chain on a real-prior bank

`scripts/t3_5_bank_check.py banks/w02_solver_smoke` (DDNM + `ddpm-celebahq-256` on M4Raw, R=4 and R=8, **3 items**, 192 regions per operator; **not a result**: face prior, tiny sample). Scales frozen at the source operator, 2-component score, relative criterion τ = 1.75.

| src → dst | gap | KS (conditional) | 90% bootstrap over items | 2·TV (cond.) | KS (unconditional) | positive rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R4 → R8 | −0.098 | 0.707 | [0.65, 0.79] | 1.258 | 0.552 | 0.16 → 0.35 |
| **R8 → R4** | **+0.469** | 0.707 | [0.65, 0.79] | 1.256 | 0.552 | 0.35 → 0.16 |

`bound_agreement`: violation rate 0.0 against all three bounds; median slack 0.52 (KS cond.), 1.07 (2·TV), 0.37 (KS unc.). The under-coverage direction sits at 0.469 against a conditional KS of 0.707 and an unconditional 0.552: the corrected chain holds with the conditional law, and here also with the unconditional (not a guarantee — §6.1 shows it can fail). The positive rate moves 0.16 → 0.35 across the pair, the P-21 effect on a real prior. The item-level bootstrap interval is only a spread over 3 items; it says nothing about the population.

## 9. The label-conditional link (T3.3 §3b) — what the harness says

`scripts/t3_5_conditional_link.py`. T3.3 §3b derives, under a common coupling of images and noise across the two operators, $d_K^+\le\big(\sup_t|G_A-G_B|+|\pi_A-\pi_B|\big)/\pi_A$ with $G_X(t)=P(S_X\le t,\Lambda_X=1)$, then bounds the two numerators by the label-flip probability $P_{\text{flip}}$ and the crossing probability. Every term is evaluated on the paired harness (calibration `random1d` R=2, τ at the 0.6 quantile of the calibration ratio).

**Structured-prior harness** (100 trials; π_cal = 0.400):

| dep | d_op | π_dep | P_flip | crossing | sup\|G_A−G_B\| | algebra bound | measured d_K⁺ | holds | d_K⁺/√d_op | P_flip/√d_op |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R3 | 0.405 | 0.414 | 0.231 | 0.369 | 0.173 | 0.467 | 0.441 | True | 0.693 | 0.363 |
| R4 | 0.500 | 0.463 | 0.276 | 0.433 | 0.234 | 0.744 | 0.619 | True | 0.875 | 0.390 |
| R8 | 0.612 | 0.403 | 0.244 | 0.429 | 0.224 | 0.569 | 0.563 | True | 0.720 | 0.312 |
| R16 | 0.661 | 0.398 | 0.244 | 0.436 | 0.227 | 0.573 | 0.565 | True | 0.695 | 0.300 |

**White-noise harness** (150 trials; π_cal = 0.400): the algebra bound holds in all four cells (1.24, 1.05, 1.34, 1.40 against measured d_K⁺ of 0.987, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000), but the laws are disjoint (§3), so the bound and the rate are vacuous there.

What this does and does not establish:
- **Step (a) is exact algebra and holds in 8 of 8 cells**; on the structured harness it is tight (within 1% at R8 and R16, 20% at R4). Pinned independently in `test_label_conditional_decomposition_is_exact_algebra`.
- **The $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$ rate is consistent, not demonstrated.** d_K⁺/√d_op stays in 0.69–0.88 over four operators, and $P_{\text{flip}}$ is 0.23–0.28. But $P_{\text{flip}}$ is flat over the range rather than growing like √d_op — it saturates, as does $d_{\text{op}}$ itself (D-052) — so four points cannot separate √d_op from a constant.
- **The crossing bound is loose** (0.37–0.44 against sup\|G_A−G_B\| of 0.17–0.23): the sum $P_{\text{flip}}+\text{crossing}$ over-counts, because flips and crossings overlap.
- **The constants of step (c)** ($L_\rho$, $M_\rho$, $M_S$) were not estimated. $M_\rho$ is a margin condition on the label statistic at τ and needs ground truth; a bank with enough items (T3.6) is the place to estimate them.
- **The D60 candidacy gate** moves with the operator (`free_content` depends on $A$), which creates label flips not covered by the Lipschitz assumption on $\rho$ (T3.3 §3b, cost 2). The harness does not apply the gate, so this was not exercised.

## Honest limitations

- **Both harnesses are toys.** Neither contains a diffusion prior. The noise harness has no image information in the fill (laws nearly deterministic, §3); the prior harness is a blur-prior alternating projection. Constants differ 2–5× between them, which is the point of §4.2 but also means no number here transfers to a real solver.
- **One image population** (Shepp–Logan-style phantoms, 128² or 32²), one region size, one measurement-noise level, equal 2-comp weights.
- **CT is 32×32, one geometry family each, 8 trials.** Indicative of structure (law holds / fails), not of magnitude. Full-size CT curves are Track 2's and blocked on D-012.
- **D is KDE-dependent** (§3); it is reported with its bandwidth and not proposed as a quantity to carry forward.
- **The real-prior bank has 3 items.** §7 is a demonstration that the check runs on real scores, not evidence about the method.
- **Prior-harness numbers use 2 mask seeds and 30–100 trials**; their spreads are large and are shown rather than averaged away.
- **The corrected chain assumes independent calibration scores.** §6.2 measures what is lost when that fails; the item-level restatement of the proof is not done here.
