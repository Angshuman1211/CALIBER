**Task:** T2.4 — Expand the score bank across the MRI grid · Build the evaluation harness
**Track:** 2 — Systems, Data Engineering and Cross-Domain Validation
**Deliverable (blueprint v3.0 §18, Week 4 card arm B):** Score bank expanded across the MRI grid (acceleration factor, mask family); evaluation harness operational — coverage, flag area, region FDR/power
**Week:** 4 (Demonstrate the Coverage Failure)
**Status:** Delivered. The grid ran — 9 operators x 30 items, 69,120 regions — and the harness reports it. **O3 is partially achieved and the shortfall is the finding:** the coverage failure under operator shift is real and quantified (§7), but it is *confounded with patient shift*, because matched-per-operator calibration — the upper-bound baseline — also fails, at 0.73-0.79 against a nominal 0.90, while landing on 0.9005 in-sample. ## 1. The harness is operational

All six metrics of Blueprint §15.2 exist in `caliber.evaluation.metrics` and are
exercised by `scripts/analyse_bank.py` on every run:

| §15.2 metric | function | in the per-operator table |
|---|---|---|
| Empirical coverage against nominal | `empirical_coverage` | `empirical_coverage`, `coverage_gap` |
| Flag area at fixed reliability | `flag_area` | `flag_area` |
| Region-level FDR and power | `region_fdr` / `region_power` | `region_fdr` |
| Coverage gap against operator distance | `coverage_curve` | `operator_distance`, Spearman ρ |
| Effective sample size under weighting | `effective_sample_size` | (Week 6, Tool 2) |
| Measured gap vs derived bound | `bound_agreement` | (Week 8, against L1) |

**One clarification worth stating because the card lists them as two things.** Region
*power* and empirical *coverage* are the same number here, and deliberately so: the
controlled risk is the region-level **miss rate**, so coverage is the fraction of
genuinely hallucinated regions that were flagged — which is precisely power. The module
defines `region_power` as an alias rather than as a second quantity, so no report can
show them disagreeing.

Output on the Week 3 bank, α = 0.10, τ = 1.75 (Track 1's ratified value), thresholds
fitted on `split=calib` and scored on `split=test`:

```
             operator_id  operator_distance  empirical_coverage  coverage_gap  flag_area
mri.random1d.R2.c0.08.s0             0.0000              0.7988        0.1012     0.7988
mri.random1d.R4.c0.08.s0             0.5000              1.0000       -0.1000     1.0000
mri.random1d.R8.c0.08.s0             0.6124              0.9941       -0.0941     0.9941
```

The harness also now reports what it *cannot* certify. Closing D-026 (§4) added a

## 2. The committed Week 4 config could never have run

`configs/experiments/w04_mri_operator_grid.yaml` was written in Week 1, before any
wall-clock had been measured, and had not been executed since. It asked for DPS at 200
steps, 3 families × 6 accelerations, 200 items and 8 posterior draws.

At this machine's measured rate — 1.46 s per UNet forward at 256², CPU only (P-16):

```
1.46 s × 200 steps × 8 draws × 200 items × 18 operators = 8.4e6 s = 97 days
```

before counting the backward pass DPS needs at every step and DDNM does not. It survived
three weeks because **nothing runs a config until someone runs it** — the config tests
check that configs *load*, which is not the same as checking that they could finish.

The replacement carries W03's measured settings (DDNM, 24 steps, 4 draws, 30 items, 256²)
with the operator set widened to 3 families × 3 accelerations:

```
1.46 s × 24 steps × 4 draws = 140 s per item-operator
      × 30 items × 9 operators = ~10.5 hours
```

Keeping the per-item settings identical to W03 is not only thrift. §13 requires the
solver held fixed across the curve, and it makes the Week 3 bank a literal subset of this
one, so the two are mergeable (`merge_banks`) and comparable without a caveat.

**Draw batching is set explicitly.** Without `max_draws_per_batch: 1` the four draws go
through the UNet in one batch and the preflight puts the peak at **18.0 GB** against this
machine's 26.8 GB commit limit; batching brings it to **6.0 GB**. The bank is identical
either way — per-draw seeds are derived, not sequential (D-041) — and on CPU there is no
batching speedup being given up. A ten-hour run is the one you least want dying at hour
seven.

## 3. Why the grid is 3 × 3, and what it cannot answer

Choosing the distance axis for the coverage-failure figure meant computing all five
candidate distances across the grid. Two findings came out of that, and both are recorded
as **D-052**.

### The candidates are not five measures

Over **all 153 pairs** of a 3-family × 6-acceleration grid at 256²:

| pair | agreement |
|---|---|
| `null_space_projection` vs `sampling_support` | identical to **1.1e-16** |
| `chordal_subspace` × √2 vs `sampling_support` | identical to **1.1e-16** |
| `operator_norm` vs `sampling_support` | within **9.1e-4**, Spearman ρ = 0.999915 |

This is a property of the operator class, not of the implementations. An MRI
undersampling operator is a diagonal projection in k-space: which frequencies it keeps
*is* its range, so "how far apart are the sampling supports" and "how far apart are the
null spaces" are one question asked twice.

**The consequence is a research one.** T1.3 asks which distance best predicts the
coverage gap, and T3.2 lists candidates to compare. On this operator class any ranking
among these four is numerical noise, and a Week 6 result that named a winner here would
not survive a reviewer who noticed they are the same function. That comparison needs an
operator class where the range is not a coordinate mask — CT view subsampling, Week 7.

### Distance saturates across mask families

Distance from the calibration operator `mri.random1d.R2`:

| | R2 | R4 | R8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `random1d` | 0.000 | 0.500 | 0.612 |
| `equispaced1d` | 0.644 | 0.656 | **0.650** |
| `variable_density2d` | 0.681 | 0.687 | 0.693 |

Any family change costs ~0.65 immediately, after which acceleration contributes ~0.01 —
and not even monotonically: equispaced R4 sits *above* R8. So the graded stretch of the
axis is entirely within-family, and a denser acceleration sweep across families buys
near-duplicate points. Three families × three accelerations covers the graded regime
(0 → 0.50 → 0.61) and the saturated one; 3 × 6 would have cost 21 hours for points the
distance measure cannot separate.

**This is worth flagging to Track 1 before the figure is drawn.** The expected headline —
coverage gap rising monotonically with operator distance — will be tested on an axis with
three clustered values and one graded arm, not on nine well-spread points.

## 4. Three defects fixed

**P-28 — the only non-degenerate distance could not be computed for any MRI operator.**
`estimate_spectrum` flattened its sketch to a real view up front, doubling the row count,
then reshaped a column of that real matrix back into the complex measurement shape: at
256² that is 131,072 values into a 65,536 slot. It raised for every complex operator,
which is every MRI operator. It worked for CT, which is real, and CT is what the tests
covered. Fixed by carrying the sketch in the operator's own domain and going real only
for the QR — bit-identical on real operators (max difference 0.0 against the old path on
a CT operator), working on complex ones.

The gap in the tests and the gap in the measurement coincided exactly. The untested path
was the one carrying the information.

**D-026 closed — the reference thresholds rounded up.** `split_conformal_threshold` and
`mondrian_thresholds` were a second implementation of a rule `conformal.py` already had,
and they had drifted: they took the `ceil((n+1)·alpha)`-th smallest positive score, which
at `n=1, alpha=0.1` returns that single score as a threshold whose true miss rate is
**1/2**. They now delegate. A second behaviour changed that was not in the original
report: with no positives at all the old code returned `+inf` — flag nothing, a 100% miss
rate presented as a successful calibration — where the delegate returns `-inf`.

On the Week 3 bank the thresholds move by **~0.001** and no reported number changes,
because every group carries 2,000–3,800 positives. That is why it survived four weeks:
the defect is invisible where the data is thick and wrong where a reviewer would look
hardest.

**Two sizing numbers I had reported were wrong**, both in the flattering direction, and
both are corrected in P-23. `sizing.py` had the test side as `96 × 0.42 = 40` patients, a
guess; the real draw at the default `calib_fraction` puts **44** there. And the Week 4
addendum scaled the R=4 paired SE while calling it the sized quantity, where the script
sizes on the widest operator. The single-operator cap is **0.119**, not the 0.125 that
went to Track 1; today's paired half-width is **0.127**, not 0.121; and the
`calib_fraction` lever buys 0.127 → 0.111, not the 0.121 → 0.091 I reported — a
contribution to needing more data, not a substitute for it.

## 5. The design split (D-051) — and what Track 1 is actually blocked on

T1.4 records T1.1 as *"complete pending the weight selection in D28, which needs the
design split and therefore real data."* The data is not the blocker. `SPLITS` was
`("calib", "test")`: there was no third side to select weights on, and that is Track 2's
module.

`split_for()` now takes `design_fraction`. The design side is carved out of the
**calibration** range, which buys two properties:

- **The test side never moves.** For any `design_fraction`, a patient lands on `test`
  exactly when it would have under the two-way split — so a design split can be
  introduced after coverage has been measured without invalidating the measurement.
- **`design_fraction=0.0` reproduces the two-way split exactly**, so no existing bank,
  pin or salt is disturbed. The golden pins and the real-fastMRI digest pass unchanged.

Counted on the real 96 patients at the default fraction, test fixed at 44 in every row:

| design_fraction | design | calib | test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 (today) | 0 | 52 | 44 |
| 0.10 | 8 | 44 | 44 |
| 0.15 | 19 | 33 | 44 |

The draw is lumpy at this size — 0.15 yields 19 rather than the nominal 14 — so the
fraction should be chosen by the count it produces. **Choosing it is Track 1's call**, and
it competes with the `calib_fraction` rebalance in P-23 for the same pool, so the two
should be decided together.

## 7. Results — the coverage failure, and what confounds it

Criterion: Track 1's ratified **relative** rule, `err_free / free_content > 1.75`
(D-037/D-038), giving 39,308 positives — **56.9%** of regions. Nominal coverage 0.90,
α = 0.10, thresholds fitted on `split=calib` and scored on `split=test`.

*(The default absolute rule, `err_free > 0.1`, labels **100%** of regions positive on this
bank, which makes region FDR identically zero and forces flag area to equal coverage. The
first pass of this analysis ran that way and the table was meaningless. P-02/D-034 is the
entry; the relative criterion is the one to use.)*

### 7.1 Naive transfer fails — but by over-flagging, not by under-covering

| operator | distance | coverage | gap | **flag area** | FDR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| random1d R2 *(calib)* | 0.000 | 0.862 | +0.038 | 0.694 | 0.627 |
| random1d R4 | 0.500 | **1.000** | −0.100 | **0.999** | 0.402 |
| random1d R8 | 0.612 | **1.000** | −0.100 | **0.991** | 0.283 |
| equispaced1d R2 | 0.644 | 0.758 | **+0.142** | 0.630 | 0.341 |
| equispaced1d R8 | 0.650 | **1.000** | −0.100 | **0.989** | 0.311 |
| equispaced1d R4 | 0.656 | 0.998 | −0.098 | 0.997 | 0.471 |
| variable_density2d R2 | 0.681 | 0.830 | +0.070 | **0.209** | 0.248 |
| variable_density2d R4 | 0.687 | 0.853 | +0.047 | 0.531 | 0.445 |
| variable_density2d R8 | 0.693 | 0.992 | −0.092 | 0.920 | 0.501 |

**Read the flag-area column, not the coverage column.** A threshold transferred from R=2
to R=4 reaches coverage 1.000 — and does it by flagging **99.9% of all regions**. That is
a flag that says "everything might be hallucinated", which is valid and worthless. Judged
on coverage alone naive transfer looks *better* than nominal at five of nine operators;
judged on flag area it has collapsed. This is the Blueprint §15.2 argument for reporting
flag area beside coverage, and this grid is the first place the project can show it
rather than assert it.

The one operator where naive transfer under-covers outright is equispaced1d R2
(0.758, gap +0.142) — a *family* change at the same acceleration.

### 7.2 The upper-bound baseline fails too, and that is the problem

Matched-per-operator calibration fits each operator's threshold on its own calibration
rows. It should sit at nominal. Held out, it does not:

| operator | in-sample | held-out |
|---|---|---|
| random1d R2 | 0.9006 | 0.862 |
| random1d R4 | 0.9003 | **0.730** |
| random1d R8 | 0.9004 | **0.726** |
| equispaced1d R2 | 0.9004 | 0.778 |
| equispaced1d R4 | 0.9006 | 0.755 |
| equispaced1d R8 | 0.9005 | 0.739 |
| variable_density2d R2 | 0.9007 | **0.973** |
| variable_density2d R4 | 0.9005 | 0.791 |
| variable_density2d R8 | 0.9004 | 0.768 |

**In-sample lands on 0.9004–0.9007 at every single operator**, so the conformal machinery
is exactly right and this is not a bug — it was checked for precisely that reason, since
a systematic 0.15 shortfall is not what sampling noise looks like.

What it is instead: **calibrating on one set of patients and deploying on another costs
~0.10–0.17 of coverage, before any operator changes at all.** That is the same order as
the operator-shift effect the experiment exists to measure. So the Week 4 headline —
"coverage degrades when the deployment operator moves" — has a competing explanation on
this bank, and the two are not separated by this design.

### 7.3 The monotone trend is not there

| method | Spearman ρ, gap vs operator distance |
|---|---|
| naive_transferred | **+0.305** |
| matched_per_operator | **−0.283** |
| group_conditional | −0.283 |

The claim under test (§11.2) is that the coverage gap rises monotonically with operator
distance. On nine operators it does not — weakly positive for naive, *negative* for
matched. D-052 predicted the axis would be poor for this: six of the nine operators sit
in a band of width 0.05 (0.644–0.693) because changing mask family saturates the
distance, so there is very little axis to be monotone along.

### 7.4 Paired gaps: only two of nine exclude zero

Against the calibration operator, paired by patient (D-049):

| deployment | gap | 95% CI | excludes 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| random1d R8 | +0.136 | [−0.098, 0.344] | no |
| random1d R4 | +0.132 | [−0.057, 0.326] | no |
| equispaced1d R8 | +0.123 | [−0.102, 0.321] | no |
| equispaced1d R4 | +0.107 | [−0.075, 0.298] | no |
| variable_density2d R8 | +0.095 | [−0.059, 0.265] | no |
| equispaced1d R2 | +0.085 | [−0.094, 0.264] | no |
| variable_density2d R4 | +0.072 | [0.019, 0.174] | **yes** |
| variable_density2d R2 | −0.110 | [−0.277, −0.001] | **yes** |
| random1d R2 *(self)* | 0.0000 | [0, 0] | — |

The self-gap comes back **exactly 0.0000** with SE 0, which is the sanity check on the
pairing. Pairing is **1.34×** narrower than differencing two independent estimates here.

**Seven of nine gaps cannot be distinguished from zero**, exactly as P-23 said they could
not be at this sample size: ±0.10 needs 59 clusters paired and this bank has 12. The
gaps are all in the expected direction and of a plausible size; the bank cannot certify
them individually.

### 7.5 Two baselines are the same method here

`matched_per_operator` and `group_conditional` agree in **every row**. That is not a bug:
`calib_group` is one-to-one with `operator_id` on this bank (checked), so Mondrian
calibration over groups *is* per-operator calibration. The group-conditional backbone only
becomes a distinct method when groups are coarser than operators, which this grid does not
exercise.

## 8. For the other tracks

**Track 1.** Three things, in order of how much they change the plan.

1. **Matched-per-operator calibration under-covers by 0.10–0.17 held out, at 0.9005
   in-sample** (§7.2). Patient shift alone costs as much coverage as operator shift is
   supposed to. Any Week 4 claim of the form "coverage degrades with operator shift" is
   confounded on this bank, and separating them needs either a within-patient design or
   the design split (D-051) used to quantify the patient term first. This is the single
   most important number this week produced.
2. **The monotone gap-vs-distance trend is absent** (ρ = +0.31 naive, −0.28 matched,
   §7.3), and D-052 explains why the axis is weak: six of nine operators sit within a
   0.05-wide band because mask-family change saturates the distance. Four of your five
   candidate distances are also one function on masks, so T1.3's "which distance predicts
   best" cannot be settled here at all — it needs Week 7's CT.
3. **Report flag area beside coverage in the figure** (§7.1). Naive transfer hits coverage
   1.000 at three operators by flagging 99% of regions. Coverage alone makes the failure
   invisible; flag area makes it obvious.

Also outstanding: the design split exists now (D-051) and needs a fraction from you, and
the sizing numbers I sent last week were wrong in three places — §4 has the corrections.

**Track 3.** `spectral_divergence` is computable on MRI for the first time (P-28), which
matters for L2 if it wants a decay exponent rather than `null_space_dim`. D-011's warning
about the randomised estimator still stands and is unaffected by the fix.
