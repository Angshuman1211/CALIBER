## Objective

The Week 2 card gives Track 2 three items:

- Integrate a first pretrained solver (DPS/DDNM, diffusion family) as a black-box sampler
- Integrate a second family — flow-matching (FlowDPS) — behind the same interface, to
  establish solver-agnosticism **early, not at ablation stage**
- Begin the operator decomposition, range vs. null-space (Stage 2)

Track 1's own sign-off calls this "the project's critical path" (C2), and it is right to:
until Week 2 every number in the repo came from `NullSpaceNoiseSampler`, which invents
its hallucinations by construction. Nothing had yet been produced by a prior *deciding*
what to put in the null space — which is the entire thing this project audits.

## What I did

### 1. Separated the algorithm from the network (D-029)

The obvious implementation calls `diffusers` inside each sampler. I did not do that. The
three back-ends are written against a two-method `Denoiser` interface, with two
implementations: `DiffusersUNetDenoiser` (the real 434 MB checkpoint) and
`AnalyticDenoiser` (a closed-form Gaussian stand-in).

This was not tidiness. It was forced by P-16: **there is no GPU here.** `torch` is the
`2.13.0+cpu` build, `torch.cuda.is_available()` is `False`, and one UNet forward at 128²
costs ~0.6 s. A test suite that needed the real prior would take tens of minutes and
would therefore not be run. Against the analytic denoiser the whole solver suite —
33 tests including bitwise determinism and data-consistency assertions — runs in
**1.6 seconds**.

It also means swapping the prior when medical weights land (D-016) touches one adapter,
not three samplers.

### 2. DPS, DDNM, and a flow-matching family

**DDNM** replaces the range component of each `x0` estimate outright:
`x0 ← x0 − A⁺(A x0 − y)`. That is *literally* the Stage-2 decomposition the project's
score is built on, which is what makes it the right first back-end — the sampler's
internals and the audit layer speak the same language. It also needs no backward pass,
which on CPU matters enormously.

**DPS** steers with the gradient of the measurement residual instead — a soft constraint.
Having both wired lets the Week 10 ablation separate "the score works" from "the score
works for solvers that happen to enforce consistency the same way our score measures it",
which is a distinction the project cannot afford to leave untested.

**FlowDPS** is the second family, and its scope is limited in a way that must not be
glossed (D-030). There is no public flow-matching checkpoint here and the project trains
nothing, so what is implemented is FlowDPS's *sampling dynamics* — a deterministic
rectified-flow ODE with per-step range replacement — driven by a velocity field derived
from the diffusion checkpoint through the probability-flow correspondence. That is
sufficient for the Week 2 deliverable and the Week 10 ablation, because both need two
families whose **dynamics genuinely differ**: stochastic ancestral sampling against
deterministic ODE integration, with posterior spread arising by different mechanisms.
It is **not** sufficient to claim the project reproduced FlowDPS, and the manuscript must
not say that it did.

### 3. A bug worth recording, because it looked like it worked

The first DPS implementation scaled the update by `step_size / ‖residual‖`, reading the
paper's `ζ = ζ'/‖r‖` literally. It ran, produced images, and had a data-consistency error
of **1.04 — worse than returning zeros.**

The paper's update is `ζ · ∇‖r‖²` with `ζ = ζ'/‖r‖`, and since `∇‖r‖² = 2‖r‖∇‖r‖`, the
`‖r‖` **cancels exactly**. Applying it again makes the correction shrink as the fit
improves, so it can never converge. Corrected, DPS shows the expected clean minimum:

| step size | 0.5 | 2 | **5** | 10 | 20 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| data-consistency error | 0.94 | 0.60 | **0.053** | 0.16 | 0.31 | 0.76 |

The thing that caught it was measuring data consistency rather than looking at outputs —
the same lesson as D-024, which is why `data_consistency_error` is a method on the
sampler and asserted in tests, not a thing checked once by hand.

### 4. Guidance strength is fixed across the grid (D-031)

`DEFAULT_STEP_SIZE = 5.0`, held constant for every operator in every curve. Sec. 13
requires the acquisition operator to be the *only* quantity that varies within a coverage
curve; re-tuning guidance per acceleration would make reconstruction quality depend on
the operator through two paths at once, and the coverage gap would stop being
attributable to the acquisition. That is the same class of confound as D-028, where an
artefact that moved with the operator masqueraded as a finding. The value is not
transferable between priors and must be re-measured — not re-tuned per operator — when
medical weights land.

### 5. Real prior, real k-space, real bank

`configs/experiments/w02_solver_smoke.yaml` runs DDNM with `ddpm-celebahq-256` over

### 6. The decomposition, pointed at real output (Stage 2)

The projectors are Week 1 infrastructure — `P_R = A⁺A` and `P_N = I − P_R` have been in
`operators/base.py` since the operator pipeline landed, and scoring has been calling them
all along. What Week 2's third item asks for is the part that could not be done until a
real solver existed: apply the split to reconstructions **a pretrained prior actually
produced**, and measure where their content, their error and their disagreement land.

`operators/decomposition.py` reports three splits per item, and they answer three
different questions:

| split | what it divides | what it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **energy** | the reconstruction itself | a property of the image and the operator, not of solver quality |
| **error** | the deviation from ground truth | whether the free-component premise holds at all |
| **spread** | disagreement across posterior draws | whether `s_disagree` is measuring what it claims |

The second and third were **assumptions**. `s_free` scores the free component and
`s_disagree` measures spread *in the free component only* — both are only measuring
invented structure if what the solver gets wrong is what the operator could not see.
Nothing checked that. Now something does, and it immediately caught something (P-17):

Null share of the error, at R=4 on the analytic denoiser, four draws:

| solver | 8 steps | 24 | 48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDNM | **0.983** | 0.997 | 0.999 |
| FlowDPS | **0.968** | 0.971 | 0.972 |
| DPS | **0.202** | 0.495 | 0.986 |

DDNM and FlowDPS replace the range component structurally, so the premise holds for them
from the first step. DPS *steers* towards data consistency instead, and below ~48 steps
four fifths of its error is still sitting in the measured component — the sampler has not
fitted the data, and scoring the free component of that output would be scoring the step
count. That is now a stated precondition on any bank (D-033), it is checked by the audit
script, and it is **per solver family**: the two families are not comparable at equal
`n_steps`, which the Week 10 solver ablation has to get right or it will be measuring
convergence rather than solver choice.

Against the real checkpoint on real M4Raw k-space — DDNM at 24 steps, 3 items × 3 draws,
the same config that produced the bank in §5:

| operator | null share of energy | of error | of spread | dc error | orthogonality defect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mri.random1d.R4` | 0.061 | **0.987** | 0.985 | 0.039 | 1.0e-06 |
| `mri.random1d.R8` | 0.083 | **0.997** | 0.996 | 0.023 | 1.6e-06 |

That first column against the second is the Stage-2 result stated in one line: **6% of
the reconstruction's energy lives in the null space, and 99% of its error does.** The
free component is small and it is where essentially everything goes wrong — which is the
premise the whole score is built on, now measured on a real prior rather than assumed.
The spread column says the same for `s_disagree`. Both improve with acceleration, as they
must: a larger null space is more room to invent in.

Two smaller things the same code settles. Every share is normalised by
`‖P_R v‖² + ‖P_N v‖²` and never by `‖v‖²`, with the difference reported as
`orthogonality_defect` — because `P_R` is a genuine orthogonal projection for mask-based
MRI (measured: <1e-6) and only approximately one for CT's CG-based inverse (D-006,
D-012), and a share normalised the other way would quietly absorb that gap and then read
as a finding. And none of it goes into the score bank (D-032): the schema is a
cross-track contract carrying evidence about *items*, and these are diagnostics about the
*pipeline*, constant across every row of a run.

## Honest limitations

**The prior is a face model.** `ddpm-celebahq-256` was trained on CelebA-HQ. Applying it
to knee or brain MRI is D-016's deliberate ordering — wire the pipeline against an open
natural-image prior first, swap in medical weights later — and *not* a claim that the
prior is appropriate. Reconstructions from it exercise the machinery. They are not
reconstructions anyone should look at and judge.

**Nothing here is reportable, on three counts**, and the manifest records all three: a
natural-image prior (D-016), a development substrate (M4Raw, D-015), and a score
combination rule that is still `v0-placeholder` (T1.1 WI-3).

**No GPU (P-16).** This bounds Weeks 3 and 4 more than it bounds Week 2. A full operator
grid at a useful step count is not affordable on CPU. The options are a GPU torch build,
the college machine, or a permanently reduced grid stated openly in Experimental Setup.

**FlowDPS is not FlowDPS (D-030).** Stated above and in the decision log; repeated here
because it is the single most misquotable thing in this week's work.

**Greyscale through an RGB model.** The checkpoint has `in_channels=3`; medical slices
are single-channel, so the image is replicated across three channels and the predicted
noise averaged back. Averaging is the right reduction for a noise estimate, but the model
was never asked to see a greyscale input, and this is an approximation.

**The analytic denoiser tests arithmetic, not quality.** Every automated assertion about
the samplers runs against a Gaussian stand-in that produces smooth blobs. It can prove
determinism, data consistency, and that guidance is connected. It cannot prove a
reconstruction is good — that still needs the real checkpoint and human eyes, and has not
been done systematically.

**The Stage-2 audit says where error lands, not whether it should be there.** A high null
share means the solver's error is in the component the operator cannot see. It does *not*
mean that error is hallucination rather than legitimate prior-driven detail — that
distinction is the ground-truth criterion (O2), it is Track 1's, and it is still open
(D-020). The audit closes a hole underneath the score; it does not close the one above it.

**The per-solver step budget is one operator, one image size, one denoiser.** The table
behind D-033 is 32² at R=4 against the analytic denoiser. The real-prior column is DDNM
only, at one step count, on three items. The *direction* is unambiguous and the DDNM/DPS
gap is far too large to be an artefact of any of those choices, but the number where DPS
becomes admissible is not established, and Week 3 should re-measure it on the UNet before
any DPS bank is produced.

**Three items is not a spread.** The real-prior audit ran on 3 items × 3 draws because a
draw costs ~15 s on this CPU (P-16). The shares are tight across those items and across
two operators, but this is a smoke-scale measurement, not a distribution.

**DPS's step size was tuned on the analytic denoiser**, not on the real prior. The value
5.0 minimises data-consistency error there; the corresponding value for the UNet is
unmeasured.

## How to run it

Every command below was executed before committing.

```bash
pip install -e ".[solvers]"          # diffusers; the core suite does not need it

# The full suite, including 33 solver and 18 decomposition tests, no checkpoint required
python -m pytest -q                   # 165 passed
python -m ruff check src tests scripts

# The real back-end, end to end, on real k-space
caliber configs/experiments/w02_solver_smoke.yaml

# Cheaper smoke while iterating
caliber configs/experiments/w02_solver_smoke.yaml \
  --set run.limit=3 --set experiment.n_posterior=3 --set experiment.solver.n_steps=12

# The Stage-2 audit — run it before producing a bank with a new solver or step count
python scripts/stage2_decomposition.py configs/experiments/w02_solver_smoke.yaml \
  --limit 3 --set experiment.n_posterior=3
```

Swap `name: ddnm` for `dps` or `flowdps` in the config to change back-end. That the only
change needed is one word is the Week 2 deliverable, stated operationally.

The audit reads the same config the runner does, so what it checks is the run that would
actually happen — and it is worth the three minutes before a long job, because a solver
that fails D-033 produces a bank that looks completely normal (P-17). Its JSON lands in
`runs/stage2/`, which is gitignored like every other run artefact; the numbers that matter
are quoted above.

## What this unblocks, and what it leaves open

**Unblocked.** Week 3's `w03_mri_fixed_operator.yaml` now has both halves it was waiting
on — fastMRI data (Week 1) and a real solver (this week). The first reportable MRI score
bank is a compute problem now, not a missing-component problem.

**Left open, owned by Track 2:**

- **P-16** — no GPU. Decide before Week 4 whether the grid shrinks or the hardware changes.
- **D-026** — reference `split_conformal_threshold` over-certifies thin groups; raised by
  Track 1, still open.
- **P-06 (LoDoPaB half)** — patient CSVs, Week 7.
- DPS step size on the real prior — unmeasured.
- CT solver support — the samplers declare `ct` but have only been exercised on MRI, and
  the Stage-2 audit has therefore only been run on MRI operators against a solver. CT's
  orthogonality defect is measured (`test_decomposition.py`) but its error and spread
  splits are not, and cannot be until Week 7 wires a CT solver.
- **D-033's threshold** is `> 0.5` because that is where the statement reverses, not
  because 0.5 was measured to be the right place to stand. The DPS numbers put the real
  boundary somewhere between 24 and 48 steps; nothing pins it more finely, and nothing
  has re-measured it on the UNet rather than the analytic denoiser.

**Owed by others:** the score combination rule (T1.1 WI-3) is still `v0-placeholder`, and
nothing is reportable until it is real. That is now the single largest blocker in front of
a headline number, and it is not Track 2's to fix.
