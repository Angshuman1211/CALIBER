## 1. A ruled-out first hypothesis: population energy-weighted null-space fraction

The natural first guess, matching blueprint §12.1 candidate 5 ("spectral divergence... captures severity of ill-posedness"): define
$$\rho(A) \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_X\big[\|P_{N(A)}X\|^2\big]}{\mathbb{E}_X\big[\|X\|^2\big]},$$
the fraction of a typical image's energy that lands in $A$'s null space — an energy-weighted refinement of raw null-space *dimension*, motivated directly by `s_free`'s definition in `caliber/scoring/score.py` ("energy in $P_N x$"). By Parseval this is exact and cheap to compute for a masked-Fourier operator: $\rho(A) = \sum_{k\notin\text{mask}}\bar S(k) \,/\, \sum_k \bar S(k)$, where $\bar S$ is the population power spectrum.

**Checked against the real operator and the real `PhantomDataset` population** (`scripts/t3_illposedness_energy_check.py`, 200-phantom empirical spectrum, same 128×128 / `center_fraction=0.04` / `mask_seed=1` setup as T3.3's real-score check):

| R | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\Delta\rho / d_{\text{op}}$ | — | 0.0258 | 0.0259 | 0.0270 | 0.0261 | 0.0254 | 0.0250 |

This is nearly flat across the whole sweep (a small hump at $R=6$, ±8% variation around the mean) — nowhere near the ~45% rise-then-plateau T3.3's actual score ratio shows. **Ruled out as the explanation.** The reason, found by reading `compute_score_components` rather than guessing further, is in §2: the current harness's null-space content is not the true image's spectral content at all.

## 2. Why `s_free` and `s_disagree` have an exact closed form in the current harness

`t3_lipschitz_real_score_check.py`'s posterior draws are `base + fill`, with `base = operator.pinv(y_solver)` and `fill = operator.project_null(FILL_NOISE * randn(...))`. Two exact facts about the mask operator (`MRIUndersamplingOperator`, `pinv = adjoint`, both re-applying the boolean mask) settle what `s_free` and `s_disagree` actually measure here:

- **`project_null(base) = 0` exactly.** `fft(base) = fft(pinv(y)) = y_{\text{solver}}\cdot\text{mask}\cdot\text{mask} = y_{\text{solver}}\cdot\text{mask}$ (mask is idempotent), so `base`'s spectrum is supported entirely *inside* the mask — none of its energy is in the null space.
- Since `fill` is already null-restricted by construction, `project_null(fill) = fill` (idempotent).

So `free = |mean_i(fill_i)|` and `spread = std_i(|fill_i|)` — **`s_free` and `s_disagree` are computed entirely from independent, injected Gaussian white noise restricted to the null space; they carry no dependence on the true image's content at all**, in this harness. This is not a flaw in the harness (documented elsewhere as a placeholder for a real posterior sampler, ahead of Track 2's pretrained solvers landing in this path) — it is a fact worth stating precisely, because it makes a closed-form prediction available: by Parseval (both FFTs are `norm="ortho"`), the per-pixel variance of null-restricted white noise is proportional to the null-space *fraction*, so

$$s_{\text{free}}(A),\ s_{\text{disagree}}(A) \;\propto\; \sqrt{\text{null\_frac}(A)} \;=\; \sqrt{\text{null\_space\_dim}(A)/\text{ambient\_dim}},$$

up to one fixed constant (`FILL_NOISE`, `N_DRAWS`, region size), independent of which image is being scored.

**Checked, not assumed** (`scripts/t3_illposedness_component_check.py`, same harness, population and sweep as T3.3): raw component values divided by $\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$, normalised to $R=2$:

| R | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $s_{\text{free}}/\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ (norm.) | 1.000 | 1.002 | 1.006 | 1.013 | 1.014 | 1.016 | 1.018 |
| $s_{\text{disagree}}/\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ (norm.) | 1.000 | 1.035 | 1.071 | 1.130 | 1.129 | 1.161 | 1.182 |
| $s_{\text{residual}}/\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ (norm.) | 1.000 | 0.713 | 0.581 | 0.449 | 0.383 | 0.312 | 0.263 |

$s_{\text{free}}$ matches the $\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ law almost exactly (within 2% across the entire tested range) — a clean, closed-form, fully derivable ill-posedness scaling for one of the three score components, not an empirical fit. $s_{\text{disagree}}$ matches to within ~18% (a noisier estimator — it is a standard deviation over only $N_{\text{DRAWS}}=8$ samples — but clearly the same underlying law, not a different one). $s_{\text{residual}}$ **does not** follow it, and moves in the opposite direction: `residual` and its `adjoint` back-projection both re-apply the mask, so it reflects genuine in-mask measurement-noise consistency, not null-space content, and its raw magnitude actually *shrinks* as $R$ grows (0.01404 → 0.00505 over the sweep) — expected, and a useful confirmation that the closed form is specific to the two null-space-driven components, not universal.

## 3. The rise-then-plateau is not inherited from any single component — it is a cancellation effect

None of the three components' own standardised movement ratios $\mathbb{E}[|\Delta s_i|]/d_{\text{op}}$ rises then plateaus over the tested range — **all three increase monotonically** through $R=16$:

| R | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ratio, free | 0.375 | 0.454 | 0.523 | 0.546 | 0.569 | 0.582 |
| ratio, disagree | 0.467 | 0.611 | 0.779 | 0.790 | 0.867 | 0.917 |
| ratio, residual | 0.430 | 0.564 | 0.708 | 0.787 | 0.879 | 0.946 |
| **naive sum / 3** (no cancellation) | 0.424 | 0.543 | 0.670 | 0.708 | 0.772 | 0.815 |
| **actual combined ratio** (T3.3's quantity) | 0.137 | 0.167 | 0.198 | 0.183 | 0.186 | 0.184 |

("naive sum / 3" is $\mathbb{E}[|\Delta s_{\text{free}}|/\text{scale}_{\text{free}}] + \mathbb{E}[|\Delta s_{\text{disagree}}|/\text{scale}_{\text{disagree}}] + \mathbb{E}[|\Delta s_{\text{residual}}|/\text{scale}_{\text{residual}}]$, averaged by the equal-thirds weights — what the combined ratio would equal if the three components' per-trial errors never cancelled. "Actual combined ratio" is $\mathbb{E}[|\Delta\text{score}|]/d_{\text{op}}$, computed on the *same* paired trials, reproducing T3.3's table to within simulation noise: T3.3 reported 0.137, 0.167, 0.198, 0.183, 0.186, 0.184 for the same $R$ values.)

**The gap between these two rows is the whole story.** The combination is affine with non-negative weights (D25) — non-negativity keeps the *combined score itself* monotone in each component, but it does not stop the per-trial *deltas* $(s_{\text{dep}} - s_{\text{cal}})$ of the three components from having different signs on a given trial, since a component can be higher or lower at deployment than at calibration depending on the random draw. By the triangle inequality, $\mathbb{E}|\Delta s_{\text{free}}+\Delta s_{\text{disagree}}+\Delta s_{\text{residual}}| \leq \mathbb{E}|\Delta s_{\text{free}}| + \mathbb{E}|\Delta s_{\text{disagree}}| + \mathbb{E}|\Delta s_{\text{residual}}|$, and the table shows this inequality is far from tight: the gap **widens** as $R$ grows (naive-sum/actual-combined ratio goes from $\approx$1.2$\times$ at $R{=}3$ to $\approx$4.4$\times$ at $R{=}16$). More null-space dimension does not just make each component move more (§2) — it makes their movements decorrelate more, and the combined score's actual sensitivity to $d_{\text{op}}$ is set by *how much cancellation survives*, not by any single component's own scaling.

$$\boxed{\ \text{The rise-then-plateau in T3.3's ratio is a growing-cancellation effect between components, not a property derivable from any one component's ill-posedness scaling.}\ }$$

This also explains *why* the plateau sets in specifically once $s_{\text{residual}}$'s contribution has shrunk enough (§2: it falls to 26% of its naively-predicted scale by $R=16$, while raw $s_{\text{free}}$/$s_{\text{disagree}}$ keep climbing) — past that point the combined ratio's shape is increasingly a two-component (free, disagree) story, and both of those already satisfy the same clean $\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ law (§2), so their *joint* cancellation pattern stabilising is what stabilises the plateau.

## 4. What this hands to the coverage-gap bound

T3.3 §3 needs a single Lipschitz constant $L$ (or a locally valid one, over the tested range) relating score movement to $d_{\text{op}}$. This task's finding sharpens what $L$ actually is here: not a fixed structural property of the score's design, but **the residual, post-cancellation sensitivity of an affine combination of components with different — and ill-posedness-dependent — correlation structure.** For the two null-space-driven components (free, disagree), the *individual* scaling with ill-posedness is now closed-form (§2, $\propto\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$), so if a future pass derives the cross-component correlation's dependence on null-space dimension, $L$ becomes fully predictable rather than empirically fitted. That correlation derivation is not attempted here — flagged forward (§5).

## 5. Open items handed forward

- **Not resolved here — the natural next step:** derive (rather than only measure) how the correlation between $\Delta s_{\text{free}}$, $\Delta s_{\text{disagree}}$, $\Delta s_{\text{residual}}$ depends on null-space dimension, which would turn §3's empirical cancellation-growth finding into a closed form for $L$ itself. This is a tractable target (the two dominant components already have exact per-component laws from §2) but is genuinely new derivation, not a numerical extension — a candidate for a further T3.4 pass or for T3.7's write-up to scope explicitly as future work rather than silently skip.
- **To T3.5, sharpened again:** does the $\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ law for `s_free`/`s_disagree` (§2) and the growing-cancellation effect (§3) still hold for $R>16$, other mask families (`equispaced1d`, `variable_density2d`), and real fastMRI data — same open range T3.3 already flagged, now with two more specific closed-form predictions to test rather than just the combined ratio.
- **The most important caveat, stated plainly:** §2's closed form is a property of the *current placeholder harness* (i.i.d. Gaussian null-space fill), not of the score design in general. Once Track 2's pretrained diffusion/flow-matching solvers fill the null space with **learned, image-correlated structure** instead of white noise, `s_free` and `s_disagree` will almost certainly stop following $\sqrt{\text{null\_frac}}$ cleanly — because that departure is exactly what "the prior is inventing structure rather than reporting noise" (the score's entire premise, per its module docstring) would look like numerically. This derivation is therefore best read as the **null hypothesis** a real solver's behaviour should be checked against, not a fact expected to survive T2.2's real solvers landing in this path.
- **Not attempted in this pass:** the density-difference constant $D$ from T3.3 §3(iii) (the TV/$W_1$ conversion). This task focused entirely on $L$, since T3.3 §6 named the rise-then-plateau shape as the load-bearing open item; $D$ remains untouched and should not be assumed resolved by this document.
