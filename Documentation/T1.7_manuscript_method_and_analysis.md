## 0. What is claimed, and what is not

Written first because it is the part most likely to be over-reached in drafting, and because
the Phase 2 gate settled it (D67).

**Claimed.** For region-level conformal hallucination flags over generative-prior
reconstruction:

1. A threshold calibrated at one acquisition operator and deployed at another **loses its
   coverage guarantee**, and the loss is measurable with intervals in two modalities whose
   operators are structurally different.
2. **Group-conditional (Mondrian) calibration restores nominal coverage** at every operator
   in both modalities, at a stated cost in flagged area and region FDR.
3. On CT, where the forward operator is not diagonal, the coverage gap is **ordered by a
   directional operator distance** computable from the two operators alone.

**Not claimed, and each for a specific measured reason.**

- **That the operator-aware score detects hallucinations better than a simpler baseline.**
  Posterior dispersion ranks hallucinations within 0.001–0.012 AUROC of `s_disagree` at
  every operator, because the solver used (DDNM) replaces the range component structurally
  and has already confined the posterior spread to the null space. The decomposition cannot
  contribute on such a solver, and the experiment that could test it (DPS) is outstanding
  (D68).
- **Any detection quality at all, as a result.** The criterion is approximately a function
  of the score under this prior: `err_free ≈ √(s_free² + free_content²)` to Spearman 0.99,
  which is the identity of §2.4 at `cos θ ≈ 0`. Reported detection figures measure the
  identity (D67).
- **Exact finite-sample coverage under arbitrary operator change.** Operator shift alters
  the measurement process, so it is not a covariate shift and no exact guarantee is claimed
  outside the discrete known-operator regime (D3, D6).
- **Clinical validity of the criterion.** It is ours, stated in the project's own terms. The
  one published alternative (Tivnan 2024) disagrees with it at κ ≈ 0 and mostly flags
  background, so there is **no external validation of the label** — a scope limit, not a gap
  to be filled quietly.
- **Anything from the current banks as a number.** The prior is a natural-image model used
  on medical anatomy (D-016). Every figure is development output until medical weights land.

## 1. Problem setting

A reconstruction method recovers `x` from `y = A x + ε` with `A` a **known** linear forward
operator. Known is not a convenience: it is true for retrospective MRI undersampling and for
CT projection geometry, where the sampling pattern is chosen rather than inferred, and the
method does not apply where `A` must be estimated.

The audit question is **not** whether `x̂` is accurate but whether a *region* of `x̂`
contains structure the prior invented. The deployment setting supplies `A`, `y` and `x̂`, and
never `x`.

**Operator shift** is a change of `A` between calibration and deployment: a different
undersampling mask, a different view count, a narrower angular arc. It is the quantity under
study, and §2.4 is the reason it cannot be treated as a covariate shift.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Regions

Flags are issued on a non-overlapping 16-pixel patch grid, identical in both modalities.
Anatomical annotations are an evaluation *overlay*, never the region definition: MRI lesion
boxes and CT organ masks are not commensurable objects, and hallucination lives in the null
space, which does not respect organ boundaries. Equal-area regions also keep every
region-level rate a plain proportion rather than an area-weighted one.

### 2.2 The range / null-space decomposition

With `P_R = A⁺A` and `P_N = I − P_R`, any reconstruction splits as `x̂ = P_R x̂ + P_N x̂`.
The measured component is pinned by the data; the free component is what the operator could
not see, and is where invented structure must live. For Cartesian MRI this is exact and
closed-form; for CT the pseudo-inverse is filtered backprojection, so `P_R` is approximate
and operators carry that flag explicitly.

Measured on real-prior output: **6% of a reconstruction's energy lies in the null space and
99% of its error does.** That is the premise the score rests on, and it is measured rather
than assumed.

### 2.3 The score

Three per-region components, computed from `x̂` and `y` only — never from `x` — and stored
separately so the combination can be revised without re-running reconstruction:

| component | quantity |
|---|---|
| `s_free` | RMS of `‖P_N x̂‖` over the region, normalised by a measurement-derived reference |
| `s_disagree` | cross-sample spread of the free component across posterior draws |
| `s_residual` | backprojected measurement residual |

Combined by a **standardised non-negative affine rule** with weights on the simplex and
scales estimated once on the calibration operator and then frozen. Both choices are
load-bearing. Unstandardised summation reduces the score to whichever component is largest.
Re-estimating scales per deployment operator would map each operator's score distribution
onto a common spread — which is precisely the difference the experiment exists to measure —
and it would also break the per-component reduction the stability argument depends on.

**`s_residual` carries no weight.** It is structurally near-zero for a solver that enforces
data consistency, and where it is not, it measures how far from convergence the sampler
stopped — a solver property, already used as the admissibility gate on a bank. Using one
quantity as both the gate and as evidence about a region double-counts it.

**The remaining weights are equal, by default rather than by selection.** On a design split
of eight patients the objective is flat: more than half the sweep sits within one standard
error of the minimum, so the selection has no information and returns the stated default
rather than the boundary of its own search. The cost of that default against the unknown
optimum is bounded at 0.015 of flagged area, measured.

### 2.4 An exact identity, and what it implies

All three free-component quantities in the cached table share one normalisation and are
region-wise reductions of the same two fields. Writing `a = P_N x̂` and `b = P_N x` over a
region, `s_free = ‖a‖`, `free_content = ‖b‖`, `err_free = ‖a − b‖`, and therefore **exactly**

> `err_free² = s_free² + free_content² − 2 · s_free · free_content · cos θ`

verified at zero relative residual across 4,608 regions. Three consequences carry through
the whole paper:

1. **`cos θ` is recoverable per region** — how much of the reconstruction's hidden content
   agrees with the truth's. `cos θ → 1` is recovered anatomy; `cos θ → 0` is invention.
   Measured median 0.073 under a mismatched prior, which is why §0 declines detection
   claims: at `cos θ ≈ 0`, `err_free → s_free` and any criterion built on `err_free` is
   approximately the score.
2. **The coverage gap is not symmetric in the operator pair.** The same two operators in the
   two directions give gaps of opposite sign with disjoint intervals, so the plotted axis
   must be directional (§2.6).
3. **A ratio criterion is denominator-dominated.** `err_free / free_content` is predicted
   better by its denominator than by its numerator, because both scale with anatomical
   density.

### 2.5 The ground-truth criterion

A region is labelled hallucinated when **`err_free > ε`**, with `ε` a quantile of `err_free`
on each substrate's **own design split**, frozen before use. The design split is a third
side of the patient-level partition, carved from the calibration range so the test side never
moves; selecting on calibration rows biases the conformal threshold and selecting on test
rows biases the coverage number, and both fail silently.

`ε` is a quantile and not a constant because an absolute error threshold transfers across
neither modalities nor substrates — a fixed value flagged every region on one real bank. A
quantile cannot do that by construction.

**The anatomy confound is handled in the analysis, not in the label** (§3.3). Four
successive attempts to make a function of `err_free` and `free_content` anatomy-neutral
within an operator failed, each in a measurable way: a ratio carries a low-anatomy bias
(AUROC 0.90–0.94), the absolute form a high-anatomy one (0.05–0.42), a conjunctive floor
amplifies the circularity of §2.4, and a candidacy gate reduces the bias without removing it
(0.66). No such function exists, because both quantities scale with anatomical density.
Encoding the correction in the outcome definition makes the *label* depend on the confound;
stratifying in the analysis leaves the label a plain statement about error magnitude.

### 2.6 Operator distance

Two measures, for two purposes, and the distinction is deliberate.

**The proved distance** is the Frobenius null-space projector distance, normalised by the
ambient dimension — a metric, computable from the two operators alone, defined identically
for a k-space mask and a projection-angle set. On Cartesian MRI it reduces to the square
root of the fraction of k-space lines whose sampled status changed.

**The plotted axis** is a *signed* net null-space growth: the fraction of the ambient space
newly hidden minus the fraction newly revealed, with a signed square root. It reproduces the
metric exactly up to sign on nested masks, and `|growth| ≤ metric` always, so a bound proved
against the metric still bounds a gap plotted against the signed axis. It is not a metric and
is not used as one.

**On MRI the candidate measures degenerate.** Four of the five coincide to 1.1e-16, because a
mask operator is diagonal in k-space, so "which frequencies it keeps" *is* its range. They
differ on CT, where the projection is not diagonal — which is itself evidence that the two
modalities exercise structurally different operator classes, and is why the distance-ordering
claim of §0 is stated for CT.

### 2.7 Calibration

Three tools over the cached table, plus one fallback:

| tool | what it does |
|---|---|
| **naive transferred** | one threshold, fitted at one operator, applied everywhere — *the failure the paper exposes, not a method* |
| **matched per operator** | fitted on the deployment operator's own calibration rows — the oracle upper bound, unavailable in deployment |
| **group-conditional** | one threshold per acquisition regime — the guaranteed backbone |
| **distance-weighted** | every calibration region contributes, weighted by operator distance — borrows across regimes, approximate |
| **group-weighted (fallback)** | per-group weights with hard truncation, retaining the nearest groups that still certify |

Thresholds use the floor convention and **refuse** a group with too few positives to certify
at the target level rather than returning one that over-promises. An uncalibrated group is
flagged wholesale, which costs efficiency and never validity.

## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 The estimand

Three contrasts, in increasing precision, all reported:

| contrast | varies | cancels | answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| coverage at one operator vs nominal | — | nothing | does the certificate hold here? |
| coverage at deployment minus at calibration | the operator | the patient | how does reliability move with the operator? |
| coverage under two calibrations at one operator | the threshold | patient **and** operator | does the remedy work? |

The design is fully crossed — every operator is applied to the same volume — so the second
and third are **within-patient paired** differences and the patient's own difficulty cancels.
The crossing also means the patient budget is not divided across operators: adding operators
costs compute, not precision.

Intervals bootstrap over **patients**, never regions; resampling regions would treat
thousands of correlated observations as independent.

### 3.2 The noise floor

Reported before any effect, because it is what every effect is measured against. The floor is
the **patient split**, not the mask seed: coverage standard deviation across seeds is 0.007
and 0.001 at two operators, against 0.034 and 0.048 across patient splits. **No effect below
≈0.05 coverage is an effect**, and that bar is printed beside every ablation table.

### 3.3 Detection, reported stratified

Every detection figure is reported per operator and within anatomy terciles, with the
artefact's own score — how well anatomical density alone separates the label, in whichever
direction it runs — in the same cell. A detection figure that does not clear its cell's
artefact score is not evidence of detection.

**Never pooled across operators.** A pooled statistic can average two opposite per-operator
biases into an apparent neutrality: one candidate label scored 0.499 pooled, which is exactly
chance, and 0.662 within one operator.

### 3.4 Figures

Drawn by `scripts/make_figures.py`, all five from the current label. Named as the artefacts
are named, so a draft cites something that exists.

| artefact | content | gate before it may be quoted |

The split presentation of the MRI distance figure is not cosmetic. The gap is ordered by
distance on CT (ρ = +0.976) and barely on MRI (+0.316), because MRI's worst failures sit at
matched acceleration across mask families, where the scalar distance is ≈ 0. A single MRI
curve would be a line fitted through two clusters.

### 3.5 The curve-collapse test (§21 R5) — Track 1's, and the data already constrains it

The cross-domain claim's strongest form is that the two modalities' coverage-gap curves
**collapse onto one curve** when plotted against operator distance: if they do, the gap is a
function of how far the operator moved and not of the modality, which is what "the failure is
a property of the acquisition in general" would mean.

`fig5` is the test, and the evidence so far says the strong form fails: the gap is ordered by
distance on CT and not on MRI, so there is no single curve for the two to collapse onto. The
honest version of the claim is therefore weaker and still worth stating — **the same failure
and the same fix appear in both modalities**, with the *ordering by distance* demonstrated
where the distance is a genuine scalar. The write-up should make that distinction rather than
assert collapse, and §0 is worded accordingly.

## 4. Threats to validity, as a reviewer would list them

1. **The prior is not a medical prior.** Gates every number, and now also the criterion's
   validity: at `cos θ ≈ 0` the label is approximately the score.
2. **No external validation of the criterion.** The one published alternative disagrees at
   κ ≈ 0.
3. **The criterion has a usable band, not a fix.** At low fill ratio it is an anatomy meter,
   at high fill ratio a mirror of the score; only the middle is interpretable, and the band
   must be reported per operator.
4. **96 patients.** Per-operator coverage is capped near ±0.12 whatever the grid; the paired
   contrasts are what the design supports.
5. **One solver family carries the results.** DDNM structurally confines spread to the null
   space, which is exactly why the decomposition cannot be tested on it.
6. **Volume, not patient, is the clustering unit** on MRI, so intervals are optimistic by
   roughly the volumes-per-patient factor.
7. **The CT forward operator is ours**, not a scanner's, and its pseudo-inverse is
   approximate.
8. **Single-coil MRI** via root-sum-of-squares reduction.
