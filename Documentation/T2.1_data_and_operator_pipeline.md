### 2. `singlecoil_test` was downloaded, and cannot be used

Checked rather than assumed, because "we have the test split" is the kind of thing that
quietly becomes a plan:

```
keys:  ['ismrmrd_header', 'kspace', 'mask']
attrs: {'acceleration': 8, 'num_low_frequency': 15, ...}
```

Two independent disqualifications. The k-space is **already undersampled** at a fixed
R=8 and ships the mask it was decimated with, so an operator grid cannot be synthesised
from it — the operator is the variable under study and has to be *chosen* and applied to
fully-sampled k-space. And there is no `reconstruction_esc`, so there is no ground truth,
hence no `err_total`, no `err_free`, and no criterion to evaluate. It is the challenge
submission set. fastMRI+ does not annotate it either.

`singlecoil_train` (72.7 GB) was deliberately not downloaded. Nothing needs it: no config
references it, and the project trains nothing (pretrained solvers only, §17). Not
downloading it also removes the D-014 hazard of train and val landing in one recursive
index.

**So `singlecoil_val` is the entire reported MRI substrate.** That is not a footnote — it
sets the ceiling on how thin `calib_group` can be sliced before groups starve, and it is
what makes the next finding load-bearing rather than tidy. Recorded as P-13.

### 3. The split rule was wrong, and the data said so

D-014 fixed the rule as *"split by patient for LoDoPaB, **by volume for fastMRI**, never
by slice"* — on the assumption that a fastMRI volume is a patient.

It is not. Reading `patient_id` from all 199 volumes:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| volumes | 199 |
| distinct `patient_id` | **96** |
| patients owning more than one volume | **96 of 96** |

Every patient in the split owns more than one volume, so splitting by filename leaks
**45 of 96 patients (47%)** across the calibration/test boundary. That is precisely the
hazard D-014 was written to prevent, occurring in the modality D-014 declared safe, and
it fails in the direction that flatters our own numbers: a patient seen during
calibration makes the test set easier, so coverage looks better than it is.

`caliber.data.splits` now owns the split. It is a pure function of
`(salt, patient_key)`, which buys four properties that a shuffled list does not:

- **patient-level** — assignment keys on `patient_id`, never a filename or slice
- **operator-independent** (D-022's binding requirement) — there is no operator argument
  to vary, so a coverage difference between R=4 and R=8 cannot be confounded with anatomy
- **identical across runs and machines** — nothing is stored, so nothing can drift
- **stable as data grows** — adding volumes moves nobody, so today's bank stays
  comparable with one built after the substrate grows

`verify_no_patient_straddles` is the assertion, and `split_summary` prints the counts
before a bank is produced. Recorded as D-027; P-06 updated.

### 5. D-024, and the result it moved

Track 1 found that `solvers/reference.py` cast `P_N(texture)` with `.real` before adding
it to the draw. A random 1-D Cartesian mask is not conjugate-symmetric, so `P_N` of a
real image genuinely has an imaginary part, and discarding it put part of the
perturbation back into the *measured* component — so the draws were not data-consistent
and `s_residual` was non-zero as a pure artefact.

Fixed in both reference samplers: the draw stays in the operator's own complex domain and
magnitudes are taken only where something is measured.

**This changed a result, not just a number**, which is the part worth flagging. Measured
before and after, same operators and seeds:

| | `s_free` R=2 | `s_free` R=10 | `s_residual` |
|---|---|---|---|
| before (`.real`) | 0.0827 | 0.0448 | 0.082 / 0.032 |
| after (complex) | 0.0326 | 0.0377 | **0.000000** |

Before, the artefact dominated the score *and was larger at the near operator*, so the
score fell as acceleration rose and a threshold calibrated at R=2 under-covered at R=10.
After, `s_free` rises with acceleration — the physically correct direction, since a larger
null space holds more free content — and naive transfer **over**-covers instead:

| calibration | deployment | coverage | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| R=2 | R=2 | 0.947 | −0.047 |
| R=2 | R=10 | **1.000** | −0.100 |
| Mondrian | R=10 | 0.914 | −0.014 |

Over-coverage is not the safe side here. Coverage 1.0 means the transferred threshold
flags *every* region, so the flag carries no information — the same vacuity D-020 records
for the absolute label threshold, arrived at by a different route.

What survives: naive transfer is materially miscalibrated under operator shift, and the
group-conditional backbone restores it (0.914 against a nominal 0.90). The test now
asserts the *magnitude* of the gap and the saturation rather than a direction the
synthetic sampler does not produce. Recorded as D-028.

### 0. What is blocked, and what is not

**fastMRI is the only true blocker.** It requires a signed Data Sharing
Agreement plus an approved application naming your institution and intended
use — a legal agreement between you and NYU that cannot be scripted, and whose
terms forbid the third-party mirrors that would otherwise route around it.

Consequences, stated plainly:

- Reported MRI results need fastMRI. There is no substitute for the
  annotation-linked evaluation, because no open raw-k-space dataset ships
  pathology annotations.
- Everything *else* can proceed now. M4Raw (D-015) supplies open, fully-sampled
  raw k-space in fastMRI's own `.h5` layout, which is all retrospective
  undersampling needs. Solver wiring, score-bank production, calibration, and
  the coverage-vs-distance machinery can all be built and tested against it.

Apply at <https://fastmri.med.nyu.edu/> before anything else this week.

### 1. fastMRI — primary empirical substrate

**Why raw measurements are non-negotiable.** The acquisition operator is the
variable under study. It must be *chosen*, not inherited. Fully-sampled k-space
lets us synthesise the whole operator grid retrospectively, which is also what
makes the operator known at both calibration and deployment — the assumption
the exact guarantee rests on (Sec. 13). A dataset of finished images cannot
support this project at all.

**Access.** Apply at <https://fastmri.med.nyu.edu/>. Approval is not instant;
treat it as the long pole. You receive time-limited download links by email.

**What to fetch first.** `knee_singlecoil_val` (~7 GB). Single-coil avoids coil
sensitivity estimation entirely, and the validation split is small enough to
develop against while the training split downloads. Knee is the split fastMRI+
annotates most densely.

```
data/raw/fastmri/singlecoil_val/*.h5
```

Each file holds `kspace` of shape `(slices, H, W)` complex, plus a
`reconstruction_esc` reference.

**Landing the files.** The loader searches recursively, so extra nesting from
staging the archive elsewhere is harmless — `singlecoil_val/singlecoil_val/`
reads the same as `singlecoil_val/`. Two things are not harmless:

- **Keep the splits in separate directories.** Configs point at
  `data/raw/fastmri/singlecoil_val`, not at `data/raw/fastmri`, precisely so a
  recursive search cannot sweep train and val into one index and put the same
  volume on both sides of a calibration/test split. That is D-014 in its
  fastMRI form.
- **A redistribution shipping only `reconstruction_*` images is useless here.**
  The loader raises rather than falling back, because the operator has to be
  applied to fully-sampled k-space; it cannot be inferred from a finished
  image.

**Preprocessing** (`caliber.data.fastmri`): `ifft2` → centre crop to the working
resolution → magnitude → 99th-percentile normalisation. Multi-coil volumes are
reduced by root-sum-of-squares.

> The studied operator is therefore **single-coil Cartesian undersampling**.
> That is a real narrowing of scope and belongs in the Experimental Setup
> section, not left implicit.

#### 1a. M4Raw — the open stopgap (D-015)

Open raw k-space in fastMRI's own `.h5` layout, so the fastMRI reader runs
unchanged. It fills the development gap while fastMRI access is pending, and
nothing more: **0.3 T brain rather than 3 T knee, and no pathology
annotations**, so it cannot carry any annotation-linked evaluation. It is wired
as the `m4raw` dataset and marked unreportable in code, so a bank built from it
announces itself as such (D-018).

Each volume ships `kspace` of shape `(18, 4, 256, 256)` complex64 plus

### 2. fastMRI+ — pathology annotations

Public: <https://github.com/microsoft/fastmri-plus>. Bounding boxes per slice
with columns `file, slice, study_level, x, y, width, height, label`.

```
data/annotations/fastmri_plus/knee.csv     # 16,154 boxes across 969 files
data/annotations/fastmri_plus/brain.csv
```

These provide the region-level labels that let a flag be judged on whether it
fires **where it matters** (Sec. 14) rather than merely somewhere.

**The boxes are drawn on fastMRI's 320x320 `reconstruction_esc` grid, not on
k-space.** Knee k-space inverts to 640x368, so rasterising there misplaces
every box — silently, because a misplaced mask is still a valid mask. The
loader crops the image to the annotation grid first and then applies one
identical crop to both. See **D-019**, which has the measurements.

One consequence worth knowing before setting the working resolution: at
256x256 every box survives the centre crop; at 128x128 only 76.7% do.

### 3. LoDoPaB-CT — cross-domain substrate

Open on Zenodo: <https://zenodo.org/records/3384092>. No approval needed, so it
can be fetched any time before Week 7.

```
data/raw/lodopab/ground_truth_*.hdf5
```

**We use the ground-truth images and re-project them with our own registered
operator, not LoDoPaB's supplied sinograms.** Using the shipped sinograms would
fix the acquisition at LoDoPaB's geometry and make the operator grid
impossible. The cost is that the CT forward model is ours, not the scanner's —
which belongs in Assumptions and Honest Scope.

**Patient IDs are shipped separately and matter for split integrity.**
`patient_ids_rand_*.csv` gives one patient ID per slice, in slice order.
LoDoPaB slices come from patients contributing many slices each, so a random
slice-level calibration/test split puts the same patient on both sides. That
inflates apparent coverage and quietly violates the exchangeability the
conformal guarantee rests on. Split by patient, never by slice — D-014, and
D-022 for the operator-independence requirement on top of it.

### 4. CT anatomical annotations

TotalSegmentator [31] (open, ~100 structures) or AbdomenCT-1K [32]
(registration). Either supplies the region-level ground truth that makes CT
flag evaluation mirror MRI.

Labels are supplied per volume and must be resampled onto the same
centre-cropped grid as the image. An off-by-one here mislabels every CT region
without any visible symptom, so this needs its own verification step when
wired (Week 7).

**TotalSegmentator's masks segment TotalSegmentator's own CT volumes, not
LoDoPaB's.** They are different cohorts — LoDoPaB derives from LIDC-IDRI — so
LoDoPaB slices cannot be annotated with them, and the two cannot simply be
paired. This is what D-013 resolves; each TotalSegmentator subject ships
`ct.nii.gz` plus 117 aligned organ masks, which is the same image-plus-region
pairing MRI gets from fastMRI+.

## Appendix B — Operator registry specification (shared interface)

*Was `operator_registry_spec.md`. A shared interface under Blueprint Sec. 19: one
definition of each operator and of the distance between operators, used identically in
MRI, CT, and Track 3's derivations. Track 1 and Track 3 both consume this — it is a
cross-track contract, not a Track 2 implementation note.*

**Status:** draft for Week 1 sign-off by all three tracks
**Owner:** Track 2 (T2.1) with Track 1 (T1.3) and Track 3 (T3.2)

Blueprint references: Sec. 19 (shared interfaces), Sec. 12.1 (distance
candidates), Week 9 card (consistency audit).

---

### 1. Why this exists

Blueprint Sec. 19 requires **one definition of each acquisition operator and of
the distance between operators, used identically in both modalities and in the
derivations.** The Week 9 card turns that into an explicit audit: confirm that
the same registered operator representation and distance measure are used
consistently across MRI, CT, and the theory (T1.3 / T3.2).

That audit is only passable if there is a single place operators are defined.
This is that place. All operator construction goes through
`caliber.operators.registry`, and nothing constructs an operator inline.

### 2. What an operator must provide

Every operator implements `ForwardOperator`:

| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `forward(x)` | `A x` — image to measurements |
| `adjoint(y)` | `A^H y` — the exact transpose |
| `pinv(y)` | `A^+ y` — exact where possible, best available otherwise; the *display* reconstruction |
| `decomposition_pinv(y)` | The inverse the decomposition below is built from. Defaults to `pinv`; overridden when `pinv` is not a genuine pseudo-inverse and a better one exists for this purpose specifically (CT, D-012) |
| `descriptor()` | Data-free summary registered here |

and inherits the decomposition of Sec. 2.3, built from `decomposition_pinv`,
**not** `pinv`, so that a display-only approximation (CT's FBP) never leaks
into `project_range` / `project_null`:

```
project_range(x) = decomposition_pinv(A x)          the measured component
project_null(x)  = x - project_range(x)             the free component
```

### 3. The descriptor

`OperatorDescriptor` is the **data-free** summary. Track 3 computes distances
from descriptors and never touches a dataset or a GPU.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `operator_id` | Stable, human-readable primary key |
| `modality`, `family` | `mri`/`ct`; mask or geometry family |
| `params` | Every parameter needed to rebuild the operator exactly |
| `image_shape`, `ambient_dim`, `measurement_dim` | Dimensions |
| `null_space_dim` | Size of the unmeasured subspace |
| `exact_pinv` | Whether `pinv(y)`, the display reconstruction, is the exact Moore-Penrose inverse — also gates the idempotence health check, unchanged by D-012 |
| `sampling_support` | MRI: k-space mask. CT: angle set |
| `singular_values` | Optional spectral summary |

#### Operator ids

```
mri.<family>.R<acceleration>.c<center_fraction>.s<mask_seed>
ct.<family>.v<n_views>.a<angular_range>.d<dose>
```

Stable across runs and machines, and tested as such. An id is the join key in
the score bank, so it must never change meaning.

### 4. Two honest caveats, carried in the descriptor

These are recorded as data, not as comments, because they must reach the
write-up and the Assumptions and Honest Scope section (Sec. 13).

**`exact_pinv`.** For mask-based MRI, `M` is an orthogonal projection and `F`
is unitary, so `A^+ = F^H M` exactly and `P_R`, `P_N` are true orthogonal
projections; `exact_pinv` is `True`. For CT, `pinv` is filtered
backprojection, which is *not* the Moore-Penrose inverse — `exact_pinv` is
`False`, and any claim resting on the exactness of the *display*
reconstruction, or on idempotence of the decomposition, must be qualified for
CT.

**This is deliberately unchanged by D-012.** D-012 fixed CT's decomposition
being built from FBP — `decomposition_pinv` now uses conjugate gradient on the
normal equations (`CTProjectionOperator.pinv_cg`) instead, which reliably
fixes the *contraction* property (`null_projection_gain() <= 1`, verified
across the Sec. 16 operator grid, often from as few as 5-10 CG iterations, and
now a hard gate — see `contraction_ok` below). It does not fix idempotence:
CG is iterative and only approximately idempotent at any budget cheap enough
to run per item, and — because applying an incompletely-converged solve twice
compounds error in a way that doesn't simply shrink with more iterations of
the first solve — pushing further towards convergence is not the free lunch
it looks like, and past a point is not even safe (`pinv_cg`'s docstring has
the numerical-divergence story). So `exact_pinv` correctly stays `False` for
CT, and the idempotence gate stays bypassed for it, exactly as before D-012.

**`null_space_dim` for CT is a bound, not a rank.** It is the dimension count
`ambient - measurements`, recorded in `params` as
`null_space_dim_method: dimension_count`. The true rank needs a spectral
estimate (`caliber.operators.spectrum.estimate_spectrum`). This matters
directly for L2, which scales the bound with null-space dimension: L2 must
either use the estimated rank or state that it uses the bound.

### 5. Health checks — a gate, not a nicety

`OperatorRegistry.check_health()` runs before any score-bank production job and
the runner aborts on failure:

- **Adjoint test.** `<A x, v> == <x, A^H v>` to a relative tolerance.
- **Idempotence.** `P_R(P_R x) == P_R x`, required only where `exact_pinv` —
  MRI only; unchanged by D-012 (see §4).
- **Contraction.** `‖P_N x‖ / ‖x‖ <= 1`, required for every operator. Gated
  (not merely reported) since D-012 — this is the check the FBP-based CT
  decomposition used to fail.

A wrong adjoint, a non-idempotent projector, or an amplifying null projector
corrupts every score in the bank in a way that is invisible downstream — the
numbers stay plausible. All three checks are cheap; there is no reason not to
run them every time.

The CT adjoint is obtained as the vector-Jacobian product of the forward map.
Because the forward map is linear, the VJP **is** the exact transpose, so the
dot-product test passes to floating-point precision despite bilinear
interpolation in the projector.

### 6. Operator grids

Grids are declared in config; list-valued axes expand to the Cartesian product.

```yaml
- modality: mri
  family: [random1d, equispaced1d, variable_density2d]
  acceleration: [2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10]
  center_fraction: 0.08
```

Grid axes are named in `registry.GRID_AXES`. MRI varies acceleration and mask
family; CT varies view count, angular range, and dose (Sec. 16).

One grid deserves attention: `mri_mask_family_shift` holds acceleration fixed
and varies only the mask family. Same undersampling budget, different null
space. It separates the *direction* of operator change from its *severity*,
which the acceleration sweep alone confounds — and the theoretical programme
needs that separation, since a distance measure that only tracks severity
cannot predict shifts that change direction.

### 7. Distance measures

All five Sec. 12.1 candidates are implemented in
`caliber.operators.distance` and selected by name in config:

| Name | Measure |
|---|---|
| `null_space_projection` | `‖P_N(A) − P_N(B)‖_F` |
| `sampling_support` | Symmetric difference of what is sampled |
| `chordal_subspace` | Principal-angle / chordal subspace distance |
| `operator_norm` | `‖A − B‖_F` |
| `spectral_divergence` | Difference in singular-value decay |

**Modality-agnosticism is a hard requirement** by Week 7: the same function
must accept an MRI pair and a CT pair. `null_space_projection` achieves this by
using a closed form when both operators expose a k-space mask — for masks,
`‖P_N(A) − P_N(B)‖_F` is exactly the square root of the mask symmetric
difference — and Hutchinson's estimator otherwise. The two paths are tested
against each other to within 15%.

Normalisation (`none` / `ambient` / `nullspace`) is configurable because Week 9
tests which choice makes the MRI and CT coverage-gap curves collapse onto a
shared trend. If none of them does, Sec. 21 R5 says revise the distance
definition before adding domains.

### 8. Open questions for Week 1 sign-off

- [ ] **Primary distance measure** (T1.3, locked Week 3). Working default is
      `null_space_projection` with `ambient` normalisation.
- [ ] **`calib_group` definition.** Currently derived in
      `runner.calibration_group`. Should it be a registry field instead, so

- [ ] **Mask seeds.** Is a mask family at fixed acceleration one group or

- [ ] **CT spectral estimation budget.** How many singular values does L2 need,

## Appendix C — Score-bank format specification (shared interface)

*Was `score_bank_spec.md`. The other half of the Sec. 19 shared interface: the cached
artefact every track reads. Produced by T2.3, consumed by all three tracks, so that
calibration, coverage, ablation and analytical work run from cached results without
repeated GPU reconstruction (Sec. 17).*

**Status:** draft for Week 1 sign-off by all three tracks
**Schema version:** `0.1.0` (`caliber.scorebank.schema.SCHEMA_VERSION`)
**Owner:** Track 2 (T2.3) · **Consumers:** Track 1 (T1.2), Track 3 (T3.6)

Blueprint references: Sec. 17 (compute strategy), Sec. 19 (shared interfaces),
Week 1 and Week 3 cards.

---

### 1. Why this exists

Reconstructions are computed **once**, on a GPU, and cached. Every subsequent
calibration experiment, coverage curve, ablation, and numerical check reads the
cache and needs no GPU at all. That single decision is what makes the project's
scope affordable on the available hardware, and it is what lets Track 1 and
Track 3 work in parallel with Track 2 rather than behind it.

The bank is the only data interface between the three tracks. If a track needs
something that is not in the bank, the answer is to add a column — never to
re-run reconstruction.

### 2. Three design rules

Each rule exists to prevent re-running GPU work, and each one has already paid
for itself once in a project of this shape.

**Rule 1 — store components, not conclusions.**
The three score components (`s_free`, `s_disagree`, `s_residual`) are stored
separately, never pre-combined. Track 1 finalises the score in Week 3 (T1.1)
and will re-weight it several times; every re-weighting must cost zero GPU
seconds.

**Rule 2 — store continuous quantities, not labels.**
`err_total`, `err_free`, and `ann_coverage` are stored raw. The ground-truth
hallucination criterion (O2) is a Week 2 decision that will be revisited; a
bank full of booleans would have to be regenerated when it changes.

**Rule 3 — store provenance on every row.**
`run_id`, `config_hash`, `operator_id`, `solver_id`, `solver_checkpoint`, and
`seed` travel with each row. Any figure must be traceable to the run that
produced it, without consulting a lab notebook.

### 3. On-disk layout

```
runs/<run_id>/
  manifest.json              provenance, environment, embedded operator registry
  operator_registry.json     the registry snapshot, standalone
  scores.parquet             the long-format table below
  recons/                    OPTIONAL float16 posterior samples, small subset only
```

A bank is **self-describing**: the manifest embeds the operator registry, so
Track 3 can compute operator distances and Track 1 can calibrate from a copied
directory with no access to the machine that produced it.

`recons/` is off by default (`run.cache_reconstructions: 0`). A full grid of
float32 posterior samples is orders of magnitude larger than the score table
and would defeat the point of caching. Enable it for a fixed handful of items
used in figures.

### 4. Table schema

One row per **(item, operator, solver, region)**.

#### Provenance
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `schema_version` | string | Must equal `0.1.0` throughout a bank |
| `run_id` | string | `<experiment name>-<config hash>` |
| `config_hash` | string | 16 hex chars over the resolved config |

#### Item identity
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `dataset` | string | `fastmri`, `lodopab`, `synthetic_phantom` |
| `split` | string | `calib`, `test` |
| `sample_id` | string | Volume or file stem |
| `slice_idx` | int32 | 2-D single slice per the Sec. 17 compute profile |

#### Acquisition operator — joins to the operator registry
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `operator_id` | string | Primary key into `operator_registry.json` |
| `modality` | string | `mri`, `ct` |
| `operator_family` | string | `random1d`, `sparse_view`, `limited_angle`, … |
| `calib_group` | string | The group for group-conditional calibration (Tool 1) |

#### Solver
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `solver_id` | string | Family, name, checkpoint, step count |
| `solver_family` | string | `diffusion`, `flow_matching`, `reference` |
| `solver_checkpoint` | string | Exact weights used |
| `n_posterior` | int16 | Posterior draws per item |
| `seed` | int32 | Per-item seed; runs are reproducible from it |

#### Region
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `region_id` | int32 | Index into the region grid |
| `region_row`, `region_col` | int16 | Grid position |
| `region_area` | int32 | Pixels; constant under the v0 patch grid |

#### Score components — Rule 1
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `score_version` | string | Bump when the component definitions change |
| `s_free` | float32 | RMS energy of the null-space component |
| `s_disagree` | float32 | Cross-sample spread, free component only |
| `s_residual` | float32 | Backprojected measurement residual |

#### Ground truth — Rule 2
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `criterion_version` | string | Bump when the O2 criterion changes |
| `err_total` | float32 | Region RMSE, reconstruction vs truth |
| `err_free` | float32 | Region RMSE restricted to the free component |
| `ann_coverage` | float32 | Fraction of the region under an annotation |
| `ref_intensity` | float32 | Scale used to normalise the above |

**Why `err_free` is separate from `err_total`:** a region can be wrong because
the prior invented structure, or because it smoothed structure away. Only the
first is hallucination in the Blueprint's sense (Sec. 4). Restricting to the
free component is what distinguishes them, and the distinction has to survive
into the bank or the criterion cannot be revised later.

### 5. Invariants

Enforced by `caliber.scorebank.schema.validate`, tested in
`tests/test_scorebank.py`:

1. No duplicate rows for the key `(dataset, split, sample_id, slice_idx,
   operator_id, solver_id, seed, region_id)`.
2. One schema version per bank.
3. No nulls in score or error columns.
4. `n_posterior >= 1`.
5. **One solver per `run_id`.** More than one is the signature of a violated
   controlled comparison (Sec. 13) and is reported as a validation problem.
   The solver ablation runs one experiment per solver and merges afterwards.

### 6. Changing this spec

Bumping `SCHEMA_VERSION` is a cross-track decision. Record it in
[T2_problems_and_decisions.md](T2_problems_and_decisions.md) Part II, with the reason
and the migration path for existing banks.
Adding a nullable column is backwards-compatible and does not require a bump.

### 7. Open questions for Week 1 sign-off

- [ ] **Region definition.** v0 is a 16px patch grid. Does the O2 risk
      definition (Week 2) need anatomically derived regions instead? If so,
      `region_area` stops being constant and region-level rates become weighted.
- [ ] **`calib_group` granularity.** Currently modality/family/severity. Too
      fine starves groups of calibration data (the declared Tool 1 trade-off);
      too coarse mixes regimes that genuinely differ.
- [ ] **Calibration/test split.** Currently a column. Should split assignment
      be fixed per `sample_id` in the registry so it is identical across every
      run and modality?
- [ ] **Multi-coil MRI.** v0 reduces coils by root-sum-of-squares, so the
      studied operator is single-coil Cartesian undersampling. Confirm this is
      acceptable and state it in the Experimental Setup.

### E. Findings referred to other tracks

Both were raised by Track 2 from measurements, and neither is Track 2's to
resolve. This is the adjudication process in Sec. 19 running for the first
time.

**D-011 → Track 3, needed by Week 6.** Randomised spectral estimation barely
separates CT operators: the fitted decay exponent moves 1.688 → 1.676 between
180 and 20 views. What distinguishes these operators is the decay tail and the
null space, which is what the estimator resolves worst. L2 relates the bound to
null-space dimension *and spectral decay*; if the decay term cannot be
estimated at this scale, L2 either rests on `null_space_dim` alone or must
declare its spectral term an estimate of unestablished accuracy.

**D-020 → Track 1, needed for the Week 2 O2 decision.** The v0 criterion
`err_free > 0.10` labels **100% of regions hallucinated** on real MRI k-space,
making region FDR identically zero and the flag vacuous. The projector is not
at fault here — MRI's `err_free`/`err_total` ratio is 0.97, within what an
orthogonal projection allows, unlike CT's 1.33. The threshold is simply
calibrated to phantoms, and real anatomy carries far more null-space content.
D-012 showed the constant does not transfer across modalities; this shows it
does not transfer across substrates within one modality. **The Week 2 criterion
cannot be an absolute constant** — it needs to be relative, or defined so the
positive rate is controlled rather than observed.

**D-012 → blocks Week 7, fix owned by Track 2.** The FBP-based CT null
projector amplifies (`‖P_N x‖/‖x‖` = 1.74 at 30 views, where an orthogonal
projection can never exceed 1), and it degrades **monotonically in the same
direction as operator distance**. A CT coverage-gap-vs-distance curve could
therefore come out monotone because our decomposition degrades, not because
reliability does. The two are currently confounded and the Week 9
curve-collapse test cannot separate them. Fix is a conjugate-gradient
pseudo-inverse for the decomposition, Week 7, before any CT coverage curve is
reported. This is also the empirical answer to what D-006 left open: the
approximation does not merely loosen constants, it inverts the inequality the
free component is supposed to satisfy.
