## Why this task opened here rather than at the grid

The natural order is grids first, statistics second. P-23 inverted it.

Track 2 measured per-operator coverage intervals on the Week 3 fastMRI bank and found them
too wide to carry a claim — ±0.125 at best against the ±0.10 that would separate "coverage
restored" from "coverage failed" — and then showed that the ceiling is a property of having
**96 patients**, not of compute. Their Week 4 update concluded:

> fastMRI `singlecoil_val` **cannot support a per-operator coverage claim at any grid
> size**, and no amount of CPU time changes that.

That is correct, and it is not a reason to redesign the study. It is a reason to state what
the study actually estimates, which nobody had written down. **D45** does that. The finding
in one line: the quantity P-23 sized against is not the quantity the project claims, and the
quantity it claims is far better determined.

## 1. The estimand

Three contrasts, in increasing precision. All three are reported; which one carries a given
claim depends on the claim.

| # | Contrast | Varies | Cancels | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Coverage at one operator vs nominal | — | nothing | Does the certificate hold here? |
| **B** | Coverage at the deployment operator vs at the calibration operator | operator | the patient | How does reliability move with operator distance? |
| **C** | Coverage under two calibrations at one operator | the threshold | the patient *and* the operator | Does the remedy work? |

**A is the weakest and it is the one P-23 sized.** It is capped at ±0.125 by the patient
count and cannot be rescued. It stays in the write-up as a descriptive quantity, always with
its interval, never as the headline.

**B is the headline figure's y-axis** — §12's coverage-gap-against-operator-distance plot.

**C is the project's central claim** — "group-conditional calibration restores the coverage
naive transfer loses" — and it is the best determined of the three, because both nuisance
terms cancel.

## 2. Why B and C are paired, and what that buys

This is a **retrospective undersampling** study (Sec. 13): one k-space volume, every mask
applied to it. So the same patient appears at every operator, and the design is **fully
crossed**. Two consequences, both measured rather than assumed.

**The patient budget is not divided across operators.** P-23's grid table divides ~40 test
patients by `k` and concludes a six-operator grid reaches only ±0.305. In a crossed design
each operator sees all ~40, so the per-operator half-width stays where one operator puts it,
whatever the grid holds. **Adding operators costs compute, not precision.** `is_crossed()`
checks this on a bank, and `scripts/sizing.py` now branches on it — a future design that
reconstructed different patients at different operators would make the division correct
again, so the check stays in the code rather than becoming an assumption in prose.

**The patient's own difficulty cancels out of a difference.** For contrast B,

```
Var(cov_cal − cov_dep) = Var(cov_cal) + Var(cov_dep) − 2·Cov(cov_cal, cov_dep)
```

and the dominant term in each variance is how hard that patient's anatomy is — shared
between the two arms, so it cancels. Estimating the gap by subtracting two independently
bootstrapped coverages discards the covariance and reports an interval the design does not
require.

**Measured, on a controlled sweep** (40 clusters, 40 positives each, `σ` = SD of the shared
per-cluster difficulty):

| `σ` (between-patient SD) | 0.00 | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.15 | 0.20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| paired SE | 0.0130 | 0.0128 | 0.0130 | 0.0126 | 0.0132 |
| unpaired SE | 0.0131 | 0.0181 | 0.0251 | 0.0322 | 0.0385 |
| **narrower by** | 1.00× | 1.41× | 1.93× | 2.55× | **2.92×** |

**The paired standard error is flat.** It does not grow with patient heterogeneity at all,
because the heterogeneity is exactly what the pairing removes. The unpaired SE triples over
the same range.

**The gain is therefore a property of the substrate, not of the estimator, and is reported
per bank.** On the phantom bank it is 1.0–1.25×, which is correct and expected:
Shepp-Logan variants are nearly identical, so there is almost no between-patient variance to
cancel. Real knee anatomy across 30 patients is the opposite case. **What the gain is on the
Week 3 fastMRI bank is unmeasured here** — that bank is not redistributable (D-045) and
stays on Track 2's machine. It is one command:

```
PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/sizing.py runs/w03_mri_fixed_operator-fc534c8f4d898e1b \
    --calibration-operator mri.random1d.R2.c0.08.s0
```

**The sharpest demonstration that the unpaired interval is inflated** needs no substrate at
all: coverage at an operator minus coverage at *the same* operator is zero with no
uncertainty whatever, and the paired estimator returns exactly that, while differencing two
independent bootstraps returns SE ≈ 0.014 for a quantity that cannot vary. That error is
what P-23's grid table was propagating. Pinned in `tests/test_paired_gap.py`.

## 3. What this does not fix

Stated plainly, because the temptation is to read a reprieve as a solution.

- **Per-operator coverage is still capped at ±0.125.** Contrast A does not improve. If the
  write-up quotes per-operator coverage, it quotes intervals, and those intervals overlap.
- **It is not more data.** fastMRI `singlecoil_train` (D-013, P-14) remains the only lever
  that raises the ceiling on contrast A, and it is 72.7 GB.
- **The clustering unit is still the volume, not the patient.** fastMRI has ~2 volumes per
  patient (D-027), so every interval here is optimistic by that factor until the bank
  carries a patient id.
- **Nothing becomes reportable.** The prior is still a face model (D-016, D-035). This ruling
  changes how the numbers are read, not whether they may be quoted.
- **The `1/√n` extrapolation is an order of magnitude**, not a power calculation, and it is
  extrapolated from 12 clusters on the real bank.

## 4. Consequences for the protocol

1. **The headline figure plots contrast B with paired intervals**, against operator distance
   (D19's `null_space_projection`/`ambient`).
2. **The central claim is carried by contrast C**, per operator, with the interval stated.
3. **Flag area is co-headline, not a footnote.** It is a fraction of every region rather than
   of the few positive ones, so it is far better determined — and P-21 showed the failure
   mode under operator shift is efficiency, not validity, which makes flag area the axis the
   result actually lives on. This adopts Track 2's option 2 from P-23, for the reason they
   gave.
4. **Grid size is a compute decision, not a precision one** (crossed design). The Week 4
   grid should be sized against wall-clock and P-16, not against a patient budget.
5. **Region-level rates are not comparable across operators at face value** — P-21's
   surviving finding, since the label base rate nearly doubles from R=2 to R=8. Flag area and
   FDR carry the operator with them; coverage, being conditional, does not.

## 5. The operator grid

**Both directions, always.** D50 measured that ascending the grid (calibrate at low
acceleration, deploy at high) breaks *efficiency* while descending it breaks *validity* —
coverage 1.000 at flag area 1.000 one way, coverage 0.000 the other. The Week 4 grid has
only ever been analysed ascending, which is the benign half. Every pair is swept both ways
and the figure carries the sign (D47). This costs nothing: the design is crossed (D45), so
the calibration operator is an analysis-time choice.

**Grid size is a compute decision, not a precision one.** The crossed design means every
operator sees every test patient, so adding operators does not divide the patient budget
(D45). Size the grid against wall-clock and P-16.

**Spacing is within-family.** D-052 and D47 together: a family change saturates the
symmetric axis at ~0.65 and contributes nothing graded, so the graded stretch is entirely
within-family. Three families × three accelerations, with family as a **categorical series**
rather than a position on the x-axis (D47's revised ruling) — one line per family, not one
curve through nine points.

**Interior operators are required, not optional.** D52 found that Tool 2 cannot
extrapolate: holding out an endpoint operator asks the weighting to reach past every
calibration regime it has, and it fails. Q4's "off-grid deployment operators" must therefore
mean *between* grid points. A grid whose held-out operators are all endpoints cannot test
the primary contribution at all.

## 6. The ablation matrix

| axis | levels | metric | decided by |
|---|---|---|---|
| score components | `s_free`, `s_disagree`, combination | flag area at fixed α, **validity first** | D39 as corrected by D51; `s_residual` excluded by D42 |
| calibration tool | naive, Mondrian, nearest, weighted (Tool 2) | validity, then flag area | D52 / `tool2_holdout.py` |
| shift direction | ascending, descending | paired coverage gap (D45) | D50 |
| mask family | `random1d`, `equispaced1d`, `variable_density2d` | categorical series | D47, D-052 |
| region size | 16 px, and at least one coarser | flag area and per-group positive count | D20's declared open sub-item |
| solver | DDNM, DPS, FlowDPS | all of the above, per family | D-033's step-budget precondition binds per family |
| mask seed | ≥3 seeds at one operator | spread of the gap — the **noise floor** | D24 |
| measurement noise | only if varied | a group axis if it is (D22) | D22 |

**Read the noise-floor row first.** Seed variation at a fixed operator is the null
distribution for everything else in the table: an effect smaller than the seed spread is not
an effect. Nothing in the project has measured it yet, and every ablation above is
uninterpretable without it.

**Validity gates every cell.** D51: an arm or component that under-covers does not compete
on flagged area, because flagging nothing is cheap once you have stopped catching anything.
