## Objective

Evaluate the five candidate operator-distance measures listed in blueprint §12.1 against the requirement T3.1 made precise: a valid choice must be defensible as **bounding** $d_{TV}(R(Z), R(Z^i))$ — the total-variation distance between non-conformity **score** vectors under an operator swap — not merely correlating with it (`T3.1_formal_review.md`, §3.3–§5), and it must be computable from the two known operators alone, without ground truth (open question Q1, `problems_and_decisions.md`). This task narrows the candidate space and records the structural reasoning. It does not prove a bound — that derivation is T3.3 (L1).

---

## 1. The five candidates are not five independent choices

Working through the definitions, two things collapse the blueprint's list of five down to a smaller set of genuinely distinct options.

**Candidates 1 and 2 are the same family.** "Null-space projection distance" (candidate 1: the separation between the orthogonal projectors onto the two null spaces) *is* a subspace/principal-angle distance (candidate 2) — specifically, $\|P_{N_1} - P_{N_2}\|_{\text{op}} = \sin(\theta_{\max})$, the sine of the largest principal angle between the two null spaces, is one standard instantiation of the family; the chordal distance $\sqrt{\sum_i \sin^2\theta_i}$ and the geodesic distance $\sqrt{\sum_i \theta_i^2}$ are others (standard identities from subspace perturbation theory — see Stewart & Sun, *Matrix Perturbation Theory*, 1990; worth confirming the exact Frobenius-norm constant before it appears in a proof, but the qualitative max-vs-sum distinction below does not depend on getting that constant exactly right). So this is really **one family with a choice of aggregation rule**, not two candidates — §3 below is about which aggregation to pick.

**Candidate 5 (spectral divergence) is not competing for the same role as 1–4.** The blueprint's own framing already says it "captures severity of ill-posedness rather than direction of change." That is precisely what blueprint §12.2's **L2** stage wants (the bound should scale with null-space dimension and spectral decay) — a *modifier* on how a given amount of subspace movement translates into coverage loss, not a candidate for measuring the movement itself. Treating it as a fifth competitor for "the" operator distance would blur T3.2's job (pick the primary distance, the x-axis of the headline plot) with T3.3/L2's job (explain how ill-posedness scales the resulting bound). Recorded here so T3.3 does not have to rediscover this separation.

That leaves three genuinely distinct candidates to compare: **(A) the subspace/principal-angle family** (candidates 1+2 merged), **(B) direct operator or pseudo-inverse norms** (candidate 3), and **(C) sampling-support difference** (candidate 4).

## 2. Candidate-by-candidate assessment

| | Definition | Computable without ground truth? | Modality-general (MRI + CT)? | Fits the score's structure? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(A) Subspace/principal-angle family** | Distance between the projectors onto $\text{null}(A_{\text{cal}})$ and $\text{null}(A_{\text{deploy}})$ | Yes — function of $A_{\text{cal}}, A_{\text{deploy}}$ alone | **Yes.** Defined purely via linear algebra on any linear/affine operator; makes no reference to a special structure like a diagonal sampling mask, so it applies identically to MRI's masked-Fourier operator and CT's angle-subsampled Radon operator | **Strong.** T1.1's score (D2) is built directly from the null-space/range decomposition; a distance between the *decompositions themselves* is the natural match |
| **(B) Operator / pseudo-inverse norms** | $\|A_{\text{cal}} - A_{\text{deploy}}\|$ or $\|A_{\text{cal}}^+ - A_{\text{deploy}}^+\|$ | Yes | Yes, same reasoning as (A) | **Weak for the null-space part, plausibly right for one other part** — see §4 |
| **(C) Sampling-support difference** | Symmetric difference of what the two acquisitions actually sample (e.g. symmetric difference of sampled k-space indices) | Yes, and cheaply | **No — this is the finding worth flagging.** See below | Exact match to (A) in MRI only |

**Why (C) is not modality-general.** For MRI with a binary Cartesian sampling mask, $A$ is (mask) $\circ$ (Fourier transform) — sampling is diagonal in the Fourier basis, so the null space is exactly spanned by the unsampled frequency indices, and the symmetric difference of sampled indices *is* the null-space projector distance under an $\ell_0$-type norm. Candidate (C) is not an approximation to (A) in MRI, it is (A), read off directly because MRI's operator happens to be diagonal. CT's forward operator (Radon transform restricted to a subset of projection angles) has no comparable diagonal structure — restricting to fewer angles does not correspond to zeroing out a known, disjoint set of basis coordinates the way a k-space mask does, so "symmetric difference of sampled angles" is at best a heuristic proxy for how the null space actually moved, not the same quantity. Since blueprint §11.2's cross-domain claim is that *a single distance measure* should predict coverage loss in both modalities, choosing (C) as the headline measure would mean using an exact quantity in one modality and an unrelated heuristic in the other — a direct risk to the paper's central cross-domain claim. Recommendation: keep (C) as a cheap, interpretable MRI-only sanity check (it is, after all, free once (A) is computed there, and easy to explain to a non-specialist per Q1's "something a radiographer would recognize" requirement) but do not use it as the primary measure.

## 3. The saturation problem in family (A) — a concrete finding, not a footnote

Within family (A), the natural first choice is the operator-norm ("gap metric") version, $\|P_{N_1} - P_{N_2}\|_{\text{op}} = \sin\theta_{\max}$, since worst-case/operator-norm quantities compose naturally with the kind of Lipschitz-style stability argument T3.3's L1 will likely need. There is a real problem with using it as the *headline* distance, though:

**A standard fact in subspace perturbation theory: when the two null spaces have different dimensions, $\|P_{N_1} - P_{N_2}\|_{\text{op}} = 1$ exactly, regardless of how different the dimensions are.** One extra hidden dimension and a thousand extra hidden dimensions both saturate this metric at its maximum value.

This is not a corner case here — it is the *typical* case across exactly the experiment the protocol requires. Blueprint §16 calls for varying the acceleration factor "so operator distance ranges from small to large," but a higher acceleration factor almost always **changes the null-space dimension** (fewer measured k-space lines ⇒ strictly larger null space). Under the operator-norm version of (A), the entire acceleration-factor sweep would report a distance pinned at 1 as soon as the dimension changes at all, which cannot be the monotone, graded quantity the headline coverage-gap-vs-distance plot needs, and directly fails Q1's requirement that the distance be "monotone in something a radiographer would recognize as a bigger change to the scan."

**The aggregate (chordal or Frobenius-norm) version of (A) does not saturate this way.** When one null space has $p$ dimensions and the other has $q>p$ (e.g. nested, as when deployment simply undersamples further than calibration), the "extra" $q-p$ principal angles are each exactly $90°$, and a sum-based aggregate ($\sqrt{\sum_i \sin^2\theta_i}$) grows with $q-p$ rather than saturating instantly — it keeps distinguishing "one extra hidden dimension" from "a thousand," which is the behavior the acceleration-factor sweep actually needs.

**Consequence for the design:** the operator-norm and aggregate versions of family (A) are not interchangeable stand-ins for each other; they likely serve two different roles. The **aggregate (chordal/Frobenius) version** is the better candidate for the *empirical* headline distance — the x-axis of the coverage-gap-vs-distance plot across a wide operator sweep. Whether the **operator-norm version** is still the right quantity to appear *inside* T3.3's actual L1 proof is a separate, open question — Lipschitz-style stability bounds often do use operator norms, but Frobenius-type norms appear in plenty of stability results too, and which one composes correctly will depend on the specific proof technique T3.3 ends up using. This project should not assume in advance that the plotted distance and the proved distance are the same object; T3.3 should check this explicitly rather than inherit it silently from T3.2.

## 4. The score has three parts (T1.1); the distance may need to as well

T1.1's hallucination score combines three components: null-space energy, cross-sample disagreement, and measurement residual. Only the first two are naturally about the null space — the residual term ($\|y - Ax\|$-type) depends on $A$ directly, not on $A$'s null space in isolation. Candidate (B) (operator/pseudo-inverse norms) is a poor match for the null-space-energy and disagreement terms — it is sensitive to harmless reparametrizations of $A$ (e.g. rescaling) that leave the null space, and therefore the score's dominant terms, completely unchanged — but it is plausibly the *right* term for bounding movement specifically in the residual component, since the residual is computed directly from $A$ and not from the null-space decomposition.

This suggests the eventual operator distance may not be a single scalar but a **composite of a family-(A) term (for the null-space-energy and disagreement terms of the score) plus a family-(B) term (for the residual term)** — structurally mirroring the score's own three-part design rather than collapsing everything into one number prematurely. This is a design direction to hand to T3.3, not a decision made here; a single aggregate family-(A) distance may turn out to be sufficient if the residual term is empirically small or highly correlated with the null-space term, but that is an empirical question, not one this task can settle analytically.

---

## 5. Recommendation

- **Primary candidate: family (A), aggregate (chordal or Frobenius-based) form.** Computable without ground truth, modality-general by construction (no dependence on MRI's special diagonal structure), directly matched to the null-space/range decomposition the score is already built on, and — unlike the operator-norm version — does not saturate across the acceleration-factor and view-count sweeps the experimental protocol requires.
- **Open, not decided:** whether the operator-norm version of family (A) is still needed *inside* the L1 proof itself, separately from its unsuitability as the plotted headline distance (§3). T3.3 must check this against whatever proof technique it uses, not assume the plotted and proved quantities coincide.
- **Secondary, exploratory:** a family-(B) term to specifically cover the score's residual component, as a composite distance rather than a replacement for family (A) (§4).
- **Not the primary measure:** candidate (C), sampling-support difference — kept only as a free, interpretable MRI-only diagnostic, explicitly not used as the cross-modality headline distance, because it is not modality-general (§2).
- **Deferred, not competing:** candidate 5, spectral divergence — belongs to L2 (ill-posedness scaling), not to the choice of primary distance made here (§1).

## 6. Open questions handed to T3.3

- Does the aggregate family-(A) distance actually **bound** $d_{TV}(R(Z), R(Z^i))$ — the requirement T3.1 established — or only correlate with it empirically? This task provides structural reasoning for why it is a *plausible* candidate (direct match to the score's null-space construction); T3.3 has to either prove a bound or find that it needs the composite form from §4.
- Does the proof technique T3.3 ends up using need the operator-norm form of family (A), the aggregate form, or both for different parts of the argument (§3's open point)?
- Is the family-(B) residual term (§4) needed at all, or does the null-space term alone already dominate empirically? This can only really be settled once T3.5's numerical verification is running on synthetic operators — flag for T3.5, not resolvable analytically here.
- CT's Radon-transform null space does not have MRI's convenient explicit diagonal form — computing family-(A) distances there will need a matrix-free (forward/adjoint-operator) formulation rather than an explicit projector matrix. This is an implementation concern for whoever builds the operator pipeline (T2.2 per the old two-track README, or T2.1 under the current three-track structure — the repo's Track ownership is not fully reconciled between the two, see the note left in `T3.1_formal_review.md`'s commit history), not a blocker for the analytical work in T3.3, but worth surfacing early rather than discovering it during cross-domain implementation.
