## Why this task exists

§19 makes coordination a design requirement rather than an administrative one:
the three tracks share **one claim**, so a disagreement between them is a
finding, not a scheduling problem. Two shared interfaces carry that, and Track 1
adjudicates when they conflict.

**Score bank** — one cached format for reconstructions and scores, produced once
by T2.3 and consumed by all three tracks, so calibration and analytical work
never duplicate reconstruction effort. Spec: `score_bank_spec.md`.

**Operator registry** — one definition of each acquisition operator *and of the
distance between operators*, used identically in both modalities and in the
derivations. Spec: `operator_registry_spec.md`.

**The one addition Track 2 had not asked for** is in B3. Their reasons for a
registry-fixed split were bank mergeability and slice-level leakage, both
correct. Track 1 added the binding one: the split must be **operator-independent**,
the same patient on the same side in *every* regime. Otherwise group-conditional
calibration compares different patient populations across regimes, and a coverage
difference between R=4 and R=8 is confounded with anatomy rather than
attributable to acquisition. That is §13's controlled-comparison assumption
applied to the split, and violating it would undermine the headline claim rather
than merely inconvenience a merge.

§19's cross-verification: unexpected empirical findings are referred to whoever
owns the explanation, not dropped. Eight have run so far, in both directions —
and **three of them are Track 2 catching an error in Track 1's own analysis**,

| — | `tool2_holdout.py` passed `split_col="__none__"`, so the same **patient** sat in calibration and evaluation and the `matched` arm landed on exactly 0.900 | **Track 2** | Track 1 | **Answered** — patient-disjoint, single-split banks refused, disjointness asserted |
| D-060 | Track 1's anatomy-control gate read 0.499, "exactly chance" — it was **pooled** across operators, and two opposite per-operator biases had cancelled (0.662 within R8) | **Track 2** | Track 1 | **Retracted** — never pool across operators |
| D-061 | Track 1's D50 direction assignment over-generalised from a reference sampler on phantoms (P-30) | **Track 2** | Track 1 | **Retracted** — D61 |
| D-065 | The null-error share cannot be taken per region: `P_N` is non-local, so `err_free²/err_total²` exceeds 1 in 97 of 384 M4Raw regions. If the 0.99-vs-0.817–0.852 figures are means of per-region ratios they are biased — and that gap is D68's whole premise | Track 1 | **Track 2** | Open — asked in D-065 |

**D-020 is the case worth studying**, because adjudication changed the question
rather than answering it. Track 2 concluded the threshold needed to be relative
rather than absolute. It is *already* relative — `labels.py` divides by
`ref_intensity`. The real cause is structural: with a pseudo-inverse-based
sampler, `A⁺y` carries zero null-space content by construction, so `err_free`
measures **the true image's own hidden content**, not what the reconstruction
invented. Reproduced on synthetic images at r = 0.95. So no threshold fixes it
while the sampler has no prior, and the criterion's threshold must not be set
from that run at all.

That is what §19's "referred back for analytical explanation" is for: the
measurement was right and the interpretation was not.
