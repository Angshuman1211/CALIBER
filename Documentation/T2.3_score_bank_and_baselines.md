## What I did

### 1. Two defects found before the bank was worth producing

Both were found while preparing the run, and both would have silently corrupted what the
bank claimed.

**P-18 — a face prior on knee MRI came out stamped `reportable: true`.** D-018 made
reportability a property of the bank and gated it on two things: is the substrate real,
is the sampler a generative prior. fastMRI is real (D-017) and DDNM is a real prior, so
both gates passed — while the prior was `ddpm-celebahq-256`, a **face model
reconstructing knee anatomy**. D-016 orders that deliberately and the T2.2 write-up lists
it as a reason nothing is reportable, but nothing in the *code* knew what the prose knew.

D-035 adds the third gate: the prior's training domain must match the modality. It fails
closed for unregistered checkpoints, and `prior_domain` is now recorded in every
manifest, so existing banks can be audited rather than re-run. The Week 3 run says:

```
[caliber] NOT REPORTABLE   prior domain 'natural_image' is not appropriate for 'mri' (D-016, P-18)
```

This was caught by one two-item smoke run, because the missing `NOT REPORTABLE` line was
conspicuous. Three weeks later, every bank in between would have carried a false flag.

**P-19 — `analyse_bank.py` had been broken for two weeks.** The script that produces this
week's two baselines crashed on every bank: `RegionTruth` gained a required
`free_content` field when A1 / D21 landed in Week 1, and this caller was never updated.
Nothing caught it because **no test executed the script** — the unit tests exercise the
library it calls, which is not the same thing.

Fixing the crash exposed two further defects that would never have raised an exception,
and both would have biased this week's baselines:

- **Calibration and evaluation shared rows.** The threshold was fitted on the calibration
  operator's rows and evaluated on a frame still containing them, so coverage *at the
  calibration operator* — the reference point the whole naive-transfer story is read
  against — was in-sample. It now calibrates on `split=calib` and evaluates on
  `split=test`, patient-disjoint by construction (D-022, D-027). *As first written, this
  held out only the naive threshold; the other two methods stayed in-sample until P-23 —
  see the correction in §4.*
- **The score was an unstandardised `1,1,1` sum**, which `score.py` says in its own
  docstring reduces to `score = s_free`. The combination was nominally three-component
  and effectively one. It now uses T1.1's ratified standardised affine rule with scales
  frozen on the calibration operator (D26, D27), printed above every table.

The real fix is `tests/test_analyse_bank.py`, which builds a bank on disk and runs
`main()` over it, plus a structural test asserting every `RegionTruth` field has a bank
column — so the next field added to the criterion cannot repeat this.

### 2. The Week 1 config was impossible, and Week 2's own findings fixed it

`w03_mri_fixed_operator.yaml` was drafted in Week 1 as `dps, n_steps: 200, 8 draws, 100
items` at 256². Measured on this machine that is **195 hours — 8.1 days**, because DPS
needs a backward pass per step and one UNet forward at 256² costs 1.46 s (P-16).

Rewritten against what Week 2 measured rather than what Week 1 guessed:

| choice | why |
|---|---|
| **DDNM, not DPS** | no backward pass, and D-033 measured DDNM's null-error share at 0.997 by 24 steps where DPS is still 0.495. Cheaper *and* better-founded. |
| **24 steps** | D-033's precondition, not a guess |
| **256²** | the checkpoint's native resolution; 128² is 4x cheaper but runs a 256-native prior off-resolution, and this bank is meant to stand as a result |
| **3 accelerations** | the two baselines are calibrate-here / deploy-there comparisons and cannot be computed from one operator at all |
| **`split: auto`** | conformal calibration needs a held-out set; the side is decided per patient and identically in every regime (D-022, D-027) |

### 4. The baselines, and what they show

> **Correction (P-23).** The first version of this section reported matched-per-operator
> and group-conditional coverage as **0.900 at every operator** and concluded that "the
> guaranteed backbone does exactly what it should". Both were wrong. `analyse_bank.py`
> held out the naive threshold but fitted those two methods' thresholds on the evaluation
> rows themselves, and split conformal scored on its own calibration rows lands on nominal
> by construction. The exactness — three operators in a row — is what gave it away. Fixed,
> with a regression test that checks the split of every row each fit and score receives.

Calibration operator R=2, deployed to R=4 and R=8, relative criterion, nominal 0.90.
Every threshold is fitted on the 13,824 `split=calib` rows (18 volumes) and scored only on
the 9,216 `split=test` rows (12 volumes), patient-disjoint.

**τ = 3.0**

| method | | R=2 | R=4 | R=8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive transferred | coverage | 0.914 | 0.915 | 0.955 |
| | flag area | 0.244 | 0.502 | 0.668 |
| matched / group-conditional | coverage | 0.914 | 0.874 | 0.779 |
| | flag area | 0.244 | 0.400 | 0.422 |

Matched and group-conditional coincide exactly here because the grid has one operator per
calibration group.

**Then the result that governs how any of this can be read.** A volume-level bootstrap
(2,000 resamples) puts 95% intervals on the coverage column:

| method | operator | coverage | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| matched | R=2 | 0.914 | [0.000, 1.000] |
| matched | R=4 | 0.874 | [0.509, 0.985] |
| matched | R=8 | 0.779 | [0.457, 0.919] |
| naive | R=8 | 0.955 | [0.871, 0.989] |

**With 12 test volumes, per-operator coverage is essentially unmeasured.** This bank can
show neither that the guaranteed backbone holds nor that it fails, and it cannot rank
naive against matched. At τ=3.0 the positives concentrate in a handful of volumes, which
is how R=2's interval spans the whole unit interval; and because volumes from one patient
are correlated (D-027), the true intervals are if anything wider.

What does survive is the qualitative pattern the *flag area* shows, because flag area is a
fraction of every region rather than of the few positive ones. **Naive transfer
over-flags as the operator shifts** — 0.24 → 0.67 — where the matched thresholds grow only
to 0.42.

The direction is robust: it holds at every τ tested (1.75, 2.5, 3.0, 4.0). At τ=1.75 the
effect is extreme — coverage 1.000 at both deployment operators with flag area 0.99.

### 5. Why, and why it matters (P-21)

Both the score **and the label** move with the operator, in the same direction:

| operator | label positive rate (τ=1.75) | median `err_free/free_content` | mean `s_free` |
|---|---|---|---|
| R=2 | 0.401 | 1.528 | 0.486 |
| R=4 | 0.681 | 2.296 | 0.847 |
| R=8 | 0.772 | 2.677 | 1.046 |

A threshold calibrated where positives are rare and scores are low, deployed where
positives are common and scores are high, flags nearly everything — and therefore catches
nearly every positive. Coverage is achieved trivially.

This is neither a calibration failure nor a pipeline failure. It is a property of the
**criterion**. A coverage comparison across operators is interpretable only if the
quantity being covered means the same thing at each operator, and the base rate of
"hallucinated" nearly doubles between R=2 and R=8. D-034 flagged exactly this risk on
M4Raw a week ago, where the trend was mild (1.480 → 1.645); on fastMRI knees with a face
prior it is severe (1.528 → 2.677), and it has crossed from a caution into a blocker.

It is **Track 1's to rule on** (O2). Track 2's part is measured and handed over.

### 6. Three infrastructure faults, each invisible to the test suite as it stood

Found after the bank and the baselines, while integrating `main` and while measuring
D-033. None raised an error. Each had a test suite that passed straight through it,
and each needed a test of a *different shape* to catch.

**P-22 — two split implementations agreed on the rules and disagreed on the patients.**
Track 1's stand-in `splits.py` (written while `main` was broken, P-20) shared the salt,
passed all 21 split tests and the full suite, and put **48 of the 96** real fastMRI
patients on the opposite side from the version that built this bank. The tests checked
what a split must *satisfy*, never *which side anyone lands on*. Resolved by keeping
Track 2's version (D-037) and pinning the mapping itself: 40 fixed keys at two
fractions, plus a SHA-256 of the real 96-patient assignment — a digest, so no patient
identifier is committed.

**P-25 — no bank the project had produced could be regenerated from its config.** Item
seeds came from Python's `hash()`, which is salted per process. Three runs of one item
gave seeds 77818, 549586 and 104573. Every determinism test compared two runs inside one
pytest process, where the salt is shared, so none could see it. Fixed with a BLAKE2b
digest (D-039); the regression test runs the pipeline in **two processes with different
`PYTHONHASHSEED`** and requires identical banks, and fails against the old derivation.
**This bank cannot be regenerated.** Its rows stay correct — each records the seed it
was drawn with — but re-running its config now draws differently.

**P-24 — at 256² the solver can die with a silent segfault.** Measured while running
D-033 (§7): with other applications holding ~21 GB of a ~26 GB commit limit, forward
passes at 256² — the operation this bank ran for 7.2 hours — segfaulted, while 128² never
did. Measured once headroom allowed: a 256² forward peaks at **6.0 GB** of commit for one
image, and at four images (this config's `n_posterior`) torch raises an explicit
out-of-memory error — so allocation failure is the cause, sometimes surfacing as a
segfault. **This week's config cannot run on this machine under today's memory load.** The runner held every row in memory and
wrote once at the end, so a crash at hour seven would have lost seven hours with no
traceback. `run.checkpoint_every` now writes a partial bank every N items, and
`read_bank` refuses a partial bank unless asked (D-038); tested by crashing a run
part-way and recovering exactly its finished rows. Restarting the same config now resumes
from the checkpoint (D-040), and is tested to produce exactly the bank an uninterrupted
run makes — which only became possible once seeds stopped depending on the process. A
preflight check (D-041) refuses a run whose estimated peak commit exceeds what the machine
has; its estimate reproduces eleven measured points within 0.4 GB and calls both observed
failures.
Also checked on the real pipeline, not only in tests: a DDNM run on fastMRI was killed
with `taskkill /F` at 3 of 9 pairs, restarted, and its 576-row bank matched an
uninterrupted run exactly.

### 7. D-033 re-measured on the real UNet

Owed since Week 2: D-033's step-budget gate was derived on the analytic denoiser, a
closed-form stand-in. Measured here on `ddpm-celebahq-256`, R=4, two items × two draws.
DPS at 256² could not run at all on this machine (P-24), so DPS and a matching DDNM
reference were measured at 128².

| solver | resolution | steps | null share of error | data-consistency error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDNM | 256² | 8 / 24 | 0.981 / 0.990 | 0.116 / 0.067 |
| DDNM | 128² | 8 / 24 | 0.969 / 0.986 | 0.118 / 0.068 |
| DPS | 128² | 8 | **0.003** | **116.7 — diverged** |
| DPS | 128² | 24 / 48 | 0.817 / 0.852 | 0.240 / 0.196 |

**DDNM** clears the gate at 8 steps at both resolutions, as on the analytic denoiser —
this bank's 24 steps is comfortably admissible. **DPS** is worse than the stand-in
predicted. At 8 steps it does not under-converge, it **diverges**. From 24 steps it
passes the gate, but plateaus near 0.85 with roughly three times DDNM's data-consistency
error even at 48 steps.

Whether that plateau is the guidance step size — D-031's 5.0, itself measured only on the
analytic denoiser — was tested directly, at 24 steps and 128²:

| guidance step size ζ | 0.5 | 1 | 2 | **5 (D-031)** | 10 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| null share of error | 0.627 | 0.707 | 0.756 | **0.817** | 0.872 | 0.931 |
| data-consistency error | 0.454 | 0.363 | 0.305 | **0.238** | 0.182 | 0.126 |

**The plateau is largely the step size, and D-031's value is wrong for this prior.** On the
analytic denoiser 5.0 was a clean minimum with 10 already worse. On the real UNet the fit
improves monotonically across the whole range tested and has **not turned at ζ=20**, so the
optimum lies above 20 and is not yet bracketed. At ζ=20, DPS reaches a data-consistency error of
0.126 — still about twice DDNM's 0.068 at the same 24 steps, but most of the way from the 0.238
the fixed constant gives. ζ=5 also reproduced the earlier sweep's 0.817 / 0.240 almost exactly
despite the seed change (D-039), so these aggregates are robust to the draws.

`DEFAULT_STEP_SIZE` is **deliberately left at 5.0**. The optimum is unbracketed; this is one
operator, two items and 128²; and stronger guidance is precisely what diverged at 8 steps, so a
larger ζ may need a larger step budget as well. D-031 still holds in its essential part — one
value, fixed across every operator in a curve — and it said explicitly that the value must be
re-measured for a new prior. The analytic denoiser was not the prior. **Before any DPS bank:
bracket ζ above 20, jointly with the step count, on the real UNet.** None of this touches this
week's bank, which is DDNM.

## Honest limitations

**Nothing here is reportable, and now the code says so.** The prior is a face model
(D-016). That is the single count remaining — the substrate is real and the solver is a
real generative prior — and it is exactly the count P-18 existed to make visible.

**The headline experiment does not yet mean what it is supposed to mean.** P-21 is open
and blocks the O3 reading of any coverage curve, including the ones in this document.
The numbers are real measurements; the *interpretation* as a coverage-failure
demonstration is what is blocked.

**30 items is not thin, it is insufficient — for coverage specifically.** The first
version of this record called it "thin" and still quoted coverage to four decimals. P-23
measured it: 12 test volumes give 95% intervals up to half the unit interval wide. 9,216
evaluation regions sounds like a lot, but regions within an image are not independent, and
the unit that matters is the volume — really the patient. Order of magnitude, a ±0.10
interval per operator needs ~170 items per operator (~6.5 CPU hours each), and fastMRI val
has only **96 patients** in total, which caps achievable precision near SE ~0.06 however
much compute is spent. That is a constraint the Week 4 protocol has to be designed around,
not a budget line.

**One mask family, one seed.** Mask seeds are members of a calibration group rather than
groups of their own (D-023), so within-group variation is entirely unmeasured here.

**τ is unset.** Every table above is quoted at a τ chosen to display the behaviour, not at
a ratified value. Track 1 owns that choice (D-034), and P-21 may change what choosing it
even means.

**The wall-clock number is contended.** 7.2 h against 3.5 estimated is a measurement of
this machine while it was also running a test suite, not of the job alone.

```bash
pip install -e ".[solvers]"

# The bank — 3.5 h on a quiet CPU, 7.2 h on a busy one. Checkpoint it (P-24, D-038),
```

## What this unblocks, and what it leaves open

**Unblocked.** Sec. 17's caching strategy is now real rather than intended: 23,040 rows of
continuous quantities, re-thresholdable and re-weightable on CPU. Whatever Track 1 decides
about P-21 is a re-analysis of this bank, not a re-run of it. From here on a bank is also
reproducible from its config (D-039) and survives a crash (D-038), neither of which was
true when this bank was built.

**Not unblocked, and worth being plain about:** Week 4's grid is *not* just a compute
question. P-23 puts a ±0.10 coverage interval per operator at ~170 items and ~6.5 CPU
hours *each*, and fastMRI val's **96 patients** cap precision near SE ~0.06 however much
compute is spent. The protocol has to be designed against that ceiling before the grid
is sized.

**Left open, owned by Track 2:**

- **P-16** — no GPU. Week 4's grid must be sized against 1.46 s per forward at 256², on a
  quiet machine. This is now the binding constraint on everything remaining.
- Wall-clock re-measurement on an uncontended machine, before Week 4 is scoped.
- **P-24** — 256² runs are at the mercy of whatever else is using memory. All three
  mitigations are now built: checkpointing (D-038), resume (D-040), and a preflight check
  that refuses a run that cannot fit (D-041). The constraint itself remains: **this week's
  config is refused on this machine today**, needing ~18 GB against ~6.6 GB available.
- DPS at 256² — unmeasurable here (P-24). §7's DPS numbers are 128² only.
- CT solver support — declared but still never exercised (Week 7).

**Owed to Track 1 as information, not decisions:** P-22's open half — whether anyone built
a `split: auto` bank on `main` under the stand-in split (D-037) — and P-23's data ceiling,
which the T1.4 protocol has to be designed around.

**Owed by others:** **P-21** is the largest open item in the project right now, and it is
Track 1's. Until the criterion's operator dependence is settled, no coverage curve —
including the ones above — can be read as evidence for or against O3. The score weights
are also still unset (D26–D28), which is the second thing standing between this bank and
a headline number.
