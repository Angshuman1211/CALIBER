## 0. What this section claims, in one paragraph

A split-conformal threshold calibrated on hallucinated regions at operator $A$ and deployed at operator $B$ loses (or gains) coverage by at most the Kolmogorov distance between the two operators' **label-conditional** score laws, plus $1/(n+1)$ when the gap is measured against the matched cell. That distance is bounded, through a coupling the crossed score bank observes, by label flips and score crossings, which in turn are bounded by $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$ times constants estimable from the bank. Every step is valid; the first is close to tight by construction and the last is loose by one to two orders of magnitude. The paper should say both.

---

## 1. Setting and notation

**Operators and the split.** $A, B$ are known linear forward operators on the same image space, $P_N^A = I - A^+A$ the projector onto $A$'s null space. A reconstruction splits as $\hat x = P_R\hat x + P_N\hat x$; the range part is pinned by the measurements and the null part is the prior's.

**Score and label.** $s$ is T1.1's fixed hallucination score (standardised affine, scales frozen on one operator, D26/D27); $S_A$ is its value on a region under $A$. The label is D-061's absolute one, $\Lambda_A = \mathbf 1\{E_A > \varepsilon\}$ with $E_A = \|P_N^A(\hat x_A - x)\|$ on the region and $\varepsilon$ a quantile of $E$ on the design patients, **one value for every operator**. Write $\pi_A = P(\Lambda_A = 1)$.

**The laws.** $F_A^+(t) = P(S_A \le t \mid \Lambda_A = 1)$, likewise $F_B^+$; $G_A(t) = P(S_A \le t, \Lambda_A = 1)$, so $F_A^+ = G_A/\pi_A$. The Kolmogorov distance is $d_K^+ = \sup_t |F_A^+(t) - F_B^+(t)|$ and $W_1^+ = \int |F_A^+ - F_B^+|$.

**Calibration.** $n_A$ calibration positives at $A$, $k = \lfloor \alpha(n_A+1)\rfloor$, threshold $\tau_A$ = the $k$-th smallest positive score; a region is flagged when $S \ge \tau_A$ (`split_threshold`; $k < 1$ flags everything and certifies nothing). Coverage at $B$ is $\text{cov}(A\to B) = P(S_B \ge \tau_A \mid \Lambda_B = 1)$.

**The two gaps** (Track 1's frozen definitions, `prereg.GAP_DEFINITION` and its sensitivity arm):
$$\text{gap}_{\text{nom}} = (1-\alpha) - \text{cov}(A\to B), \qquad \text{gap}_{\text{mat}} = \text{cov}(B\to B) - \text{cov}(A\to B).$$

**The coupling.** The score bank is *crossed*: every (patient, slice, region) is reconstructed under every operator with the same image and the same noise seed. So $(S_A, \Lambda_A, S_B, \Lambda_B)$ is jointly observed per region. Nothing below needs a coupling a deployment would have; the bound is stated over the one the experiment has.

## 2. Step 1 — the gap is bounded by a distance between two score laws

**Theorem 1.** Let the $n_A$ calibration positives at $A$ be independent draws from $F_A^+$, and the test positive at $B$ an independent draw from $F_B^+$. Then

(a) $\mathbb E[\text{miss}_B] \le \alpha + d_K^+$, i.e. $\mathbb E[\text{gap}_{\text{nom}}] \le d_K^+$;

(b) if the scores have no ties, $-\,d_K^+ - \dfrac{1}{n_A+1} \;\le\; \mathbb E[\text{gap}_{\text{mat}}] \;\le\; d_K^+ + \dfrac{1}{n_B+1}$.

*Proof.* Conditional on the calibration draw, $\text{miss}_B = F_B^+(\tau_A^-)$ and $\text{miss}_A = F_A^+(\tau_A^-)$, so $|\text{miss}_B - \text{miss}_A| \le d_K^+$ pointwise in $\tau_A$. Split-conformal validity gives $\mathbb E[\text{miss}_A] = k/(n_A+1) \le \alpha$, which is (a). Without ties $k/(n+1) \ge \alpha - 1/(n+1)$, so $\mathbb E[\text{cov}(X\to X)] \in [1-\alpha,\ 1-\alpha+\frac{1}{n_X+1}]$ for $X = A, B$; combining with $|\text{cov}(A\to B) - \text{cov}(A\to A)| \le d_K^+$ gives both sides of (b). $\square$

Four remarks, each of which changes how the paper should read the bound.

1. **The law is the label-conditional one.** The calibrator fits on positives, so $d_K^+$, not the unconditional $d_K$, is the quantity in the bound. The unconditional version is false often enough to matter: on the harness it fails at 6 of 12 pairs of the CT structured-prior bank and 1 of 2 evaluable pairs of the MRI one, where the label-conditional bound never does (T3.6 §4.5).
2. **(b) is two-sided.** T3.3 §4b said the bound "does not describe the over-coverage failure". For $\text{gap}_{\text{nom}}$ that is right. For $\text{gap}_{\text{mat}}$ it is not: an inherited threshold can over-cover by no more than $d_K^+ + 1/(n_A+1)$ in expectation either. What the bound cannot see is the *cost* of over-coverage, which is flag area.
3. **The slack $1/(n+1)$ is negligible at the region count** (about $10^{-4}$ on the LIDC bank's thousands of calibration positives) and is not the binding term. What binds is that regions are not independent: Proposition 2.
4. **Tightness has a meaning.** By D89's identity $\text{gap}_{\text{mat}} = F_B(\tau_A^-) - F_B(\tau_B^-)$, so in expectation the gap is the CDF difference *at the calibration quantile*, while $d_K^+$ is its supremum. The tightness ratio $\text{gap}_{\text{mat}}/(d_K^+ + \frac{1}{n_B+1})$ is therefore close to the share of the laws' largest separation that happens to sit at the threshold, not a measure of sampling luck. T3-A reports both (`tightness`, `location_ratio`).

**Proposition 2 (images, not regions).** Calibration uses $m$ i.i.d. images; image $j$ has regions $(S_{jk}, \Lambda_{jk})_{k\le r}$. Put $P_j = \frac1r\sum_k\Lambda_{jk}$, $\bar p = \mathbb E P_j > 0$, and let $F_A^+$ be the law of a positive region drawn with probability proportional to positives. For every $\eta\in(0,\bar p)$ and integer $K\ge1$, with $\epsilon_\eta = 2\eta/(\bar p - \eta)$,
$$\mathbb E[\text{miss}_B] \;\le\; \alpha + d_K^+ + \epsilon_\eta + \tfrac1K + 2(K+1)e^{-2m\eta^2}.$$

*Proof.* Let $A_j(t) = \frac1r\sum_k \Lambda_{jk}\mathbf 1\{S_{jk}\le t\}$, $\bar A = \frac1m\sum_j A_j$, $\hat p = \frac1m\sum_j P_j$, so the pooled positive CDF is the ratio of sums $\hat F^+ = \bar A/\hat p$ and its target is $F_A^+ = A/\bar p$ with $A = \mathbb E A_j$.
(1) *Algebra:* $\hat F^+ - F_A^+ = [(\bar A - A) + F_A^+(\bar p - \hat p)]/\hat p$, and $F_A^+ \le 1$.
(2) *Concentration:* $A_j(t), P_j \in [0,1]$ are i.i.d.; Hoeffding at $K$ grid points $t_i$ with $F_A^+(t_i) = i/K$ and once for $\hat p$, union bound: off an event of probability $2(K+1)e^{-2m\eta^2}$, $\hat p \ge \bar p - \eta$ and $|\hat F^+ - F_A^+| \le \epsilon_\eta$ at every grid point.
(3) *Grid:* both CDFs are non-decreasing, so $\sup_t(\hat F^+ - F_A^+) \le \epsilon_\eta + 1/K$.
(4) *Conformal step:* $\hat F^+(\tau_A^-) = (k-1)/N \le \alpha$, so $F_A^+(\tau_A^-) \le \alpha + \epsilon_\eta + 1/K$, and $F_B^+(\tau_A^-) \le F_A^+(\tau_A^-) + d_K^+$. On the bad event bound the miss rate by 1. $\square$

The penalty decays like $1/\sqrt m$ in **images** and blows up as $\bar p \to 0$ (D3's thin-group warning, in the label). It is vacuous below about 50 images, so at the LIDC bank's 41 calibration subjects it certifies nothing. **That is why T3-A's intervals come from a patient-level bootstrap that refits the threshold in every replicate** (`prereg.REFIT_THRESHOLD_PER_REPLICATE`), and why a pair counts against Theorem 1 only when its gap interval lies wholly above the $d_K^+$ interval. Holding the threshold fixed instead is D73's defect: on the harness banks it gives intervals a median 1.4–2.1 times too narrow, and up to 9 times in a thin-source cell (T3.6 §3, §4.1).

## 3. Step 2 — from the score laws to the operators

The coupling argument of T3.3 §3(i) bounds the *unconditional* law and cannot be applied to $F^+$ directly: under a common image the label itself changes with the operator, so $F_A^+$ and $F_B^+$ are laws of different subpopulations. The route is to split the flips off.

**Theorem 3.** On the coupled space, with $P_{\text{flip}} = P(\Lambda_A \ne \Lambda_B)$ and $P_\times(t) = P(t \text{ lies between } S_A \text{ and } S_B)$,
$$d_K^+ \;\le\; \frac{\sup_t |G_A(t) - G_B(t)| + |\pi_A - \pi_B|}{\pi_A} \;\le\; \frac{2P_{\text{flip}} + \sup_t P_\times(t)}{\pi_A}.$$

*Proof.* $F_A^+ - F_B^+ = (G_A - G_B)/\pi_A - F_B^+(\pi_A - \pi_B)/\pi_A$ exactly, and $0 \le F_B^+ \le 1$: the first inequality. Pointwise, $|\mathbf 1\{S_A\le t\}\Lambda_A - \mathbf 1\{S_B\le t\}\Lambda_B| \le \mathbf 1\{\Lambda_A\ne\Lambda_B\} + \mathbf 1\{t\text{ between } S_A, S_B\}$; take expectations, and $|\pi_A - \pi_B| \le P_{\text{flip}}$. $\square$

**Corollary 4 (the $\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}}$ rate).** Suppose (i) $\mathbb E|S_A - S_B| \le L_S\, d_{\text{op}}$ and $S_A$ has density at most $M_S$; (ii) $\mathbb E|E_A - E_B| \le L_\varepsilon\, d_{\text{op}}$ and $E_A$ has density at most $M_\varepsilon$ near $\varepsilon$. Then
$$\text{gap}_{\text{nom}} \;\le\; d_K^+ \;\le\; \frac{4\sqrt{2M_\varepsilon L_\varepsilon} + 2\sqrt{2M_S L_S}}{\pi_A}\,\sqrt{d_{\text{op}}(A,B)} \quad\text{(in expectation)}.$$

*Proof.* For any $\delta>0$, $P_\times(t) \le P(|S_A - S_B| > \delta) + P(S_A \in (t-\delta, t+\delta]) \le L_S d_{\text{op}}/\delta + 2M_S\delta$ by Markov; at $\delta = \sqrt{L_S d_{\text{op}}/(2M_S)}$ this is $2\sqrt{2M_SL_S d_{\text{op}}}$. The same argument on $E$ against $\varepsilon$ gives $P_{\text{flip}} \le 2\sqrt{2M_\varepsilon L_\varepsilon d_{\text{op}}}$. Substitute into Theorem 3. $\square$

What the assumptions cost, stated where the result is stated:
- **For a fixed solver only.** $E$ and $S$ are Lipschitz in $d_{\text{op}}$ with the reconstruction held fixed ($\|P_N^A - P_N^B\|$ is controlled by $d_{\text{op}}$), but $\hat x$ moves with the operator through the solver, which is a black box (D1). $L_S$ and $L_\varepsilon$ are therefore properties of the paired experiment, measured, not derived.
- **$\pi_A$ is operator-dependent** because $\varepsilon$ is one global value: calibrating at the mildest operator gives the loosest bound. On the harness, $\pi$ ranges from 0.000 to 0.70 across operators (T3.6 §4.4).
- **It is valid and far from tight.** On every harness bank the rate bound held and was never below 3.8 (a gap cannot exceed 1); its median is 11–63 times $d_K^+$ (T3.6 §3, §4.7). The constants are dominated by densities, which are large at the scale of scores. State the corollary as an *order* result; the quantities to report numerically are $d_K^+$ itself and Theorem 3's middle term.

**Proposition 5 (coupled bridges to $W_1$).** On the coupled space:

(a) $W_1(F_A, F_B) \le \mathbb E|S_A - S_B|$ for the unconditional laws (any coupling bounds $W_1$);

(b) for the label-conditional laws, with $H \ge \max(S_A, S_B)$,
$$W_1^+ \le \frac{\mathbb E\big[\Lambda_A\Lambda_B|S_A - S_B|\big] + \mathbb E\big[\Lambda_A(1-\Lambda_B)(H - S_A)\big] + \mathbb E\big[(1-\Lambda_A)\Lambda_B(H - S_B)\big] + |\pi_A - \pi_B|\,\big(H - \mathbb E[S_B\mid\Lambda_B]\big)}{\pi_A},$$
and the same with $A$ and $B$ exchanged.

*Proof of (b).* From Theorem 3's identity, $\int|F_A^+ - F_B^+| \le [\int|G_A - G_B| + |\pi_A - \pi_B|\int_{-\infty}^H F_B^+]/\pi_A$, and $\int_{-\infty}^{H} F_B^+ = H - \mathbb E[S_B \mid \Lambda_B]$ for a law supported below $H$. Pointwise, $\int_{-\infty}^H |\mathbf 1\{S_A\le t\}\Lambda_A - \mathbf 1\{S_B\le t\}\Lambda_B|\,dt$ equals $|S_A - S_B|$ when both labels are 1, $H - S_A$ (or $H - S_B$) when only one is, and 0 otherwise. Take expectations. $\square$

(a) is the bridge T3.3 §3(i) uses; (b) is its analogue for the law the calibrator actually uses. Both are theorems, so in T3-A's table (rows 4) a violation is a code defect, not a finding: both held at every pair on every harness bank. (b) charges a flipped region the whole score range, so it is loose exactly where flips are common. With Lipschitz scores, (a) gives $W_1 \le L_S d_{\text{op}}$, and T3.3 §3(iii)'s tent argument gives $d_K \le \sqrt{D\,W_1}$ with $D = \sup|q_A - q_B|$, hence the unconditional chain $\text{gap} \le d_K \le \sqrt{D L_S d_{\text{op}}}$. **$D$ is a smoothing-scale-dependent constant** (it moves by about 2 across a 16× bandwidth change, T3.5 §3) and is not reported as a number; $d_K$ and $W_1$ are.

## 4. What does and does not depend on the prior's alignment (T3-C)

The medical priors moved $\cos\theta$ (the alignment between the reconstruction's hidden content and the truth's) from ≈0 to +0.60 (MRI) and +0.88 (CT). T3.6 Part II (§10) audits every Track 3 step for the orthogonal-error approximation $E \approx \mathrm{hypot}(s, c)$. The result, for this section:

- **No statement above uses it.** Theorems 1 and 3, Corollary 4 and Propositions 2 and 5 are distribution-free in the score and the label; they hold at any alignment. Only the *values* of $L_S, L_\varepsilon, M_S, M_\varepsilon$ and $\pi$ change, and those are measured on each bank, never carried over.
- **The direction statements do.** Which ordered pairs under-cover is set by how the threshold orders the operators ($\text{sign}(\text{gap}_{\text{mat}}) = \text{sign}(\tau_A - \tau_B)$, D89), and that ordering is a property of the alignment regime: on the face prior $\tau$ falls as the operator hides more, on the LIDC prior it rises (T2.11 §1j). T3.6 §11 gives a mechanism for both, as a hypothesis with its falsifier, from one exact split, $\text{err}^2 = (s\cos\theta - c)^2 + (s\sin\theta)^2$. The paper should state the bound per ordered pair, as it is, and never say "deploying to a more ill-posed operator under-covers" without naming the prior.

## 5. Relation to the classical bound

Barber et al.'s Theorem 4 (T3.1 §3.3) bounds the gap by total variation between the *data* distributions. Applied to the score sequence (T3.3 §2) it gives $\text{gap} \le 2\,d_{TV}(Q_A, Q_B)$ on the unconditional score laws. Theorem 1 is tighter in three ways at once: Kolmogorov instead of total variation ($d_K \le d_{TV}$), no factor 2, and the label-conditional law the calibrator uses. By data processing the score-level $d_{TV}$ is at most the data-level one, so a score-level TV that is already loose (T3-A row 6, histogram TV at several binnings) means the classical bound is looser still.

**Do not write:**
- *"The bound predicts the gap."* On the LIDC bank the operator distance does not predict the gap under the frozen form (T1-B, 0 of 4 cells). The bound is an upper envelope, and row 5 of T3-A says how well the distance orders the *law* distances, which is a different claim.
- *"Gap ≤ d_K"* as a finding. For split conformal with continuous scores it is close to a tautology (Theorem 1's proof is three lines); its value is as an implementation check and as the tightness ratio.
- A number for $D$ without its bandwidth.
- *"Under-coverage occurs when deploying to a more ill-posed operator"* without naming the prior (§4).

## 8. Limitations, stated where the result is

- **Known, linear operators** at calibration and deployment; the coupling is the crossed bank's.
- **Fixed solver.** Step 2's constants are measured per solver; nothing is derived about solver stability.
- **The label uses ground truth** and a design-split quantile; deployment never sees it.
- **Clustered regions.** Proposition 2 is vacuous at 41 calibration subjects; the bootstrap is what supports the per-pair intervals, and it is only as good as the assumption that patients are exchangeable.
- **The CT range/null split is approximate** (CG at its iteration cap, additivity 2–12%, T2-A5), so $E$, $s$ and $\cos\theta$ inherit that error on CT.
- **$\varepsilon$ is global**, so $\pi$ is operator-dependent and the rate's $1/\pi_A$ is vacuous at mild source operators.
