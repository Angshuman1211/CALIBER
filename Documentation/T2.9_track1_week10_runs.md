## 0. Track 1's question: how the D-033 shares were computed

**At image level, per item. Not as a mean of per-region ratios, so the bias does not apply.**

`decompose_samples` (`operators/decomposition.py`) takes the whole-image deviation of the posterior mean, δ = mean reconstruction − truth, and splits it once with the operator's projectors. The share is the energy ratio ‖P_N δ‖² / (‖P_R δ‖² + ‖P_N δ‖²), one value per item. `aggregate_reports` then averages it over items, skipping NaNs. Region boundaries never enter, so the non-locality of P_N cannot inflate it. That is the same global quantity `solver_ablation.py` reconstructs by area-weighting the bank's per-region `err_free²` / `err_total²` over each volume.

Two things do differ from what the ablation measures, and neither is a bias:

- **Scale.** D-033's 0.817 / 0.852 were 2 items × 2 draws at 128², R=4. The ablation measures its own premise on the full bank at 256².
- **Aggregate.** A mean over items, against the script's median over volume-operator cells.

The new bank re-measures the premise either way (§1), so D68 does not rest on the Week 3 numbers.

## 1. The solver ablation (D68)

**The DPS arm** (D-066) matches the DDNM bank of record in everything but the solver: the same prior, 24 steps, 4 draws, 256², the same 199 fastMRI volumes and patient split. Guidance is ζ = 5, the value D68's premise was measured at. It covers the `random1d` family at R2 / R4 / R8: 597 pairs, 152,832 regions, ~29 s/pair on the RTX 3050, built in three stretches from one checkpoint (§8).

### The premise holds, and is widest where DPS is least converged

| Null-error share, area-weighted per volume | median | 10th pct | min | volumes below D-033's 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DDNM, all 9 operators (1,791 cells) | **0.994** | 0.975 | 0.957 | 0 |
| DPS, `random1d R2` | 0.432 | 0.384 | 0.019 | **196 of 199** |
| DPS, `random1d R4` | 0.691 | 0.632 | 0.348 | 1 of 199 |
| DPS, `random1d R8` | 0.846 | 0.786 | 0.609 | 0 of 199 |
| DPS, all (597 cells) | **0.690** | 0.410 | 0.019 | 197 of 597 |

The gap is 0.304, far above `MIN_SHARE_GAP` (0.05), so the script proceeds. (The per-region ratio exceeds 1 in 22.9% of DDNM's regions and 1.0% of DPS's, as `P_N`'s non-locality predicts. §0's answer is why the image-level share is the right one.)

**At R2, the calibration operator, DPS fails D-033's admissibility gate on 196 of 199 volumes.** More than half its error there is in the *range*, the component the measurements pin down: an unconverged fit, which is what D-033 exists to exclude. The premise gap is therefore widest exactly where the DPS bank is least admissible. That is not an accident. A solver that leaves room for the decomposition to act is one that has not converged, and ζ = 5 is not DPS's optimum (D-031: unbracketed above 20).

### The script's verdict, and why it does not stand

`solver_ablation.py` prints **"D68 ANSWERED, POSITIVE"**. On DPS the score beats `s_disagree` by ≥ 0.05 AUC at 3 of 3 operators and the anatomy control at 3 of 3. On DDNM it beats `s_disagree` at 1 of 9. Its last line says to read the direction label first, because under a magnitude label an excess "can be the identity rather than a detector". Read both ways, per bank, each label valued on that bank's own design split (ε = 0.906 DDNM, 0.726 DPS):

| Label | Operator | Score − `s_disagree`, DDNM | Score − `s_disagree`, DPS | DPS − DDNM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| magnitude (`err_free > ε`) | R2 | +0.091 | +0.059 | −0.033 |
| magnitude | R4 | +0.024 | +0.077 | +0.053 |
| magnitude | R8 | +0.027 | +0.059 | +0.033 |
| **direction** (cos θ, D74) | R2 | +0.059 | **−0.034** | −0.092 |
| **direction** | R4 | −0.001 | **−0.014** | −0.013 |
| **direction** | R8 | +0.000 | **−0.002** | −0.003 |

- **The positive is the magnitude label's.** On the DPS bank that label is the identity even more tightly than on DDNM (Spearman of `err_free` with hypot(`s_free`, `free_content`) = +0.9957). The score contains `s_free`; `s_disagree` does not.
- **Under the direction label, the decomposition does not help on DPS at any operator.** The score ranks *below* `s_disagree` at all three, by −0.034, −0.014 and −0.002. It is at chance there (AUC 0.516–0.562) and beats the anatomy control at 0 of 3 (`label_comparison.py` on the DPS bank), as on DDNM and CT (§4).
- **Even on the magnitude label, the solver difference is mostly inside the noise.** Of the three matched operators, DPS's excess is larger at R4 (+0.053) and R8 (+0.033), and smaller at R2 (−0.033). The AUROC split noise floor is 0.034–0.048 (D-062). The "3 of 3 against 1 of 9" count comes from the 0.05 cut-off.

**D68, answered: negative on the label that is not the identity.** DPS is the configuration where the decomposition has the most room. On a label it cannot satisfy by construction, the score does no better than the dispersion inside it. The paper should say so as a finding, as the script's own negative branch puts it, not claim a positive. The one place the score beats `s_disagree` on the direction label is DDNM at R2 (+0.059). That is the operator with fewest positives, and a single cell is not a claim.

`solver_ablation.py` values ε once on the *pooled* design rows of both banks (0.856). That gives each arm a different positive rate than its own split would (DDNM 0.906, DPS 0.726). The per-bank table above avoids it; the conclusion is the same either way.

## 2. The baseline on the coverage axis (D73)

`transfer_baseline.py` asks whose threshold survives an operator change. For each detector, it fits a split-conformal threshold twice: once at the calibration operator, and once at the deployment operator. The coverage lost between the two is that detector's **penalty**, and `delta = penalty(baseline) − penalty(CALIBER)`.

### 2a. CT, against `s_disagree`

The CT bank has no `patient_id`. CT has one slice per subject and the subject is the patient, so `sample_id` is D-027's cluster, and the run uses `--cluster sample_id`.

| Deployed at | Asc. (from v180): penalty base / CALIBER | delta [95% CI] | Desc. (from v30): penalty base / CALIBER | delta [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `limited a120` | 0.082 / 0.071 | +0.011 [−0.039, +0.046] | 0.065 / −0.103 | +0.168 [+0.094, +0.312] |
| `limited a150` | 0.031 / 0.034 | −0.003 [−0.048, +0.035] | 0.016 / −0.095 | +0.111 [+0.039, +0.266] |
| `limited a90` | 0.122 / 0.141 | −0.019 [−0.086, +0.036] | 0.100 / −0.122 | +0.221 [+0.154, +0.324] |
| `sparse v120` | −0.015 / 0.019 | −0.035 [−0.104, +0.008] | −0.030 / −0.115 | +0.085 [+0.008, +0.239] |
| `sparse v90` | −0.017 / 0.070 | **−0.087** [−0.191, −0.034] | −0.032 / −0.083 | +0.051 [−0.023, +0.177] |
| `sparse v60` | −0.010 / 0.136 | **−0.146** [−0.261, −0.067] | −0.023 / −0.101 | +0.078 [+0.026, +0.178] |

| `sparse v30` / `v180` | 0.022 / 0.207 | **−0.184** [−0.309, −0.091] | −0.021 / −0.103 | +0.081 [+0.025, +0.232] |

The script's verdicts are "the baseline's threshold transfers better at 4 of 8" ascending and "CALIBER's transfers better at 7 of 8" descending. **The second is not a transfer result, and the reason matters for how D73 reads anywhere.**

**The penalty is signed, so over-coverage counts as transferring well.** Descending, CALIBER's penalty is negative at every operator: its transferred threshold covers *more* than its own in-group threshold does. The flag areas show how. Its transferred arm flags ≥ 99.7% of all regions at 6 of 8 operators (0.565 at v45, 0.824 at v60), against 0.32–0.86 in-group. A detector that flags everything has coverage 1.000 and a negative penalty. The `interpretable` gate checks the *in-group* arm only (it must reach nominal and not be trivial), so the transferred arm's flag area never enters. The baseline, by contrast, flags almost the same area either way: transferred 0.080–0.391 against in-group 0.076–0.519 (`runs/w10_analysis/transfer_ct_arms.csv`).

Read two-sided, on |penalty| (coverage lost in either direction), **the baseline transfers as well or better at all 16 CT cells and worse at none**. Ascending, it is better by more than 0.02 at 4 cells and within 0.02 at the other 4. Descending, it is better at all 8. The largest |penalty| is 0.122 for `s_disagree` and 0.207 for the CALIBER score. On CT, then, the composite's threshold transfers worse than its own dispersion component's. It loses up to a fifth of its coverage going to sparse views, and it over-flags coming back.

## 3. Tool 2 on CT, with and without its nearest neighbour (D75)

**The run first needed the full distance matrix.** A leave-one-operator-out fold asks for the distance between every two operators. `ct_distances.csv` held only the pairs from 180 and 30 views (17 of the 36 unordered pairs). `tool2_holdout.py` computed each missing pair inline, on the CPU and without memoisation, so the first attempt had produced nothing after 33 minutes and was stopped. `ct_distances.py --all-pairs` now fills the matrix on the GPU: 19 pairs, 37 minutes, two measures (`null_space_growth`, `null_space_projection`). It computes one order per pair, because the readers look either order up. The metric is symmetric, and `null_space_growth` is exactly antisymmetric on the same seeded probes. The one pair present in both orders, from two separate runs, agrees in reverse to 5×10⁻⁴. With the matrix cached, each Tool 2 run takes about 30 seconds.

Interior folds: 7 (the two endpoints, v180 and v30, are outside what weighting can reach).

| Interior folds (7) | `--exclude-nearest 0`: coverage | flag area | valid folds | `--exclude-nearest 1`: coverage | flag area | valid folds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| matched | 0.890 | 0.260 | 6 | 0.890 | 0.260 | 6 |
| nearest | 0.840 | 0.276 | 5 | 0.800 | 0.240 | 4 |
| **weighted (Tool 2)** | **0.950** | 0.354 | **6** | **0.929** | 0.343 | **6** |
| group_weighted | 0.840 | 0.276 | 5 | 0.800 | 0.240 | 4 |
| mondrian | 1.000 | 1.000 | 7 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 7 |

- **Tool 2 is valid in 6 of 7 interior folds in both settings, and in neither is it valid everywhere.** It misses at v45 on the full grid (0.820 [0.691, 0.898]) and at v60 with the nearest neighbour removed (0.777 [0.618, 0.872]).
- **Its advantage over `nearest` is larger on the sparse grid, which is D75's point.** With the nearest neighbour dropped, `nearest` collapses at v60 (0.390), at v90 (0.768) and at 90° (0.797). Tool 2 holds v90 (0.976) and 90° (0.988). Interior mean coverage: 0.929 against 0.800.
- **It buys that coverage with area.** It flags 0.343 against `nearest`'s 0.240, and at 90° it flags 0.83 of all regions.
- **`mondrian` is valid in every fold only trivially.** A held-out group has no calibration rows, so the Mondrian arm flags everything (area 1.000). The script's "valid in every fold: mondrian" line is that, not a result.
- **`group_weighted` matches `nearest` to within 0.002 in every fold.** Its retained-group mass is 1.000, so the group weighting puts everything on one group.

## 4. Label comparison on CT (D74)

`label_comparison.py` scores the CALIBER score against two labels, per operator, with direction-agnostic AUCs and the anatomy control `free_content`.

| | Absolute (`err_free > 0.360`) | Alignment (cos θ < 0.0023, D74) |
|---|---|---|
| what the label inherits | Spearman(`err_free`, hypot(`s_free`, `free_content`)) = **+0.9905** | Spearman(cos θ, `s_free`) = **+0.017** |
| score AUC, 9 operators | 0.936 – 0.997 | 0.504 – 0.578 |

| excess over anatomy | +0.137 to +0.219 | −0.081 to +0.022 |

cos θ is defined on 100% of CT regions (sd 0.162); its threshold is the design split's q25. **Against a label that thresholds a direction rather than a magnitude, the score ranks hallucinations at chance and never beats the anatomy control.** The 0.94–1.00 under the absolute label is the identity D67 describes, now measured on CT as well as MRI.

## 5. Weight sensitivity on MRI (D76)

D54 found that 8 design patients cannot select `w_free`. This sweeps it and recomputes the paired coverage restoration, gap = coverage(group-conditional) − coverage(transferred), from `random1d R2` to `R8`.

| w_free | 0.0 | 0.2 | 0.4 | **0.5 (equal)** | 0.6 | 0.8 | 0.9 | 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| descending (R8 → R2) | +0.178 | +0.147 | +0.103 | **+0.075** [+0.042, +0.148] | +0.041 | +0.005 | −0.015 | −0.031 |
| ascending (R2 → R8) | −0.101 | −0.085 | −0.065 | **−0.050** [−0.068, −0.035] | −0.037 | −0.007 | +0.014 | +0.035 |

- **The restoration claim depends on the weighting.** Descending, where naive transfer under-covers, group-conditional calibration restores coverage at w_free 0 to 0.8 (9 of 11 grid points, every interval above zero). It reverses sign at 0.9 and 1.0, where the score is nearly `s_free` alone.
- **Ascending, there is nothing to restore** at most weightings. The transferred threshold over-covers, so the gap is negative from 0 to 0.8.
- **At equal weights, the shipped default, the claim holds in the direction that needs it.** It is not robust to the weighting, and D54 leaves the weighting unselected. That belongs in the limitations, as the script says.

**Flag area at matched coverage 0.90** (D39 with D51's gate) is smallest for `s_free` alone at all 9 operators. The CALIBER score sits between `s_free` and `s_disagree` at every operator; at `random1d R2` the three are 0.092, 0.143 and 0.409. The comparison cannot be won by flagging nothing, but it is not independent of the label either. `s_free` is the component the magnitude label's identity runs through (§4), so a smaller area for it at matched coverage is partly that identity again.

## 6. Curve collapse (D72)

`curve_collapse.py` tests whether one gap-against-distance line fits both modalities. Its verdict rests on magnitude: one curve must cost less than D69's 0.05 coverage over two.

**It first needed the MRI distances and two fixes.**

- `ct_distances.py` on the MRI bank (Track 1's fix for non-CT banks) took seconds, from both `random1d R2` and `R8`, so both directions have their distances.
- **The script matched each coverage row to a distance by the deployment operator alone, taking the first calibration in the CSV.** The CT CSV holds three calibrations (180, 30 and 120 views). A row calibrated at 30 views and deployed at 90 would have been plotted at its distance *from 180*. It now matches the row's own (calibrated_at, deployed_at) pair. A pair stored in the other order is negated, because the measure is signed. A table that does not name its calibration is refused when the CSV is ambiguous.
- **The Week 8 coverage tables name the arms `naive` / `group`,** where the script expected `naive_transferred`. Both names are now accepted. `--direction` selects one direction.

| | CT slope [CI], Spearman | MRI slope [CI], Spearman | single-curve cost | F, p | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| both directions | +0.244 [+0.190, +0.293], +0.965 | +0.180 [+0.095, +0.258], +0.743 | +0.0015 | 0.55, 0.58 | collapses |
| ascending | +0.692 [+0.431, +0.868], +0.929 | +0.083 [−0.349, +1.713], +0.340 | +0.0079 | 1.09, 0.37 | collapses |
| descending | +0.021 [−0.000, +0.036], +0.764 | +0.050 [−0.154, +0.168], +0.393 | +0.0028 | 1.42, 0.28 | collapses |

**The test says "collapses" in every reading, and that should not be read as collapse demonstrated.** D70 predicted it would fail. It passes because it cannot reject, not because one curve fits:

- **The two separate lines fit badly themselves.** With both directions, the separate fits' RMSE is 0.075, already above the 0.05 floor the single curve is judged against. One line costs little *over two poor lines*.
- **MRI's curve is barely ordered by distance within a direction** (Spearman +0.34 ascending, +0.39 descending). Ascending, its slope interval runs from −0.35 to +1.71, so it overlaps any CT slope.
- With both directions pooled, MRI's correlation rises to +0.743. Most of that comes from the sign of the distance separating the two directions, not from order within either.

**The control passes too.** D-064 asked for `group_conditional` as the control, where a fix should show no trend:

| `group_conditional` | CT slope [CI], Spearman | MRI slope [CI], Spearman | single-curve cost | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| both directions | +0.003 [−0.018, +0.022], +0.174 | +0.009 [−0.043, +0.065], +0.075 | +0.0006 | collapses |
| ascending | +0.064 [−0.110, +0.181], +0.310 | +0.087 [−0.122, +0.211], +0.454 | +0.0006 | collapses |
| descending | +0.050 [−0.077, +0.119], +0.286 | +0.026 [−0.110, +0.239], +0.101 | +0.0007 | collapses |

The fix shows no trend in either modality, as it should. Every slope interval spans zero, against the naive arm's CT slope of +0.692 ascending. That collapse is two flat lines agreeing, which D-064 named in advance. **So the test returns the same verdict for the claim and for the control.** What separates them is the slope itself, not the verdict. A collapse verdict would need an adequacy condition alongside the magnitude one: the separate fits' own RMSE under the floor, or a bound on the slope interval's width. That is Track 1's call (§10).

## 8. Running it on this machine

- **Commit, not the GPU, was the limit.** With Chrome, VS Code, the ChatGPT app and other sessions open, the machine had 2–7 GB of commit free out of 32–34 GB. On Windows a GPU allocation also needs backing commit. DPS failed three times at the start of sampling: a CUDA OOM with 3.2 GB of the card still free, an "unknown error" with 1.9 GB of commit available, and an OOM at 4.9 GB. Measured steady, the runner commits ~6.2 GB on the GPU path, so the preflight's 6.4 GB estimate is about right there too. It refused (6.35 < 6.4) only because it is checked after the runner's own imports. The DPS config sets `skip_memory_check` in the file, because it is part of the config hash and a resume must see the same hash. What makes the run fit is the launcher, which waits for 7 GB of free commit before every (re)start; that left 1.4 GB to spare. It took closing the browser, the ChatGPT app and Teams.
- **DPS on the RTX 3050:** ~27.5 s/pair steady, 3.0 GB of GPU memory, ~2.2× DDNM. The bank is 597 pairs, ~4.6 h.
- **The bank was built in three stretches** (pairs 1–29, 30–330, 331–597), resumed each time from its checkpoint under the same config hash (D-042). It stopped twice: once when the first version of the battery guard read a failed battery query as an empty battery, and once by hand for a power cut. From pair 331, and for the calibration-side dispersion, the runner ran with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`. That is numpy's CPU maths library limited to one thread, which cut the runner's commit from ~6.2 to ~5.25 GB: imports alone measured 1.58 vs 0.98 GB. It is not part of the config. The GPU work, which is all of DPS, is unaffected; the CPU-side scoring could differ only at rounding level.
- **The disks were nearly full.** C: had 9.1 GB free and D: 1.6 GB. With the paging file system-managed on C:, the commit limit had fallen from 34 GB in the morning to 25 GB. It took emptying the Recycle Bin and quitting OneDrive and the Copilot app to get the run going again.
- **The queue** (`runs/w10_gpu_queue.ps1`, local): DPS bank, then calibration-side dispersion (828 pairs, ~2.9 h), then the ablation and the MRI transfer. It keeps the machine awake, stops the running step below 20% battery (losing ≤ 10 pairs), and resumes from the checkpoint at 60%.
- **A stopped background job kept running.** Stopping the first CPU chain's task killed its shell's tracking but not the loop. The loop went on to compute CT distances on the CPU for 40 minutes, and that is what held memory during the first DPS attempt. Found by checking the command lines of the running processes; it was stopped. No result was affected: the rerun outputs are byte-identical.

## Honest limitations

- **The D73 verdicts are signed.** As implemented they favour whichever detector over-flags after transfer (§2). The two-sided reading here is descriptive, computed from the point estimates, without intervals.
