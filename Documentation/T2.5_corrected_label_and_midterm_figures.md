## 1. The label changed twice, and the result changed with it

T2.4 §8 reported O3 in the descending direction, against the ratio-only label `err_free / free_content > 1.75`. D-059 found that label defective: its denominator drives it harder than its numerator. **On this bank the defect reproduces, and is worse than on M4Raw.** Pooled over test regions, `-free_content` alone ranks the label at AUROC **0.833** against the numerator's 0.721 (M4Raw: 0.826, 0.741). It labelled **60.4%** of all regions hallucinated.

D-059's first fix, a floor on `err_free`, was wrong too. It kept exactly the regions where `err_free` approaches `s_free` (D-037's identity), so `s_free` reached AUROC 0.99–1.00, the score graded against itself. D-060 replaced it with the **candidacy gate**: a region counts only if its `free_content` exceeds the 33rd percentile on the design split. The reason is physical. A region where the operator hides almost nothing cannot host a null-space hallucination.

**Implementation.** `derive_score_and_label` takes `min_free_content` and passes it to the gate `label_hallucinated` already had (its 1e-3 default is a numerical guard that never fires). `design_quantile` computes the gate's value from **design rows only** and refuses a bank that has none (D-051), so the label never sees the patients whose coverage it scores. Every script that reports a label-dependent number now applies it by default: `coverage_by_patient.py`, `component_auroc.py`, `seed_noise_floor.py`, and `dispersion_baseline.py evaluate`. `--candidacy-quantile 0` reproduces the old label, with a warning, for comparison only.

## 4. The gate is not the whole fix: the label check within operators

D-060 judged the gate by one number, pooled AUROC of `-free_content` over all regions, which came to 0.499 on M4Raw ("exactly chance"). On this bank that number is **0.395**, and the per-operator values in §3 run from **0.321 to 0.608**. Chance is not reached in either direction. Splitting the label into candidates and non-candidates shows why. Below, everything is on test regions, with D-059's own decomposition:

| Regions | n | Pos. rate | AUROC(`-free_content`) | AUROC(`err_free`) | ρ(ratio, `free_content`) | ρ(ratio, `err_free`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All, ratio-only label | 209,664 | 0.577 | **0.833** | 0.721 | −0.690 | +0.508 |
| Candidates (`free_content` > q33) | 137,109 | 0.415 | 0.752 | **0.797** | −0.503 | +0.578 |
| Candidates, top tercile of `free_content` | 69,888 | 0.233 | 0.672 | **0.905** | −0.327 | +0.662 |
| `random1d R2` candidates | 12,743 | 0.147 | 0.726 | **0.865** | −0.489 | +0.397 |
| `random1d R8` candidates | 16,510 | 0.671 | **0.920** | 0.651 | −0.771 | +0.463 |
| `variable_density2d R2` candidates | 12,766 | 0.052 | 0.829 | **0.878** | −0.668 | **+0.026** |

The gate does two things, and the pooled AUROC adds them together:

1. It **forces non-candidates negative**, and those are the lowest-`free_content` regions. That alone pushes AUROC(`-free_content`) below 0.5.
2. **Inside the candidates the ratio is still denominator-led**, at 0.752. Pooled, the numerator has overtaken it (0.797), so the gate is a real improvement. Per operator it has not: at `random1d R8`, `-free_content` still beats `err_free` by 0.920 to 0.651.

A pooled value near 0.5 can therefore be these two effects cancelling, with the artefact still present. Choosing q to hit 0.5 tunes the cancellation and does not remove the artefact. Within-candidate, per-operator numbers are the better check. This matters most for the headline cell. At `variable_density2d R2`, among candidates, the ratio is uncorrelated with `err_free` (ρ = +0.026) and driven by `free_content` (ρ = −0.668), so which 5% of regions count as hallucinated there is decided mostly by how little each one hides. **The 0.328 is measured correctly against the ruled label, and the ruled label at that operator still measures the denominator.** The label is Track 1's call (D-059, D-060), so it is reported as ruled, logged as P-31, and sent to them (§10).

## 6. Posterior-dispersion baseline (T2.6)

A non-conformal reference detector: the pixel-wise standard deviation across the posterior draws, averaged over each region and divided by the reference intensity, so it shares `s_disagree`'s scale. It needs no calibration data, so it runs on test patients only.

The bank stores `s_disagree`, the spread *in the null space*, not the full-image spread the literature's detector uses. So `dispersion_baseline.py generate` **regenerates** the test-side draws. Draws are a pure function of the config and the pair (D-041), so the script proves it gets the same draws back before trusting anything: it recomputes `s_disagree` from them and compares with the bank. On the smoke run the difference was **0.0**, bit for bit. It saves every 10 pairs and resumes, retries a non-finite pair with the same seed (P-29), and has 9 tests for AUROC, average precision (ties broken pessimistically) and the dispersion itself.

`evaluate` scores dispersion, `s_disagree` and the CALIBER score on the same regions under the D-060 label, with patient-bootstrap intervals. The full run regenerated all 819 test-side pairs (91 volumes × 9 operators) in 2 h 52 min on the GPU. The reproduction check matched the bank over all 209,664 regions, with median and 99th-percentile relative difference **0.0**.

**On this solver, the literature's detector and CALIBER's disagreement component measure nearly the same thing.** Dispersion is below `s_disagree` at all nine operators, by 0.001–0.012. That is expected from DDNM: it makes every draw consistent with the measured data, so the draws can differ only in what the operator does not measure. Full-image spread is then almost entirely null-space spread, and the two scores differ mainly in how that spread is reduced to a region. So for a range–null-space solver, posterior dispersion is not an independent baseline. Separating the two needs a solver that does not enforce data consistency exactly, such as DPS (D-033).

The CALIBER score is within 0.025 of dispersion everywhere. It is ahead at the R2 operators (0.762 against 0.738 at `equispaced1d R2`, 0.864 against 0.851 at `random1d R2`) and up to 0.012 behind at R4 and R8, where `s_residual` pulls the combination down (§3). Average precision shows the same pattern (`runs/w05_analysis/dispersion_eval.csv`). The intervals are unpaired and overlap everywhere, so the 9-of-9 ordering is the better evidence for the small dispersion < `s_disagree` gap.

### 6.1 The published Hallucination Index [22]

The second Week 6 baseline is the published **Hallucination Index** (Tivnan et al., MICCAI 2024). It is the Hellinger distance between the distribution of a model's reconstructions and a zero-hallucination reference: the ground truth plus noise at the same apparent level, both taken as Gaussian. It reads as the share of the error that is hallucination rather than noise. **It needs the ground truth**, so it cannot flag anything at deployment, and the blueprint lists it as non-conformal context. It enters here as what it is: an independent, published *definition* of hallucination, to set beside the project's label and its score.

**Adaptation, per region, from cached columns (no GPU).** `hallucination_index` in `evaluation/baselines.py` makes four choices:

- **Reference noise.** The reference shares the reconstructions' covariance, "the same apparent noise". With equal covariances the closed form is `H = sqrt(1 − exp(−D²/8))`, where D² is the mean error's Mahalanobis distance. A test checks it against the Hellinger distance integrated numerically.
- **Covariance.** One variance per region, the dispersion run's spread: the regional form of the paper's single noise spectrum per image.
- **Monte Carlo correction.** The mean of 4 draws (the paper used 64) carries σ²/4 of Monte Carlo error, which is subtracted, so a region with no hallucination scores 0 on average and not 64. A simulated test checks this too.
- **Ranking.** Hellinger distance saturates over 256 pixels: 57% of regions have HI > 0.99. So regions are ranked by the hallucination-to-noise ratio (HNR, squared bias over noise variance), of which HI is a monotone function.

A region counts as hallucinated by the index when HNR > 1, meaning more of its error is bias than noise.

**What it found on the 819 test pairs:**

| | Whole image | Interior only (outer two region rings dropped) |
|---|---|---|
| Regions the index calls hallucinated | 5.3% | **0.9%** (0.1–0.2% at most operators, **6.1% at `variable_density2d R2`**) |
| Agreement with the D-060 label (Cohen's kappa, per operator) | −0.11 to +0.03 | −0.03 to +0.01 |
| AUROC of HNR for the D-060 label | 0.26–0.46 | 0.33–0.55 |

1. **On fastMRI the index mostly flags background.** 91% of its positives sit in the image border, which is 44% of regions, and their posterior spread is half the typical value. fastMRI's ground truth is itself noisy there. DDNM's prior removes that noise, the four draws agree it is gone, and the index reads a confident deviation from the truth as hallucination. The index assumes a noiseless reference, which this substrate does not have.
2. **Inside the anatomy it finds almost none.** DDNM's mean error is mostly within its own posterior spread. The exception is `variable_density2d R2`, the operator where naive transfer collapses (§2): there the draws agree with each other but not with the truth. Every spread-based score ranks those regions lowest (dispersion AUROC 0.14, `s_disagree` 0.15, CALIBER's score 0.17, `s_free` 0.20).
3. **It does not agree with the project's label** (kappa ≈ 0). The two measure different things: error relative to *posterior noise*, against error relative to *hidden content*. Neither validates the other, so the index is not the independent check P-31 needs, at least on a substrate whose ground truth is noisy and with 4 draws.

What it does say is narrower and worth recording. **Hallucinations the draws agree on are invisible to spread-based scores by construction.** Where they occur (`variable_density2d R2`), `s_disagree` and dispersion rank them last, and `s_free`, which does not depend on the spread, does barely better. Rows are in `runs/w05_analysis/hallucination_index.csv`.

## 7. Seed noise floor (D-057, D-059)

Every ablation cell in T1.4 §6 is read against the noise in a coverage number. T2.4 measured the patient-split part (sd 0.028 across 13 splits, under the old label). This measures the mask-seed part: `w05_seed_noise_floor_gpu.yaml` and `w05_seed_noise_floor_R8_gpu.yaml` run `random1d R4` and `R8` at seeds 1 and 2 on all 199 volumes, and `seed_noise_floor.py` merges them with seed 0 from the main bank. The merge is checked, not assumed: every seed must cover the same volumes on the same split side. The script answers three questions:

1. **Per seed.** Calibrated and scored on itself. The spread across seeds is the floor.
2. **Is a seed change a shift?** Seed 0's threshold is carried to seeds 1 and 2, and D-023's pooled group is checked alongside.
3. **Seed against split.** Per-seed coverage under the same 13 splits, so both noise sources are measured on one footing.

The gate's value comes from the main bank's design split, so the seed analysis uses the same label as §2 and does not re-fit its own.

**`random1d R4`**, seeds 0–2, 199 volumes, 44 test patients:

| | Coverage (95% interval by patient) | Flag area |
|---|---|---|
| Seed 0, calibrated on itself | 0.854 [0.792, 0.905] | 0.719 |
| Seed 1, calibrated on itself | 0.863 [0.807, 0.911] | 0.710 |
| Seed 2, calibrated on itself | 0.848 [0.784, 0.903] | 0.715 |
| Seed 0's threshold, deployed on seed 1 | 0.836 [0.771, 0.891] | 0.668 |
| Seed 0's threshold, deployed on seed 2 | 0.832 [0.763, 0.891] | 0.688 |
| Pooled over seeds (D-023), on seeds 0 / 1 / 2 | 0.868 / 0.852 / 0.847 | 0.743 / 0.691 / 0.712 |

Over 13 patient splits × 3 seeds, mean coverage is 0.895. **The sd across seeds within a split is 0.005, and across splits within a seed it is 0.034.**

**At R4 the seed is a negligible noise source, and the patient split is about seven times larger.** The floor an ablation effect has to clear is the split's ~0.03, not the seed's. Carrying seed 0's threshold to another seed costs ~0.02 of coverage at the point estimate, well inside every interval, so D-023's treatment of the seed as within-group variation holds at R4. The project split reads low here too (0.85 against 0.895), as in §2.1.

**`random1d R8`**, seeds 0–2, the descending calibration operator:

| | Coverage (95% interval by patient) | Flag area |
|---|---|---|
| Seed 0, calibrated on itself | 0.836 [0.767, 0.897] | 0.791 |
| Seed 1, calibrated on itself | 0.836 [0.766, 0.897] | 0.788 |
| Seed 2, calibrated on itself | 0.838 [0.767, 0.900] | 0.775 |
| Seed 0's threshold, deployed on seed 1 | 0.867 [0.807, 0.919] | 0.833 |
| Seed 0's threshold, deployed on seed 2 | 0.828 [0.755, 0.893] | 0.760 |
| Pooled over seeds (D-023), on seeds 0 / 1 / 2 | 0.828 / 0.858 / 0.820 | 0.779 / 0.821 / 0.750 |

Over 13 patient splits × 3 seeds, mean coverage is 0.887. **The sd across seeds within a split is 0.003, and across splits within a seed it is 0.048.**

**D-059 expected the seed spread to grow with acceleration, and it does not.** Calibrated on itself, each seed reaches the same coverage to within 0.002 (sd 0.001), tighter than R4's 0.007. What grows is the split noise, from 0.034 to 0.048, so at R8 the floor is ~0.05. What the seed does change at R8 is where the threshold falls. Seed 0's threshold over-covers seed 1 (0.867, area 0.833) and under-covers seed 2 (0.828, area 0.760): +0.031 and −0.010 in coverage against those seeds' own calibration, and +0.045 and −0.015 in flag area. Calibrating on each seed removes it. It sits inside every interval, so D-023 stands at R8 as well. The finding is that the mask seed perturbs the score distribution, not the coverage a calibrated threshold reaches.

A consistency check came free: seed 0 here reproduces §2's group-conditional cell at `random1d R8` (descending) exactly, 0.836 [0.767, 0.897]. That cell comes from the main bank alone, so the merge with the seed bank changed nothing it should not.

**How the runs got done.** At first the runner's memory preflight (D-043) refused both seed runs: it wants ~6.0 GB, and other applications left 4.2–4.5 GB. A queue retried them once the dispersion run had finished and memory had freed. R4 resumed from its 120-pair checkpoint (D-042) and finished the remaining 278 pairs in 59 min. R8 then passed the preflight unaided and ran all 398 pairs in 1 h 24 min. The fallback the queue carried (overriding the preflight, which would have cost R4 its checkpoint because the override changes the config hash) was never needed.

## 8. Smaller changes

- **One gate, one constant, every reporting script.** `CANDIDACY_QUANTILE = 0.33` and `candidacy_gate()` live in `evaluation/bank.py`. All eight scripts that report a label-dependent number take their default from there: `coverage_by_patient`, `component_auroc`, `split_noise_floor`, `seed_noise_floor`, `dispersion_baseline`, `sizing`, `analyse_bank` (with `--relative`) and Track 1's `select_weights` and `tool2_holdout`. Before this, Track 1's two scripts still computed the defective label, which is P-19's failure mode: each caller had to remember the rule. A bank without a design side is refused with a message naming `data.dataset.design_fraction` (D-048) and the opt-out (`--candidacy-quantile 0`). It is never quietly given the old label. Two of Track 1's tests ran scripts on the committed `w02_solver_smoke` bank, which has no design side. `test_weight_selection.py` passes unchanged because `select_weights.py` keeps its own refusal path. `test_paired_gap.py`'s sizing test now opts out explicitly, since what it checks (the crossed design's patient budget) does not depend on the label.
- **`tool2_patient_disjoint.py` is removed.** Track 1 fixed `tool2_holdout.py` upstream, and on the w05 bank it reproduces the patient-disjoint result exactly (matched 0.8592; nearest operator 0 of 3 valid; Tool 2 weighted 0.9074, 2 of 3 valid). Two copies of one experiment are one too many. Those numbers are under the old label.
- **`add_patient_ids.py`** now exposes `patient_map(data_root)`, which `seed_noise_floor.py` reuses rather than duplicating.
- **Analysis memory on this laptop is mostly BLAS buffers, not data.** The component analysis reached 1.83 GB of commit for a 71 MB table. numpy and scipy each load their own OpenBLAS, and each reserves ~650 MB of per-thread buffers at import. With `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` the same run peaks at **0.53 GB** with identical output. That is why the first ordering attempt ran out of memory beside other jobs. None of these scripts uses BLAS for their work.
- **T2.4 §8 carries a correction box** and **P-30** records what the label change did to it. T2.4 was merged in PR #33, so its numbers are kept as reported, not rewritten.

## 9. Honest limitations

- **Not reportable.** The prior is a face model on knee MRI (D-016, P-18). Every number here exercises the method on real anatomy and real operators; none may appear in the write-up as a result.
- **The ruled label is still denominator-led within operators** (§4, P-31). The coverage numbers are correct for the label as ruled, and the label is not yet a clean hallucination measure at high acceleration or at `variable_density2d R2`.
- **The headline cell rests on few positives.** At `variable_density2d R2` the positive rate is 2.8%, hence the interval [0.151, 0.492], the widest in the table.
- **One patient split for the intervals.** §2.1 shows the failures hold on all 13 splits and that the project split reads ~0.02 low for group-conditional calibration. The patient-bootstrap intervals in §2 are still conditional on the project split.
- **Posterior dispersion is not an independent baseline on DDNM** (§6). The comparison that could separate it from `s_disagree` needs a solver that does not enforce data consistency exactly.
- **The seed floor covers one family at two accelerations.** `random1d` is the only family whose lines are drawn at random; `equispaced1d` has no seed to vary, and `variable_density2d`'s seed was not run.
- **The ordering uses one calibration operator's scales** (`random1d R2`). The single components do not depend on that choice, but the combined score does.
