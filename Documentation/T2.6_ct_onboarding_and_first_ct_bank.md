## 1. The CT substrate: TotalSegmentator (D-013)

LoDoPaB has no region annotations, and TotalSegmentator's masks cannot be laid over LoDoPaB images: they are different cohorts (LIDC-IDRI against Basel), so no subject correspondence exists (P-03). D-013 resolved this in Week 1 as Option A, making TotalSegmentator the CT substrate. This week wires it. Like LoDoPaB, its images are ground truth that the project re-projects with its own operator (D-005), so no projection data is needed.

`TotalSegmentatorDataset` (`src/caliber/data/totalsegmentator.py`) mirrors the fastMRI loader:

| Choice | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | One subject (`s0000`) is one patient, and the split key | `meta.csv` has no patient id. The assumption is stated, not checkable |
| Split | `split: auto` through the same registry as fastMRI (D-022, D-051) | 8 design / 41 calib / 51 test subjects at calib 0.5, design 0.10 |
| Resolution | Native 1.5 mm, centre-cropped or zero-padded to 256 x 256 (384 mm) | A pixel is the same physical size in every item, as fastMRI's centre crop gives |
| Intensity | A fixed HU window, [-1000, 1000] to [0, 1] | Air is 0, padding is air, and a tissue has one value in every item |
| Annotation | The union of 24 organ masks: solid and hollow organs, lungs, brain, thyroid | Bones, muscles and vessels would cover so much of the body that coverage would be ~1 everywhere. An overlay, not the label (D20) |
| Slices | The middle half of the organ-bearing z-range, ranked among organ slices | Every chosen slice has an organ in it. Choosing by position in the range first-to-last landed in gaps between organs |

**Checked on the data, not assumed:**

- The 100 subjects on disk are all RAS, 1.5 mm isotropic, int16 HU.
- The masks share the CT grid to **2e-4 mm**, and the loader refuses any that do not (0.01 mm tolerance).
- A rendered check confirmed anterior-up orientation and the mask outlines sitting on the organs (bladder, rectum, bowel). A misplaced mask produces no error, so this had to be looked at.
- Over all 100 subjects, **every one yields an annotated slice**. Median organ coverage is **8% of the slice** (range 0.1–34%).
- Loading takes 3.8 s per subject, once per item, not per operator.

The subjects cover 21 study types: 32 abdomen-pelvis, ~23 more spanning the abdomen, and the rest neck, spine, thorax, heart and limbs. That spread is why the annotation is a broad organ set rather than an abdominal one.

`tests/test_totalsegmentator.py` (10 tests, on synthetic NIfTI files) pins:

- that no chosen slice falls in a gap;
- the display orientation;
- that a mask lands exactly on its organ;
- the fixed HU window;
- the subject-level split;
- the refusal of an off-grid mask;
- the body fallback for a subject without organs.

## 2. The CT solver path: three bugs, all now fixed

Every diffusion and flow solver declares CT support. None had ever run on a CT operator. Putting one on it found three defects.

**P-32 — no solver could run on CT at all.** The CT adjoint is the VJP of the forward map, computed with autograd. The samplers run under `torch.no_grad()`, so the first consistency step raised. The adjoint now enables autograd locally.

**P-33 — DDNM and FlowDPS used filtered backprojection for data consistency.** Both pin the range component with `x0 - A^+(A x0 - y)`, taking `A^+` from `operator.pinv`. For CT that is FBP, which D-012 had already removed from the decomposition because it is not a pseudo-inverse. One step on a real slice:

| views | residual before | after an FBP step | after a CG step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 180 | 0.063 | 0.046 | 0.006 |
| 60 | 0.064 | 0.086 | 0.003 |
| 30 | 0.064 | **0.207** | 0.001 |

The FBP step made sparse-view estimates *less* consistent with their data. That would have confounded the view-count axis with the solver failing to fit. The step now uses `decomposition_pinv` (`solvers.diffusion.range_replace`). **For MRI that is the same function, so MRI draws are bit-identical**, and a test asserts it.

**P-34 — the three limited-angle operators shared one calibration group.** The group name ignored the arc, which is limited-angle's severity. It is now `ct/{family}/v{n_views}/a{arc}`, so the 9-operator grid has 9 groups (it would have had 7).

**The CG inverse is the cost of CT, so it can run on the GPU.** Measured at 256²:

| views | CG solve on CPU | on GPU | CPU vs GPU difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 180 | 5.33 s | 0.85 s | 5e-5 |
| 60 | 1.80 s | 0.32 s | 5e-5 |
| 30 | 0.88 s | 0.20 s | 8e-5 |

Two opt-in settings move it: `solver.consistency_device` for DDNM's ~96 solves per pair, and `run.scoring_device` for scoring's n+4. Both default to off, which is what keeps every existing MRI bank reproducible. `tests/test_ct_solver.py` (11 tests) pins:

- MRI bit-identity;
- the CT step beating FBP at 8, 16 and 32 views;
- device agreement, including a GPU test;
- DDNM ending data-consistent on CT;
- that the scoring device changes no bank;
- the group names.

## 3. The first CT banks

**Smoke bank** (`w07_ct_totalseg_smoke_gpu`): 6 subjects × 4 operators (v180, v60, v30, limited-angle a120) = 24 pairs at **66 s per pair**, with no non-finite values. The whole-image `||P_N e|| / ||e||` came out at **0.984–1.000 on every pair**. So the CT projection is a contraction, and DDNM's error lies almost entirely in the null space, as a data-consistent solver's should. Some single regions exceed 1, because a projection spreads error across regions. That is why the check is made on the whole image.

**The full CT bank** (`w07_ct_onboarding`, rewritten this week because the old config could not run):

- Grid: TotalSegmentator, 100 subjects × 9 operators. Sparse-view runs 180/120/90/60/45/30 views at 180°; limited-angle runs 150/120/90° at 120 views.
- Solver: DDNM with 24 steps and 4 draws, the same as the w05 MRI bank (Sec. 13). The two modalities differ only in operator and data.

It ran in four sittings over ~16 h of GPU time, at **63.5 s per pair**:

| Sitting | Pairs | What ended it |
|---|---|---|
| 23:43–08:22 | 0 → 480 | an automatic stop just after a save, before college |
| 09:35–15:27 | 480 → 810 | the battery guard, at 11% while plugged in |
| 15:52–16:49 | 810 → 860 | an automatic stop just after a save, before going home |
| 18:17–19:04 | 860 → 900 | done |

Each resume recovered its pairs from the checkpoint (D-042), and none was recomputed.

| Integrity check | Result |
|---|---|
| Rows | 230,400 = 900 pairs × 256 regions, every pair complete |
| Non-finite values | 0 |
| Subjects by side | 8 design / 41 calib / 51 test |
| Calibration groups | 9, one per operator (P-34) |
| Whole-image ‖P_N e‖ / ‖e‖ | 0.978–1.000 over all 900 pairs; none above 1 |
| Regions with organ annotation | 19.7% |

**Why CT costs ~5x MRI per pair.** The network is the same ~12 s per pair. The rest is ~104 CG solves per pair (96 in DDNM's consistency step, 8 in scoring), each up to 180 projections and back-projections, 20 times over. MRI's equivalent is one FFT.

### 4.2 Across 13 patient splits

`split_noise_floor.py` re-draws the subject split 13 ways and re-runs everything, re-valuing the gate each time (0.032–0.044).

- **Group-conditional calibration averages 0.901** across splits (sd 0.036). Its per-cell means run 0.894–0.907, which is nominal, as the method promises. **The project split is the worst of the 13** (0.823), so §4.1's shortfall is that split. It is the same effect T2.5 §2.1 measured on MRI (0.875, second-lowest), larger here because CT has 51 test subjects against MRI's 44 patients and a wider spread (sd 0.036 against 0.026).
- **Naive transfer rarely loses coverage on CT; it over-flags.** Its per-cell mean coverage is at least 0.89 everywhere. No cell falls below nominal on more than half the splits; the worst are `v120` ascending and the 180-view calibration cell, 6 of 13 each. The cost is flag area: calibrated at 30 views, it flags **100% of regions** at six of eight deployment operators, and 89–98% at the other two. Group-conditional calibration reaches the same nominal coverage while flagging 54–85%.

So on CT the operator-shift failure is the T2.4 kind: valid and useless. It is not MRI's under-coverage (T2.5 §2). **Whether that is a property of CT or of the label is the open question (§4.3), and it decides how this can be written up.**

### 4.3 The label does not transfer from MRI

The relative label `err_free / free_content > 1.75` was ratified on MRI (D37), with D-060's gate set on MRI. D-020 warned that magnitudes do not transfer across substrates. On CT, among candidate regions on test subjects:

| | Positive rate | AUROC(`-free_content`) | AUROC(`err_free`) | ρ(ratio, `free_content`) | ρ(ratio, `err_free`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All candidates | **0.882** | **0.912** | 0.527 | −0.68 | +0.33 |
| `sparse v180` | 0.736 | 0.825 | 0.675 | −0.49 | +0.59 |
| `sparse v30` | 0.956 | 0.976 | **0.346** | −0.76 | +0.21 |
| `limited a90` | 0.778 | 0.929 | 0.529 | −0.85 | +0.12 |

(MRI, T2.5 §4: candidates 0.415 positive, `-free_content` 0.752 against `err_free` 0.797.)

**On CT the label is near-saturated and almost wholly denominator-led.** Nine candidate regions in ten are "hallucinated". Which ones are depends on how little each hides (0.912), not on how wrong the reconstruction is (0.527; at 30 views the error *anti*-predicts it). A coverage of 0.90 under this label mostly means flagging the regions with the least free content. That is why §4.1–4.2 are reported as the pipeline's output, not as CT's hallucination result. The fix is Track 1's: a CT threshold, or a criterion that does not divide by `free_content` (§7).

### 4.4 The score on CT

`component_auroc.py` (paired subject bootstrap, scales at `v180`):

- The components rank the (defective) label at AUROC 0.605–0.807 for `s_free`, and similarly for `s_disagree`. `s_residual` is weakest everywhere, at 0.54–0.65.
- `s_free` and `s_disagree` are tied to within 0.007. The equal-weight combination is within ±0.012 of the best single component, ahead in 6 of 9 operators by at most 0.003. That is the same story as MRI (T2.5 §3): combining neither helps nor costs much.

These rank a label §4.3 says is mostly `-free_content`, so they are recorded and not interpreted.

## 5. Smaller changes

- **The analysis scripts take `--cluster`** (`component_auroc.py`, `split_noise_floor.py`, as `coverage_by_patient.py` already did). On TotalSegmentator the patient column is `sample_id`.
- **P-06 is moot while LoDoPaB is unused.** If LoDoPaB is ever used again, it needs two fixes first. The patient lookup has to be per slice, because slices are shuffled within each split. And the last shard of each split is zero-padded (`validation_027` has 66 real slices of 128), which the stride sampler can pick as a blank image.
- **The laptop's charger cannot keep up with the GPU at full load,** so the battery drains slowly during a long run even when plugged in. The full bank ran in three sittings. A battery guard pauses the run after a save below 20% and resumes it at 60%; checkpoints every 10 pairs (D-042) make each sitting resume exactly where the last stopped.

## 6. Honest limitations

- **Not reportable.** The prior is a face model on CT (D-016, D-035). No CT prior exists; Song et al.'s CT score model (D-016) was never fetched.
- **The CT label is not a hallucination measure yet** (§4.3). Every coverage number in §4 is the pipeline working under the MRI label, not CT's result.
- **100 of 1,228 subjects.** Only part of TotalSegmentator is extracted (P-10), and the full set needs ~28 GB. The 51 test subjects make the split noise larger than MRI's.
- **One subject is assumed to be one patient** (§1). If TotalSegmentator repeats patients, subject-clustered intervals are slightly too narrow.
- **No dose axis.** `photon_count` names the operator but applies no noise. Week 8's "dose" axis is not built.
- **The cross-modality distance check is still open.** D-053's signed distance has not been computed for real CT pairs at 256²: Hutchinson estimation with CG solves takes minutes per pair. So the coverage gap has not been plotted against distance on CT (T1.3's open box).
- **Organ annotations mark anatomy, not pathology.** TotalSegmentator has no lesion masks, so CT's region-level FDR/power measures something different from fastMRI+'s.

## 7. For the other tracks

**Track 1.**

1. **The CT label needs a ruling before CT can be read** (§4.3). Under the MRI-ratified τ = 1.75 with D-060's q33 gate, 88% of CT candidate regions are positive. The label tracks `-free_content` at AUROC 0.912 and the error at 0.527 (0.346 at 30 views). D-020 anticipated this: magnitudes do not transfer across substrates. A CT-specific τ, set on CT's design split, is the minimal fix. A criterion that does not divide by `free_content` would also address P-31 on MRI.
2. **Group-conditional calibration holds nominal on CT on average** (0.901 over 13 splits). This split is the unluckiest of the 13, and naive transfer fails mainly by over-flagging, marking 100% of regions in most descending cells.
3. **P-34 was a bug against D-021/D-023's own rule,** not a new ruling: limited-angle's severity is its arc. CT groups are now `ct/{family}/v{views}/a{arc}`.
4. **Equal weights stay a neutral default on CT** (§4.4), as on MRI.

**Track 3.** The CT bank is the real-data substrate T3.5 lacked (it worked at 32×32). The contraction check holds on every pair (0.978–1.000), and DDNM's error lies almost entirely in the null space. D-053's distances for these nine operators are still uncomputed (§6).
