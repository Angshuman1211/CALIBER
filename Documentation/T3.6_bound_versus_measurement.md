## Objective

T3.3 derives `gap ≤ d_K⁺` (Step 1: Kolmogorov distance between the score laws given `label = 1`) and, under Lipschitz and margin assumptions, `d_K⁺ ≤ C √d_op / π` (Step 2, §3b). This study asks the question the blueprint set: **does the bound sit above the gap measured on a score bank, and by how much?** It answers per ordered operator pair, on the label the project reports (D-061), with patient-level intervals, and with every constant estimated from the bank rather than assumed. The Phase 4 plan fixes the table as six rows; §2 defines them and §7 runs them.

## 2. Method — the six rows

For an ordered pair `(src, dst)` of a crossed bank (every operator sees every item and region):

**Score, label, sides.** T1.1's standardised affine score; scales frozen on `src` per pair, or on one `--scale-operator` for the whole table (T2-A3's convention: the score is then one function at every operator, and `cov_dst` / `cov_matched` reproduce `pair_coverage_registry.csv` exactly, which `test_transfer_cells_reproduce_track_2s_pair_table…` pins). Label from `label_spec` (D-061's absolute label, `ε` the design-split q75 of `err_free`). Thresholds by `split_threshold` on `calib`, coverage on `test`, patient-disjoint and checked.

### 2.1 Row 1 — the measured gap

Both of Track 1's frozen definitions: `gap_matched = cov(dst→dst) − cov(src→dst)` (`prereg.GAP_DEFINITION`) and `gap = (1 − α) − cov(src→dst)` (its sensitivity arm). **The interval:** each replicate draws calibration patients and test patients separately, refits `τ_src` and `τ_dst` on the drawn calibration patients, and reads both coverages on the drawn test patients (`_bootstrap`). A replicate whose calibration draw cannot certify α is dropped, as `reliability.clustered_coverage` drops it; fewer than a tenth usable gives no interval. 2,000 replicates, 95%, which matches Track 2's cell intervals. The October interval (τ fixed, test side only) is still computed as `gap_fixed_lo/hi`, so its defect is on the record rather than asserted.

### 2.2 Row 2 — d_K and the bound check

`d_K⁺` over every non-design row of both operators, with an interval from the **same** patient resample as row 1 (the union of the drawn calibration and test patients), so the tightness ratio's numerator and denominator move together. The check is `gap_matched ≤ d_K⁺ + 1/(n+1)` with `n` the calibration positives at `dst`: a matched split-conformal cell sits between `1 − α` and `1 − α + 1/(n+1)` in expectation, so this is Theorem 1(b) of T3.7. A pair counts **against** it only when the whole gap interval lies above `d_K⁺_hi + 1/(n+1)` (`significant_violation_matched`). **Tightness** = `gap_matched / (d_K⁺ + 1/(n+1))`. **Location ratio** = `|F_dst⁺(τ_src) − F_src⁺(τ_src)| / d_K⁺`: by D89's identity the gap estimates the CDF difference *at the threshold*, so this says how much of the bound's slack comes from the laws separating most somewhere other than at τ.

### 2.3 Rows 3–4 — W₁ and the coupled bounds

Row 3: `W₁` between the label-conditional and the unconditional score laws, with intervals. Row 4 (a): `E|s_dst − s_src|` over the coupled regions, which bounds the unconditional `W₁` because the bank observes the identical-item coupling (T3.3 §3(i)). Row 4 (b): the label-conditional laws have no such coupling, because the label flips with the operator. Splitting the flips off gives an exact bound for them too (`coupled_w1_cond_bound`, T3.7 Proposition 5). Both are theorems; `holds_coupled*` False would be a bug.

### 2.4 Row 5 — the operator distance

`d_op` (D19 `null_space_projection`) and the signed `null_space_growth` (D47) per pair. They come from T1-0's CSV (`--distances`, symmetric and antisymmetric fill) or, for small banks, from the manifest. `distance_correlations` gives Spearman of each distance against `d_K⁺`, `d_K`, `W₁⁺`, `W₁`, `E|Δs|` and both gaps, **pooled and per family cell, never pooled alone**. The interval resamples operators (`prereg.GAP_CLUSTER_UNIT`), and `cells_agree_in_sign` applies D93's unanimity reading.

### 2.5 Row 6 — histogram TV

`0.5 Σ|p_src − p_dst|` of the label-conditional laws over 10, 20, 50 and 100 equal bins. By data processing it is at most the data-level TV in Barber et al.'s bound. **Bins are equal-mass (quantile edges) since 23 November.** The equal-width version put almost all LIDC positives in one or two bins and returned a "TV" below `d_K⁺` at 58–72 of 72 pairs (§3b).

Plus T3.3 §3b's decomposition and rate constants, unchanged from October (`p_flip`, `crossing`, `algebra_bound`, `L_S`, `L_ε`, `M_S`, `M_ε`, `rate_bound`).

**Evaluable.** A pair counts when both thresholds are certifiable and `dst` has held-out positives (`PairReport.evaluable`); otherwise the matched cell flags everything and the gap is 0 by construction. One global `ε` makes positive rates operator-dependent (§4.4), so on 60-item harness banks this leaves 2 to 12 of 12 pairs.

The cell signs disagree for every law distance. The last row reproduces Track 1's Simpson pattern (D89, D93) independently, from the bank rather than from the cell table. Signed growth against a *symmetric* law distance is near 0 inside a cell by construction, because both directions of every pair are in it; that block of `t36_corr.csv` carries no information.

*Why, within sparse view* (exploratory, read after the result, not pre-registered): the law distances follow the **view ratio**, and `d_op` does not. ρ(|log(v_a/v_b)|, ·) is +0.92 for `E|Δs|`, +0.71 for `d_K⁺` and +0.87 for `d_K`. ρ(`d_op`, ·) is +0.17, −0.01 and +0.18. The distance itself is larger for sv180↔sv120 (0.495) than for sv180↔sv90 (0.421), and smallest for sv60↔sv30 (0.295), where the score moves most (`E|Δs|` 0.414, against 0.137 for sv180↔sv120). The ambient-normalised projection distance counts how many measured directions change status. That count shrinks as both operators become sparse and depends on whether one angle grid nests in the other. The prior's response follows the relative change. Accordingly `L_S = E|Δs| / d_op` ranges from 0.27 to 4.53 over the 72 pairs: Step 2's Lipschitz "constant" varies 17-fold on one bank.

**What this means for the paper.** Step 2 is valid at every pair (the rate bound holds 72 of 72) and loose by two orders of magnitude, and on this bank the distance it is written in does not rank the shifts it bounds. This agrees with T1-B (the distance does not separate from the views/arc baseline for the gap), now at the level of the score law. The plan called row 5 the place where "the substantive claim must link operator quantities to the shift in the score law"; on the LIDC bank that link is absent within families.

**Row 6 was uninformative as first implemented.** Equal-width bins over the pooled range put almost every positive score in one or two bins, because a few large scores stretch the range. The resulting "TV" (0.15–0.26 for the median pair) fell below `d_K⁺` at 58–72 of 72 pairs, which no total variation can. The bins are now equal-mass (`histogram_tv`'s quantile edges, pinned by `test_quantile_bins_survive_an_outlier…`). The harness numbers in §3 used equal-width bins too and are not re-quoted. A LIDC rerun for row 6 is optional: nothing in Theorems 1–3 depends on it.

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 The interval was the defect, and the bound survives its repair

The October interval was the test-side-only, fixed-threshold one D73 found understating by about 5× on Track 1's banks. Repaired, it is a median 1.4–2.1 times wider on the harness, and 9 times wider at `v30 → v12`, where a source with π = 0.05 makes the threshold itself the dominant noise. **A correction made before this table was committed:** the first rerun counted a replicate whose calibration draw could not certify α as full coverage, which put the `v60 → v12` width at 87× and its lower end at −0.10. Such replicates are now dropped, as `reliability.clustered_coverage` drops them, and that cell's interval is [0.858, 0.973], no wider than the fixed one. **The bound's verdict does not change**: separated violations were 0 under the defective interval and are 0 under the right one, because the repair widens the gap interval and only a *separated* pair counts. What changes is that the October intervals could not have been quoted, and these can.

### 4.2 Where under-coverage is real, the bound is close to attained

Tightness among under-covering pairs is a median 0.84 on the CT structured-prior bank (0.73–0.94) and 0.71 on MRI white noise. The location ratio is 1.00 and 0.86: the two label-conditional laws separate most **at the calibration quantile**. That is why `gap ≤ d_K⁺` is close to a tautology for split conformal with continuous scores (the plan's warning). Its value is as an implementation check and as this number. The CT white-noise bank's one under-covering pair has tightness 0.40 and location 0.44, the case where the laws separate most away from the threshold.

### 4.3 Row 5 needed the two-family bank, and on it the answer is no

`d_op` orders `d_K⁺` at +0.60 to +0.63 and W₁ more weakly. On these banks every pair is within one family, so the D93 reading (within-cell, signs unanimous) has one cell and `cells_agree_in_sign` is `n/a`. The LIDC bank has four cells, and on it Track 1 found the *gap* reversing sign between pooled and within-cell (D93). **Row 5 is the test of whether the operator distance carries information about the score-law shift where T1-B found it carries none about the gap.** It cannot be read off the harness. **On the LIDC bank it does not** (§3b): within family cells ρ(`d_op`, `d_K⁺`) is −0.15 to −0.01 on 66 of 72 pairs, while the view ratio orders the same law distances at up to +0.92.

### 4.4 A single global ε makes the positive rate operator-dependent (unchanged)

D-061 sets `ε` once per substrate. On these banks `π` runs from 0.000 to 0.70 across operators, so a source with `π ≈ 0` cannot certify α and a destination with none cannot be scored. This is now enforced: such pairs are not evaluable (§2), and `n_pos_calib_*` and `pi_*` are in the CSV beside every gap.

### 4.5 The unconditional bound is still the wrong object

`gap ≤ d_K` on the unconditional laws fails at 6 of 12 pairs on the CT structured-prior bank and at 1 of 2 on MRI prior; the label-conditional bound never does. Part II adds a reason the October text did not have. The October explanation was that conditioning selects high-error regions, and high error is not high score once `cos θ ≠ 0`. The statement stands; that explanation of it does not generalise.

### 4.6 The over-coverage direction

Half of the pairs on the CT prior bank over-cover. Under `gap_matched` the bound is two-sided (T3.7 Theorem 1(b)), so it bounds how much coverage an inherited threshold can *gain* too; it says nothing about the flag area that gain costs, which is the real failure in that direction.

### 4.7 Step 2's rate bound: valid and vacuous (unchanged)

Never below 3.8 on any evaluable pair, a median 11 to 63 times `d_K⁺`; the densities `M_S`, `M_ε` dominate. State Corollary 4 as an order result (T3.7 §3).

# Part II — T3-C: the orthogonal-error approximation, re-checked

## 8. Why this needed doing

Every bank carries three null-space norms per region: `s = ‖P_N x̂‖`, `c = ‖P_N x‖` (the truth's hidden content) and `err = ‖P_N(x̂ − x)‖`. They satisfy `err² = s² + c² − 2sc cos θ` exactly. Under the face prior `cos θ ≈ 0`, so `err ≈ hypot(s, c)`: the orthogonal-error approximation. On the medical priors `cos θ` is +0.60 (MRI, BraTS) and +0.88 (CT, LIDC). T2.10 §11 asked Track 3 to re-check every step that leans on the approximation, because a moved `cos θ` invalidates an assumption, not just a number.

## 9. The exact split

Write `a = s cos θ` (the reconstruction's hidden content along the truth's) and `o = s |sin θ|` (orthogonal to it). Then, with no approximation:

```
s²   = a² + o²
err² = (a − c)² + o²
```

`o` is invention in the project's sense; `a − c` is recovery error. The orthogonal-error approximation is the statement `a ≈ 0`. `alignment.decompose` returns the split per region and the approximation's relative error `(hypot(s,c) − err)/err`; `test_the_split_is_exact…` checks it against vectors with a known angle. A region whose three columns contradict the identity is NaN and counted, not clipped (on CT, the solver's 2–12% non-additivity, T2-A5/A6).

## 11. Why the two CT banks order their thresholds oppositely: a mechanism

D89 proved `sign(gap_matched) = sign(τ_src − τ_dst)`. The direction of every transfer failure is therefore the direction in which `τ` moves with ill-posedness. On the face-prior CT bank `τ` falls as the operator hides more; on the LIDC bank it rises (T2.11 §1j, ρ −0.94 against +0.55 to +1.00 within cells). D89 predicted the face bank's direction from consistency with its +0.976, without a mechanism. The split gives one.

**Exact, under orthogonality.** If `cos θ ≥ 0`, a region can be positive only if `s` clears the **orthogonal edge** `√max(ε² − c², 0)`. At `cos θ = 0` clearing it is also sufficient. The positive set is then *exactly* the score law truncated at an edge set by the hidden truth alone, plus every region whose hidden truth alone exceeds `ε` (`c ≥ ε`), whatever its score. Either way, **which regions are positive is decided by `c`, and their score only has to clear a floor `c` sets.** As an operator hides more, `c` grows, the edge drops and more `c ≥ ε` regions appear, so the positive set admits lower-scoring regions. `τ` is the α-quantile of positive scores, so it can fall.

**Under recovery.** With `cos θ` near 1, hidden truth that the prior recovers is not error (`err² = (a − c)² + o²`), so `c` alone no longer makes a region positive; a positive is a recovery *failure*, and its score carries the recovered part `a = s cos θ`, which grows with `c`. Selection and scale then move together, and `τ` rises with ill-posedness.

**The hypothesis (H-T3C).** `τ` falls with ill-posedness when the positives it is fitted on (the marginal ones, at or below the 2α quantile of positive scores) are made positive by the hidden truth, and rises when they are recovery failures whose score is coupled to the recovered truth.

**Evidence, and what it is not.**

| bank | median `cos θ` | marginal positives: `cos θ` | on the edge / truth-driven | `τ` with ill-posedness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MRI harness, white noise | +0.00 | −0.03 to 0.00 | edge-bound (`(s − edge)/s` 0.06–0.09) | falls (1.16 → 1.03 → 0.94) |
| MRI harness, structured prior | −0.2 to +0.07 | −0.35 to −0.29 | truth-driven (`c ≥ ε` at 0.83–0.86) | falls (0.75 → 0.61) |
| CT harness, white noise | +0.01 to +0.14 (operators with positives) | −0.05 to −0.01 | edge-bound (0.07) | falls (1.08 → 0.85) |
| CT harness, structured prior | +0.26 to +0.65 | +0.22 to +0.32 | truth-driven (1.00) | falls (1.52 → 0.82 → 0.68) |
| face-prior CT bank (Track 2) | +0.07 | not yet read | not yet read | falls (T1-B-i, ρ −0.94) |
| LIDC CT bank (Track 2) | +0.88 | not yet read | not yet read | rises (ρ +0.55 to +1.00 in cells) |

The four harness banks are consistent, and Track 2's two are consistent as far as they have been read (direction of `τ` and median `cos θ`, not yet their marginal positives). That is weaker than it sounds: **every harness bank sits in the orthogonal regime at the margin**, so the harness tests only one side of the hypothesis. A one-line generative model (`threshold_direction_model`, `--model`), in which scores are widely spread and invention grows like `λ^β` against the truth's `λ`, reproduces the recovery side: `τ` rises above a `cos θ` crossover that sits between 0.39 and 0.82 at `β = 0.75` and between 0.90 and 0.98 at `β = 0`. It also shows that **orthogonality is not sufficient**: with wide score spreads and invention growing as fast as the hidden truth (`β ≥ 1`), `τ` rises even at `cos θ = 0`, because the edge no longer binds. So the claim is about the marginal positives, not about the median `cos θ`, and the model's map (`scripts/t3c_alignment_check.py --model`) is for reading a bank against, not a law.

**The decisive check is Track 2's, and it can fail.** §7's second command reads, on both CT banks, the marginal positives' `cos θ`, their position against the edge, the truth-driven share and `positive_score_truth_spearman`. H-T3C predicts the face bank's marginal positives are truth-driven or edge-bound with `cos θ ≈ 0`, and the LIDC bank's are not, with their score coupled to `c`. **If LIDC's marginal positives turn out orthogonal and truth-driven while its `τ` rises, H-T3C is wrong**, and the two banks' opposite orderings go back to being unexplained, as D89 required.

**What T3-C was asked, answered.** The face bank is in the orthogonal regime at the margin, and the LIDC bank is not, by a factor of about 100 in the approximation's error. Every statement §10 found to depend on orthogonality is therefore false at LIDC's margin. The bound itself never depended on it.

**H-T3C against its own falsifier.** §11 named one outcome that would refute it: LIDC's marginal positives orthogonal and truth-driven while `τ` rises. That did not happen. LIDC's marginal positives are aligned (+0.57 to +0.65), their score is coupled to the hidden truth, and `τ` rises. The face bank's are orthogonal, edge-bound and decoupled, and `τ` falls. Both banks match the regime §11 assigned them.

**One sub-prediction failed as worded, and it is recorded as a miss.** Track 2 caught it. §11 said LIDC's marginal positives would *not* be truth-driven; they are, at 0.88–0.99. The column counts `c ≥ ε`. That means "positive whatever the score" only at `cos θ = 0`, and I wrote the prediction with the column's name and the mechanism's meaning. The miss stands.

**What the column means, exactly** (derived after the result, so it supports nothing by itself). Holding a region's `c` and `θ` fixed, the identity makes it positive iff `s ∉ [s₋, s₊]`, where `s± = c cos θ ± √(ε² − c² sin²θ)`. Every positive falls in one of three classes:
- **score-free**: `c |sin θ| ≥ ε`, positive at every score;
- **lower branch**: `s ≤ s₋` (under-recovery), which exists exactly when `c > ε` and `cos θ > 0`, with a boundary that always rises with `c`;
- **upper branch**: `s ≥ s₊`, which at `cos θ = 0` is the orthogonal edge and falls with `c`.

So `c ≥ ε` on LIDC says its marginal positives *can* be under-recovery positives, not that their positivity ignores the score. `operator_alignment` now reports the three shares and the boundary's slope (`marginal_score_free`, `marginal_lower_branch`, `marginal_upper_branch`, `marginal_boundary_slope`), and `test_every_positive_is_in_exactly_one_branch` pins the split.

**The harness says the split is not a one-number predictor.** On the white-noise banks 90–100% of marginal positives sit on the upper branch, the boundary slope is negative, and `τ` falls, as the algebra says. On the CT structured prior, though, 78–100% are score-free and the few branch positives sit on a *rising* boundary, yet `τ` still falls. Admission of new score-free regions, not a boundary, moves the threshold there. **So H-T3C remains a description of two regimes, consistent with six banks, not a law.** A rerun of the T3-C command on both CT banks (seconds) reports which class carries each bank's threshold. That is descriptive and is not claimed as a further test.
